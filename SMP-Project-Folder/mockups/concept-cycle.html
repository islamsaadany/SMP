Strategy Management Platform · concept

The reporting cycle

The SMO opens a window, it becomes a request sitting with each unit's owner, they report in the platform, and closing it snapshots everything. Concept for review — not built.

Why this one first

Today the platform can render any moment beautifully and knows nothing about time. A CEO seeing 66% cannot tell whether that is up from 58 or down from 74 — which is the only thing a CEO actually asks. Closing a cycle is what writes history, and history is what lets every other screen carry a delta.

1 · Open

The SMO sets the window

A span and a due date. Every unit's owner receives a request naming exactly what is expected of them.

2 · Report

Owners enter their numbers

One screen, only their own items, only what is due. Save as you go; submit when done.

3 · Chase

The SMO watches completeness

Who has reported, who is partial, who has not started. The chasing is the SMO's real job between reviews, and today the platform cannot even say who is late.

4 · Close

Everything is snapshotted

The figures freeze against this cycle. From the second cycle onward, every score can show what it was last time.

1 · Opening a cycle

Setup, SMO only. A cycle covers a span — a quarter, a half, a month — and the span is what decides which tactics are in scope. Opening it is what turns a plan into a request.

Opening locks the plan. Targets, tactics and quarter flags stop being editable for the span of the cycle. A number reported against a target that can still move is not a report — it is a negotiation. Focus marks lock at the same moment, for the same reason.

What the span decides

The cycle's end sets as-of, which is what should be delivered by now is measured from. Today that is a constant of 2 sitting in the data.

A tactic marked Q3–Q4 is not yet due in an H1 cycle and simply is not asked for. It appears in the H2 request instead.

Saving keeps a draft. Submit tells the SMO you are done — after that the SMO can reopen it for you, but you cannot change it silently.
Only what is due is asked for. A tactic outside the span shows as not asked rather than sitting there as an empty box that looks like a chore somebody forgot.
Blank is not zero. An unfilled row stays unreported at close — it does not become a nil return. The platform's oldest rule, and the one most likely to be broken by a form.

The SMO can close with gaps. Waiting for every last number means never closing, and a cycle that never closes writes no history. Unreported items close as unreported — visible as such forever, which is a stronger prompt than a reminder email.

What a snapshot holds

  1. Every actual and every derived score, as they stood at close.
  2. The target each was judged against — already the platform's rule, now with somewhere to live.
  3. Which items were unreported, kept distinct from those reported as zero.
A closed cycle is read-only. Reopening is possible and deliberate, and it says so on the screen. A snapshot that can be quietly rewritten is not a record.

Decisions I need from you

On the shape

  1. Does opening a cycle lock the plan? Shown as yes — targets and quarter flags freeze for the span. The alternative lets a unit adjust a target while reporting against it.
  2. Submit, or continuous saving? Shown as both: save a draft freely, submit once. Submission is what lets the SMO tell still working from finished and short.
  3. Can the SMO close with gaps? Shown as yes, with unreported items closing as unreported.

On the reach

  1. Who reports? Shown as the unit's owner, with the head able to see it. Should the head also be able to enter numbers?
  2. Does the group unit report too — its own Key Objectives and its eight capabilities — or does the SMO enter those directly?
  3. How many cycles a year? The span is free, but the deltas read best against a steady rhythm. Quarterly, or half-yearly as today?
