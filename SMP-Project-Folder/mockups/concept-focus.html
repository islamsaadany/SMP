Focus measures

The CEO marks a few measures per unit as the ones that carry reward. This is a concept for review — not yet built into the platform.

1 · What a focus measure looks like where it already lives

A mark, not a column. The row keeps its shape and its width — the measure is already carrying six numbers, and a word beside every marked one turns a quiet signal into a column of shouting. Hovering says what it means; the page legend says it once.

2 · One rule, not a target per measure

Where reward begins is a property of the scheme, not of each measure. It is set once for the cycle and every focus measure is read against it — so no measure grows a second target, and nobody negotiates a stretch line one row at a time.

Set it at 100% and delivering the commitment earns reward. Set it above — 110%, say — and reward starts only past it, while the measure is still scored against its own target as it always was.

At a rule of 110% a third standing appears between them — Met, not earning — for a measure that delivered its commitment without clearing the reward line.

Focus changes no score. The unit headline, the pillar figures and the group compile are computed exactly as they are today. Unit weights already decide how much a unit counts; letting focus alter scoring would count the same emphasis twice and make the group number impossible to explain in a room. And no compensation arithmetic lives here — the platform reports standing; the scheme pays.

3 · Where a measure gets marked

Inline, where the measure already is. Marking is a judgement about that measure in its context — you are looking at its target, its history and its owner when you make it. A separate list of names strips away everything that informs the choice.

The one trade-off

Marking inline means walking ten units to set a cycle. The board in the next section is therefore also where they are reviewed and unmarked — all ten at once, without leaving the choice divorced from the evidence when it is first made.

4 · Two views of the same thing

The CEO sees every unit. A unit sees its own and no one else's — what another business unit is rewarded on is not its business, and a league table nobody asked for is the fastest way to make this feature political.

5 · The unit's own focus reading

A strip at the top of the unit's Performance page, above the pillars. It is the first thing an owner sees on their own page, and it appears nowhere on the group cards — the card is the group's reading of a unit; this is the unit's reading of itself.

The count leads, not the average. Mobile's three focus measures average to 82%, which reads as nearly there. It is not: one is earning and two are short, and reward is won per measure, not on a mean. An average of 163% on one measure and 44% on another describes no situation anyone is actually in. The mean stays on the strip because the rest of the platform reads in percentages — but it sits second and quiet.

Where it does and does not appear

  1. On the unit's Performance page, above the pillars — for that unit's head and owner, and for the CEO and SMO.
  2. Not on the group's business-unit cards. The card is a two-box composition settled over six iterations, and it is the group's reading of a unit. A third box would break both the layout and the distinction.
  3. Not visible to other units, in keeping with section 4.

Settled

Settled

  1. A mark, no word, and no extra column. The row keeps its width.
  2. One reward rule per cycle — reward begins at 100%, or above it — instead of a target per measure.
  3. No cap. Three is the usual choice and stays a choice.
  4. No compensation arithmetic. The platform reports standing; the scheme pays.
  5. Locked once the cycle opens.
  6. A unit sees only its own. The CEO sees all.
  7. The CEO marks; the SMO may mark on their behalf.
  8. Each unit gets a focus strip on its own Performance page — count first, mean second, and never on the group cards.

Also settled

  1. Marking lives inline, where the measure already sits. The board reviews and unmarks across all units, but the choice is made beside the evidence.
  2. Focus is a business-unit idea only. The group unit carries none, even though it has Key Objectives of its own.
  3. Every cycle starts unmarked. Nothing carries forward — which means the marks are stored against the cycle, not against the measure, and last year's emphasis cannot quietly become this year's by default.

Nothing outstanding. This is ready to build into the product.
