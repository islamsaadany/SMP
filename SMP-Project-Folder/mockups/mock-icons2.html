Icons — shown at the size they will actually be used. Nothing is built.

Each drawn at 17px inside a 34px control, and again at 13px for the per-row mark, since an icon that works large and fails small is no use here.

For Edit

RestingActiveIn a row NameWhat it says
PencilA plain pencil. The most used mark for edit anywhere, and the least likely to be misread.
Pencil on a squareSays edit THIS PAGE rather than edit a thing. Slightly busier at 17px.
SlidersReads as settings or filtering, not editing rows. Risks colliding with the Setup gear.
UnlockSays the page is open for change, which is what edit mode means. Uncommon for this job.

For Clear

RestingActiveIn a row NameWhat it says
Broom (current)What is drawn today. You had to ask what it was, which is the answer.
EraserRubs out content and leaves the thing. That is exactly what clearing does.
BackspaceThe erase key. Instantly legible to anyone who types, but reads as undo not empty.
BinUnderstood by everyone — and wrong. A bin removes the row; clearing empties it and the row stays.
Circle with a slashSays forbidden, not empty. Reads as a blocked action.
My reading: pencil for edit, eraser for clear. The pencil is the most used mark for editing anywhere and needs no thought. The eraser is the only one of the five that means rub out the contents and leave the thing — which is precisely what clearing does, and precisely what a bin does not. The bin is the most recognisable and the most wrong: it says the row is going away.
If you would rather avoid icons for the destructive one, the clear control can stay a word. A rare, irreversible action is the one place where a label costs little and removes all doubt.
