Navigation options — same platform, four ways in. Nothing is built.
Option AOne row, a divider before the capabilities

No new depth and no extra click, at the cost of a very long row for the two roles who reach everything.

SMO — reaches everything

Eighteen entries in one row. It wraps to two lines at this width, three on a laptop.

Finance — owns one capability

Direct, one click. The divider does nothing when there is one of each.

Mobile's owner — owns one unit
Option BA category row: Business units, Supporting functions

Cleanest conceptually and it scales, but it charges every person a row of chrome and a click — including the ones who own exactly one thing.

SMO — reaches everything

Three rows of chrome on every page, and Mobile's SWOT is three clicks deep.

Finance — owns one capability

A category row and a list, both holding exactly one thing.

Mobile's owner — owns one unit

Two extra clicks to reach the only thing this person can see.

Option CCapabilities under Group, direct entry for the owner

A business unit is a place you go; a capability is a thing you open. The SMO reads eight rows on one page instead of eight tabs, and the owner still gets a direct entry.

SMO — reaches everything

Nav unchanged. All eight capabilities are rows on one page, not eight tabs.

Finance — owns one capability

The owner gets a direct entry; only the SMO reads them from the Group page.

Mobile's owner — owns one unit

Untouched.

Option DC, plus a Reporting button in the chrome

Not an alternative — it sits on top of any of the three. The button means one thing for everyone: what I owe this cycle. It goes to a unit's reporting, a capability's reporting, or the SMO's cycle board, depending on who is looking.

SMO — reaches everything

The button shows what the SMO is waiting on, from any page.

Finance — owns one capability

One click to what they owe, from wherever they are.

Mobile's owner — owns one unit

Same button, same meaning: everything you owe this cycle.

The button is a shortcut, not a place. It takes you to the page that already exists rather than assembling a new one — which is where the last attempt went wrong. Reporting still lives with the thing being reported.
