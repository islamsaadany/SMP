My recommendation — A

A shows the picture, names it, and never moves the box you are typing in. The picture is the part a sentence cannot replace: you see which screenshot you are about to send, which matters when you have taken three. And it sits above the composer, so the thing under your cursor stays where it was.

B is the boldest and is a real option if you want it unmissable — the cost is about 100px of panel, which on a short laptop is a row or two of the conversation. C is elegant and I think too quiet, which is the fault we are fixing. D reads best conceptually — the picture becomes part of the message — but it grows the box as you type, which is the one thing that has always been steady.

The panel is anchored at the bottom, so what you saw moving was the top — a 33px jump every time you switched. Two causes: the search box appears on one half and not the other, and I had made the line under the title say nothing on the queue side, which made that header shorter.

Both are fixed the same way: the panel now holds the height and the list absorbs the difference, so anything added to the chrome costs a row rather than moving the whole panel. And the line under the title always says something — whose conversation you are in, or the office's promise — rather than being padded out with a guessed number that would go stale the next time the type changes.

Checked at 900, 760 and 640px tall: one size on both halves, and the box you type in stays on screen at every height.
