Case studies, as a library

A different way in. Finished case studies stand as covers on shelves, one shelf per industry, with a search at the front. Opening one reads like a published report: the story down the page, and the facts, the contents and the people beside it. Work still running or being written sits on the desk below the shelves until it is done. Insights become index cards.

Strategy build 2025

Wander · written by Islam Saadany

Done

Wander asked us for a five-year strategy after two years of fast growth had left each business unit planning on its own. Over six months we built one group strategy with three business-unit plans under it, a scorecard of 24 measures, and the reporting routine. The board approved it in June 2025, and Wander ran its first quarterly review in September without us.

01The client and why they came to us

Wander develops and manages residential and holiday property. Growth had come project by project, and by 2024 each business unit set its own targets. The new CEO wanted one direction the board could hold the units to.

02What we were asked to do

Build the group strategy and the three business-unit plans under it, agree the measures the board would track, and set up quarterly reporting so the team could run it alone.

06What came of it

The other sections, the team and the three insights from the interview follow the same way, each open to change.

What is new here

  • Covers on shelves. One shelf per industry, plus Newest. A cover shows the client's mark, the industry, the name of the work and its months.
  • The report page. The story runs down the left. The facts, the contents and the buttons stay on the right as you read.
  • The desk. Running and being-written work stays off the shelves until it is done, so the shelves hold only finished case studies.

What it costs

  • It is a new look on the console. No other page has covers or shelves. The colours, buttons and type are still the console's own.
  • A cover needs the client's mark. A past client added in three fields has none, so its cover shows its initials until a logo is added.
  • Covers take more room than rows. About four to a shelf on a laptop and two on a phone.

Still to draw, once this direction is right
