Strategy build 2025

Jan 2025 – Jun 2025 · 6 months · written up 14 Jul 2025

In short

Wander asked us for a five-year strategy after two years of fast growth had left each business unit planning on its own. Over six months we built one group strategy with three business-unit plans under it, a scorecard of 24 measures, and the reporting routine. The board approved it in June 2025, and Wander ran its first quarterly review in September without us.

The client and why they came to us

Wander develops and manages residential and holiday property. Growth had come project by project, and by 2024 each business unit set its own targets. The new CEO wanted one direction the board could hold the units to.

What we were asked to do

Build the group strategy and the three business-unit plans under it, agree the measures the board would track, and set up quarterly reporting so the team could run it alone.

Operating model 2023

Mar 2023 – Nov 2023 · asked by Mohamed Essam · wanted by Fri 13 Nov

1 · Copy the prompt, and talk it through with your assistant 
I am writing up a case study for my consulting firm about a piece of
work we finished:

  Client:    Nile Logistics (Transportation)
  The work:  Operating model 2023
  When:      March 2023 to November 2023
  Our team:  Islam Saadany, Mohamed Essam

It will be read by consultants who have just joined and were not there.

Interview me. Ask ONE question at a time and wait for my answer before
asking the next one. Push back when my answer is vague, and do not move
on until it would make sense to somebody who was not there.

Ask me about, in this order:
  · the client, and why they came to us
  · what we were asked to do
  · the client's people we worked with, and the role of each
  · the main stages of the work, with the month of each
  · every deliverable: its name, what it was, the month we handed it over
  · what came of it
  · for each of us on the team, what a new joiner should ask them about
  · and last, one paragraph a new joiner should read first

Then ask what went better than expected, what cost us time, what I know
now that I did not at the start, and what I would warn the next
consultant about. For each, ask what happened, what we did, what came of
it, and what the next person should know.

When I say I am done, write it back in exactly this shape and nothing else:

=== IN SHORT
=== THE CLIENT AND WHY THEY CAME TO US
=== WHAT WE WERE ASKED TO DO
=== THEIR TEAM            (one line each: name · role)
=== TIMELINE              (one line each: month · stage)
=== DELIVERABLES          (one line each: name · what it was · month)
=== WHAT CAME OF IT
=== WHO TO ASK            (one line each: name · what to ask them about)

and then each insight as a block:

===
KIND: hiccup | practice | lesson
TITLE: one line, the thing itself
WHEN: the month
WHAT HAPPENED: ...
WHAT WE DID: ...
WHAT CAME OF IT: ...
WHAT THE NEXT PERSON SHOULD KNOW: ...
===

Use my words, not yours. If I only half answered something, leave that
line out rather than filling it in.

Adding an insight

Add an insight

What changes

  • Memory becomes Case studies, in the same place in the column. It opens on case studies. Insights is the same list as today, one press away.
  • A case study is one piece of work: eight sections, then the insights from that work.
  • Past work starts with adding the past client in three fields. An admin then asks someone to write it up, and the request lands on their My work.

What it costs

  • My work is no longer only Tracker actions. A write-up has no tick: it is done when marked done on the case study.
  • Adding an insight asks one more thing, which piece of work. It can stay empty.
  • An admin opens every piece of work when it starts, and asks for every write-up when it ends.
  • The 12 insights written so far wait under their client until someone places each one. Nothing places them by guessing.

For you to decide

  1. Opening a piece of work when it starts (drawings 4 and 5), so insights land in it along the way. Without it, insights are only placed at the end.
  2. The small entries keep their name, insight, as today. The word you used was lessons, which is also one of the three kinds.
  3. A past client's card shows its case studies (drawing 9), the only thing on the card that opens anything.
