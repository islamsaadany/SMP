Chat settings, in the order you'd decide them

Seven controls, re-sequenced and re-titled, with every explanation moved to a tooltip. Same controls, same keys, nothing removed.

Now, and proposed

Both are the real panel in the real build — the proposal is that same panel reordered in the browser, not a drawing of one.

The sequence

The order today isn't one. The master switch — the one that decides whether any of this exists — sits third, under the assistant, which is a decision about the chat rather than a decision above it. And the two email settings sit five rows apart with three unrelated rows between them.

The proposed order runs one way, from does this exist down to a tuning knob, so a reader never has to go back up.

  1. Does it exist, and what do people get
  2. ChatThe master switch. Off, nothing below it means anything — so it goes first.
  3. PromiseThe sentence every person reads at the top of the panel. What they see before they type.
  4. ScreenshotsWhat they're allowed to send. Still the only setting with real storage cost.
  5. Who answers
  6. AssistantKeeps Test the assistant directly beneath it — this is where you stand after flipping it.
  7. How people are reached
  8. Handover emailTold when the assistant gives up. Only shown when the assistant is on, as today.
  9. Away emailTold when your reply lands and they aren't looking. Beside its sibling at last.
  10. How it runs
  11. Reply checksLive or Relaxed. The only row that changes nothing anybody sees — so it goes last.

Two things I'd hold back

A status is not an explanation, and it stays on the page

“No one is set — handoffs wait in this inbox until somebody is chosen” is a fact about right now, not prose about how a setting works. Behind a tooltip, somebody switches Handover email on, nobody is chosen, and nothing ever says so. Same for “Emailed to Islam Saadany”: it names who, and who can change.

So: explanations become tooltips, live statuses stay visible. That's the only line I'd draw inside what you asked for.

A tooltip is unreachable on a touch screen

Hover doesn't exist on a tablet or a phone, and the Relaxed / Live cost is the one piece of prose that exists to make a decision rather than to describe one — it's the whole reason that control is a setting instead of a number in the source.

The i is focusable and carries an aria-label, so a keyboard and a screen reader both reach it. A finger doesn't. Say the word and it opens on tap as well as hover — a few lines, and it removes the objection entirely.
