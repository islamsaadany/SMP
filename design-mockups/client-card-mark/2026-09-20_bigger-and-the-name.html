A bigger client mark

Two decisions, and they are separate: how big the logo is and where it sits, and whether the client's name goes when the logo already says it. Every picture here is the running console photographed with each treatment applied — the Raya mark is the real artwork.

FirstWhat the last round changed — and what it did not

Worth saying plainly, because you are right to ask. The roundel used to be drawn about 30px wide, floating roughly 52px in from the card's left edge, on a line where everything else starts at the left. What the fix moved is where it sits: it now starts at the same edge as every other line, in a square slot, with the name beside it.

The picture itself went from 30px to 34px. That is not bigger in any way an eye would notice, and the wordmark got no help at all — it is squeezed into the same square as a round logo, which is the cost that round recorded and the thing you are now objecting to. So: the placement was fixed, the size was not. This page is the size.

Decision oneHow big, and where?

“Covering even the line of the settings button” means the mark has to start at y=12 rather than y=37 — level with the chip rather than under it. Both options below do that. What separates them is whether the mark keeps sharing its line with the name.

A · The same square slot, just bigger

Killed by drawing it

The smallest change: leave the mark beside the name and grow the square from 40 to 56. It reaches the Settings line and it costs no height at all. It also does three things the drawing makes obvious and a description would not have.

  • mark 56×56 at y=12
  • card 191px — unchanged
  • name width 111 → 53px
  • every name now two lines

Every name wraps (“ElAbd / Foods”), one is cut (“Nile Manufactur…”), and the Raya wordmark is smaller than it is today — because the slot is still a square, and a wide mark contained into a square is tiny whatever the square's size. It answers “bigger” for a round logo and makes your second complaint worse.

B · The mark takes its own line, at its own shape

Recommended

The mark stops sharing a line with the name. A round logo is a square; a wordmark is as wide as it is. The name and the industry sit under it, each on one line at the card's full width.

  • round mark 56×56, Raya 179×56
  • both at y=12 — level with the chip
  • every name starts at 15px
  • card 191 → 226px (+35)

This is a better answer to the problem the square was solving, not a reversal of it. The square existed so the name would start in the same place on every card when it sat after the mark — 65px in, whatever the mark was. With the mark on its own line every name starts at 15px, the same edge as the industry, the chips and the module rows beneath. The wordmark is then free to be a wordmark.

The cost is 35px per card. Nothing is squeezed and nothing is cut, but every card in the grid gets taller — a grid levels a row against its tallest card, so this is charged to all of them.

And how tall?

Each step of the mark is a step of the card, one for one. 56 is the recommendation — it clears the Settings line with room to spare and keeps the card under a screen-and-a-bit on a laptop.

Decision twoDoes the name go when there is a logo?

You are right about Raya: the wordmark says “RAYA TRADE”, so printing it again beside it says the same thing twice. The question is whether that is true of every logo — and it is not, which the drawing shows better than an argument does.

C · The name and the industry go wherever there is a logo

Your proposal, drawn

Exactly as asked: if the card has a logo, no name and no industry.

  • Raya — reads perfectly
  • third card — no name anywhere
  • card 226px — no height saved

Three things the picture says. Raya is right — nothing is lost. The third card is now anonymous: a green circle and no way to tell which client it is, because a symbol logo carries no name. ElAbd's own name is inside its roundel and is about 8px tall there — present, and not readable at a glance. And it saves no height at all: the card with no logo keeps its name, and the grid levels the row against it, so the space where the names were is simply empty.

The industry is worth separating from the name. “F&B” is written nowhere else on the card and no logo carries it — the name is the only thing a logo can say twice.

D · The client says whether its logo carries the name

Recommended

One tick beside the logo upload on that client's settings — this logo already says the name. Ticked, the card drops the name; unticked, it keeps it. The industry always stays.

  • Raya — mark only
  • everyone else — name kept
  • card 226px, same as B

It is the only version that is right for both kinds of logo, and whoever uploads the logo is the one person who can see whether it says the name. The cost is one stored setting and one control beside the upload you already use — small, but it is a build rather than a style change.

A cheaper version was considered and is not recommended: let the platform guess from the shape — a logo much wider than it is tall is probably a wordmark. It is free and it is a guess, and the day it is wrong a card loses its name with nobody told. The platform has a standing rule against filling in what it was not told.
