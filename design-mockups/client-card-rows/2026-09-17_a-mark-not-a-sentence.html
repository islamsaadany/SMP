What is on those rows

Nine pieces of text, and only one row is about something in flight

Counted off your screenshot. This is the argument for taking the prose off: the platform spends a line per row, and almost always to say that nothing is happening.

  • 5say nothing is happening — “No cycle open” ×3, “No new reports this month” ×2
  • 2are a real alarm — “no plan yet” on El Abd and on Test Client
  • 2are the same fact twice — Meridian's row says “Cycle open” in the line and “cycle open” again on the right

And that duplicate pair is the row that wrapped. .mrow i has no flex-shrink:0, so a long line beside it squeezes the two-word state until it breaks onto a second line — which is the two-line row you are looking at.

Option A

A mark, not a sentence

The row keeps its name and gains a short mark on the right — and draws nothing at all when there is nothing. Silence is the resting state, so the compactness comes free rather than from squeezing anything. The tail keeps the size, ink and position it has today; what changes is that the words get short and the sentence beside them goes.

What it says
3 of 10 when units still owe a report · 2 new when reports landed · No plan and Not answering in the warning ink, because those two are the card's only alarm · nothing otherwise. Internal Tracker and Meeting Notes say nothing at all today and go on saying nothing — they declare no line (lib/modules.ts).
What it costs
A short mark needs a moment's learning where a sentence did not, so the full wording sits on the hover — and a hover is not reachable on a tablet, where tapping the row opens the module. Stated rather than solved: the mark is the fact in the fewest words, so the hover is a nicety and not the only copy.
What it does not change
No row moves, no door moves, nothing is stored, and the mark is made from the same facts the client's own landing reads — so the console cannot start saying one thing while the landing says another.

Option B

One notification on the card — your ask, read literally

Rows carry their name and nothing else; one count sits on the card. The most compact of the three, and the one I would argue against — for a reason that is arithmetic rather than taste.

Why I would not ship it
A card-level number has to add things that cannot be added. Meridian's 3 above is three units still to submit — but if two reports had also landed, is the card a 3, a 2, or a 5? Any answer is a number nobody can read back. And it cannot tell three units are late from this client is not answering, which is the one thing on that grid that means something is broken.
What survives from it
Its instinct is right and it is what A keeps: nothing drawn when there is nothing.

Option C

Names only, and the alarm moves up to the tags

Rows are doors and say nothing at all; the client's health joins Super user and 10 units, where the card's other facts already live. Quietest, and shortest by a row's worth of nothing.

What it costs
Two things. Nothing on the grid then says a cycle is running or that work is owed — you would open a client to find out, which is what the card exists to save. And it reverses a decision already taken: the state was deliberately moved out of the tags and into the row, because cycle open and no plan yet are Strategy's answers about this client, and with two modules able to have a state the tags cannot say which one they are about.

My recommendation: A, and the two-line row is a defect either way

You are right that the notes are the cost, and right that the answer is a mark. Where I would push back is on “if something is new” — and it is worth settling before anything is built, because it decides what the mark can honestly say.

  1. Nothing stores when you last looked at a client. The facts answer state, not new: “new reports this month” is a calendar window, and a cycle being open is true for eight weeks. A dot meaning “new” would light on Meridian for two months and stop meaning anything.
  2. So A's mark says what is outstanding, not what is new — a figure you can act on, which is the thing that decides which client you open next.
  3. Truly “new since I last looked” is a separate, bigger thing: it needs a last-seen mark per consultant per client, which the platform stores nowhere today. Worth doing if you want it; worth naming rather than quietly not doing.

And the wrap is a one-line fix I would make whichever you pick: the row's tail can never be allowed to break, whatever is beside it.

One decision inside A, and it is yours

Whose answer is the mark? The client picks the wording of their own landing line — the cycle's state or what is waiting. If the console's mark follows that pick, then a client who chose “the cycle's state” shows no count even when three units are late. If the mark is Forefront's own and always says what is waiting, it is more useful for scanning, and it is a second answer to keep in step with the first — though the card already has one of those in the health word, so there is precedent.

My recommendation is Forefront's own: this card exists so you can pick which client to open, and “3 of 10 still to submit” is the fact that decides it.
