Your first note

The boxes fit. That was my drawing, not the product.

You were looking at a real fault and it was mine. The register turns a long list into a searchable button — the same one the roles half of this page uses — and that happens as the very last step of drawing the page. My transform stopped one step short, so it drew a plain dropdown where the product draws a button, and a plain dropdown sizes itself to its longest entry rather than to its cell.

Measured on the shot you sent: Official BU ran 17px past its cell and Unit 23px. With the missing step put back, every field sits inside its own cell — over 0, all six of them — and the open row goes from 1498px wide to 1449. Nothing in the product changes; one line in the drawing does.

And it is worth saying what I nearly shipped instead

I found the plain dropdown carrying a rule that lets it size itself past its cell, measured four ways of overriding it, and had the best one ready. None of them was needed. Asking what the page actually draws, rather than reading the stylesheet, is what found the real cause — and a rule that changes nothing is worse than no rule, because the next person reads it as load-bearing.

Your second note

The double-click: yes, and here is exactly what it costs

It is not new vocabulary either — the Internal Tracker already renames an action by double-clicking it. Enter or leaving the box saves, Escape puts it back, and a single click does nothing. Same rule here, one module over.

I drove it on the real page rather than reasoning about it, because a double-click is two real clicks first, and the email is a button.

Double-clicking…What the two clicks doWhat is left behindThe answer
the email or the mobilethe copy runs twice — same value both timesthe address is on your clipboard, and the word Copied flashesthe box replaces that word as it opens, so the flash is not seen; the clipboard holds the value you are about to edit
any other valuenothing — there is no button therethe browser highlights the word under the pointerone line clears it as the box opens

Driven in the real register: two clicks reached the copy button, it read Copied, and nothing was left selected. On the Job title the word Care was left highlighted.

So nothing is lost either way

A single press still copies, exactly as it does today — you asked for that and it stays. The double-click is a second thing the same value can do, and the two do not fight.

The one real cost is the tablet. A double-tap there is the browser’s own zoom gesture — the platform allows pinch-zoom, so it is live — and it can be handed to the table with one line scoped to those cells, which I have not proved here. Either way the three-dots menu keeps Edit details as the way in, and that is also how anybody discovers the row can be edited at all: a double-click is invisible until somebody tries it.

Why not a single press, which is one press cheaper

Because a press on the email already means copy. Making it also mean edit takes that away on the two columns you most want to edit — and there is nowhere to put the copy back.

The one thing left to pick

One box, or the whole row?

With the double-click as the way in, the pen is not needed — so at rest the table does not change at all, on either of these. What differs is what opens when you double-click.

C — the one box you pressed

recommended

The Tracker’s shape exactly. One cell becomes a box, leaving it saves, Escape puts it back. No Save, no Cancel — because every other field in the platform already saves when you leave it, and a pair of buttons here would be a second answer to what saving means.

The one thing A has that C does not

Cancel. With C, leaving a box saves it — so putting a wrong value back means typing the old one in again. That is fine on a job title and it is worth a thought on Unit, which is where somebody sits and what decides some of what they can open. Escape still puts a box back while you are in it; what C has no answer to is changing your mind after leaving.

A — the whole row

Every field on the row becomes a box at once, with Save and Cancel at the end. Better when you are correcting a whole person — a new joiner’s title, unit and phone together — and it is the shape the other six Setup tables already use.

One cost both of them have, measured

A box has a border and some padding, so it holds about 17px less text than the plain value did. On the two narrowest columns that shows: with the row open, Emp. ID and Mobile lose their last few characters — RT‑11010 reads as RT‑1101. The value is whole, the box scrolls, and it reads in full again the moment the box closes. I tried letting the open cell grow and it changes nothing: a column is one width for every row.

C is the recommendation, because it is what you described and it moves nothing. But it is genuinely close: if most of what you do on this page is fix a whole person at once rather than one field, A is the better one and the cost is 94px on a table that already scrolls.

The other half, unchanged

Roles as a ticking list

Yes — and the platform already has the control. It is the searchable ticking list a tactic’s collaborators use, which is the same control the two dropdowns above turn out to be.

Drawn on Mostafa Deif, who on your register holds all three kinds of role at once: one given at his own place, one given somewhere else, and one that comes from the plan and cannot be given or taken here at all.

Three things the list still has to do

  • A seat still asks before it lands. Super user, SMO team, Group CEO and Company CEO raise the question they raise today — after the list closes, not on the tick. That question exists because a custodian once ended up with Super user from one stray change of a dropdown, and a tick is exactly that stray change.
  • Roles that come from the plan are not in the list. Project owner, Pillar owner and Contributor come from being named on the work — they stay as grey chips saying where they come from. They already have no × today for the same reason.
  • A role only lands where the person sits. This register has never been able to reach a second place, so Company CEO and Function head are shown with the reason rather than quietly missing. Anything held elsewhere — five people have one — stays as a chip naming the place.

The one cost, and it is yours to decide

A unit has one owner and one custodian, so ticking a role takes it off whoever holds it now. That is already true today, one pick at a time. With ticks you could take three roles off three people in one press.

So the list says whose it is before you close it — you can see it in the shot above: Strategy custodian · held by Rania Fahmy, which is true on your register right now. And there is deliberately no Select all: giving one person every role is not an act anybody wants, and on a list that displaces people it is the one press this whole question is about.

Worth saying plainly: 26 of 33 people hold exactly one role, so ticking several at once is the minority case. What the list buys on every row is that today’s control is a plain dropdown with no search, and giving two roles is open-pick-open-pick.

Found while drawing it

Three things the picture caught

  • Save and Cancel would have scrolled off the screen. The shared open-row cell the other six tables use is not frozen to the right edge, and the register is the one Setup table wide enough to scroll. Measured: without the fix Save sits at x 1552 in a box that ends at 1401 — 151px past the edge. With it, x 1268. One class. This only matters if you pick A.
  • Two fixes I was sure of, and neither was needed. First a missing rule for dropdowns in an open row — measured the same width with it and without. Then the override for the overrun you spotted — also not needed, once the drawing drew the control the product actually draws. Neither is proposed. Both were caught by measuring rather than by reading, which is the only reason this page is not asking you to approve two rules that do nothing.
  • And the dark shot in the first drawing was a light one. The platform reads light-or-dark once when the page opens and writes it down, so telling the browser to go dark after that changes nothing. The caption said Dark, same build over a light picture — the worst kind of mistake, because at that size the words are what get read. It is genuinely dark above.
