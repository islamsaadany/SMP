Where we are

Today: three presses and a form of twelve

Changing one phone number costs the three-dots menu, then Edit details, then Save — and the form that opens holds every field there is, whichever one you came for, over the top of the table you were reading.

Half one

Editing in the row

Six of the seven Setup tables already edit in the row. The register is the only one that opens a form instead — so this is the odd one out rejoining the rest, not a new idea.

And the reason it was moved out was never that a form is better. It was two specific collisions: the old role picker drew two dropdowns inside a 150-pixel cell, which laid out side by side and landed on top of the Email box, which then took every click; and the Add-a-person row spanned nine columns, so none of its three boxes sat under its own heading. Neither is about the plain boxes.

Shape A — a pen at the end of the row

recommended

The shape the other six tables already use: press the pen, the row’s cells become fields, Save and Cancel replace the pen until you are done. Two presses instead of three, and the table stays where it was.

One limit worth knowing before you choose

Editing in the row can only ever reach a column that is shown. Emp. ID and Mobile are off by default and turned on under Columns; Full name, Sign-in name and Company are off too. The form reaches all twelve whatever is on screen, which is the clearest reason it stays.

Shape B — press the value itself

No new control anywhere. Press a value, that one cell becomes a field, and leaving it saves — which is how every other field in the platform already works. One press, and the table does not gain a pixel.

What shape B costs

Pressing the email copies it — you asked for that, and it is there because selecting text inside a table that scrolls sideways starts a scroll instead. If a press means edit, that one-press copy goes on the two columns you most want to edit. A double-click would keep both, and a tablet has no double-click.

Half two

Roles as a ticking list

Yes — and the platform already has the control. It is the searchable ticking list a tactic’s collaborators use, so nothing new is invented.

Drawn on Mostafa Deif, who on your register holds all three kinds of role at once: one given at his own place, one given somewhere else, and one that comes from the plan and cannot be given or taken here at all.

Three things the list still has to do

  • A seat still asks before it lands. Super user, SMO team, Group CEO and Company CEO raise the question they raise today — after the list closes, not on the tick. That question exists because a custodian once ended up with Super user from one stray change of a dropdown, and a tick is exactly that stray change.
  • Roles that come from the plan are not in the list. Project owner, Pillar owner and Contributor come from being named on the work — they stay as grey chips saying where they come from. They already have no × today for the same reason.
  • A role only lands where the person sits. This register has never been able to reach a second place, so Company CEO and Function head are shown with the reason rather than quietly missing. Anything held elsewhere — five people have one — stays as a chip naming the place.

The one cost, and it is yours to decide

A unit has one owner and one custodian, so ticking a role takes it off whoever holds it now. That is already true today, one pick at a time. With ticks you could take three roles off three people in one press.

So the list says whose it is before you close it — you can see it in the shot above: Strategy custodian · held by Rania Fahmy, which is true on your register right now. And there is deliberately no Select all: giving one person every role is not an act anybody wants, and on a list that displaces people it is the one press this whole question is about.

Worth saying plainly: 26 of 33 people hold exactly one role, so ticking several at once is the minority case. What the list buys on every row is that today’s control is a plain dropdown with no search, and giving two roles is open-pick-open-pick.

Found while drawing it

Two things the picture caught

  • Save and Cancel would have scrolled off the screen. The shared open-row cell the other six tables use is not frozen to the right edge, and the register is the one Setup table wide enough to scroll. Measured: without the fix Save sits at x 1601 in a box that ends at 1401 — 200px past the edge. With it, x 1268. One class.
  • A fix I was sure of turned out to change nothing. The rule that stops an open row widening the table names text boxes and not dropdowns, so I added the missing line — and measured 1498 with it and 1498 without. A cap one block below was already doing the job. The line is not proposed.
