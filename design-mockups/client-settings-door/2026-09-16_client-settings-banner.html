Where the door is — a row under the module rail's head, the exact mirror of the Back row the client rail already has. Same place on every module's rail, never under the fold.
The banner — a strip in the client rail, under its head: one line, folds open to the six steps. The pages themselves do not change shape, and it is on every client page without pushing any of them down.
Why recommended — the two rails become each other's mirror, and the set-up is buried where you said, inside the client's settings, without costing any page a line.
Cost: the strip is small, so "4 of 6 done" is the whole of what it says until pressed; it goes away once every step is done, with nothing to bring it back — the six pages are right under it.

What it buys: room for a sentence per step, and a page to send a new consultant to.

What is the same in A, B and C

The welcome screen loses the block and keeps everything else; the house mark inside the platform opens this landing, so there is one welcome screen and not two.
The six steps read the data — the register's count, whether the seats are placed, the units and functions, whether a mark and a sender are set — so a tick is never typed and never goes stale.
Who sees it: the client's Setup rail is the office's, as it is today; the door draws for whoever may open it and for nobody else.
Nothing stored, nothing migrated for A or C. B and A's Hide this store one mark on the group, as an absence, so a hidden banner stays hidden for the whole office.
