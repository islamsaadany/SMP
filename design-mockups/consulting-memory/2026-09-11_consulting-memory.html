Hiccup Raya Trade Distribution & retail

The weighting workshop ran twice because the CEO was not in the first one

12 August 2026 · during the plan build

What happened

We ran the weighting session with the ten unit heads over a full morning and came out with a set everybody had argued for and signed. We took the outcome to the group CEO the same week as a read-out rather than a decision.

What we did

He moved three weights inside ten minutes — Mobile up, two of the smaller units down. We went back to the heads individually rather than reconvening them, which took four days and two of them heard it from a colleague first.

What came of it

The weights are right and the room is not. Two heads have since described the workshop as “the one that got overruled”, and it comes up whenever we ask them to agree anything else.

What the next person should know

The weighting is the owner's decision, not the heads' — so either he is in the room, or the session is run as a recommendation and everybody is told that at the start. The cost is not the four days, it is that the next workshop starts from a room that has been overruled once.

Write up a period

Take this to your assistant and talk it through 
I have just finished a period of work at Raya Trade (distribution & retail),
covering 1 August to 11 September 2026. Help me write up what we learned from
it, for my firm's consulting memory.

Interview me. Ask ONE question at a time and wait for my answer before asking
the next one.

Start by asking me to walk you through the period — what we worked on and what
stands out. Then draw out, one at a time:

  · what went better than we expected, and why
  · what went wrong, or cost us time
  · what I know now that I did not know at the start
  · anything I would warn the next consultant about

For each thing I raise, ask me what happened, what we did, what came of it, and
what the next person should know. Push back when my answer is vague, and do not
move on until it would make sense to somebody who was not there.

When I say I am done, write every item back in exactly this shape, and nothing
else — no summary, no preamble:

===
KIND: hiccup | practice | lesson
TITLE: one line, the thing itself
WHEN: 1 August to 11 September 2026
WHAT HAPPENED: ...
WHAT WE DID: ...
WHAT CAME OF IT: ...
WHAT THE NEXT PERSON SHOULD KNOW: ...
===

Repeat that block for each item. Use my words, not yours. If I only half
answered something, leave that line out rather than filling it in.
Then paste what came back
Nothing is saved until you have read them.

What this drawing decides

  • A list, not cards. The client cards on the Clients tab are for choosing between ten things you already know. This is for finding one of eventually several hundred you do not, so it is a list you scan down with the fact line — client · industry · author · when — in one size and one colour on every row.
  • Two of the five example entries are deliberately short (§245). A layout drawn only on full write-ups is drawn on the one state it survives — a one-line insight has to look like a finished row, not like a broken one, or nobody writes the quick ones.
  • The author is a block, not a byline. Decision 3: what a reader does with an insight is go and ask the person who wrote it, so the name, the address and Ask … sit together at the foot with a rule above them, rather than being set in grey under the title.
  • Nothing counts or names who READ one (decision 4) — so there is nothing on this page for it, and that is the drawing being faithful rather than incomplete.
  • No description under any heading (rule 1b‑ii). The search box says what it searches in its own placeholder; the only sentence on the empty page is a fact the screen does not otherwise state.
  • The industry is drawn but never typed — it is the client's, read from the client's own card, so it is a filter and a chip and never a field on this form.
  • The four questions are the structure. Stored as prose under four headings, not as twenty fields — twenty fields is what nobody fills in once the engagement is over.
  • Copy the prompt sits under a rule, not in the flow. It is not step five of the form, it is an alternative to filling the form in at all — so it takes the page's own hairline-and-key device rather than looking like the next thing to do.
  • The period debrief is the main door, and the single insight is the exception. Islam: “it will not be case by case usually, it would be a period of time to share.” So Write up a period takes the loud button and Add one insight stands beside it quietly — the form is for the day something happens, the debrief is for the month you have just had.
  • One conversation, several insights. The debrief prompt interviews, then writes every item back in a fixed block, and the platform splits the paste into drafts. Storing the whole conversation as one entry would lose the filter, the search and the kinds — which is most of what the memory is for.
  • The prompt asks one question at a time and pushes back on a vague answer. That sentence is the difference between a debrief and a transcript; without it the assistant asks five things at once and gets five half-answers.
  • It forbids invention — and the review screen exists because that cannot be enforced. “Use my words, not yours. If I only half answered something, leave that line out rather than filling it in.” The platform cannot tell a paragraph somebody said from one a model completed, so a person reads every draft before it is saved. That is why nothing lands straight in the list, and it is not politeness.
  • A block it could not read is shown, never dropped (§184). It comes back in the warning ground with its text intact, so the one thing the platform never does with somebody's own words is lose them quietly.
  • The count and the rows are one list (§108.1) — three read, and one it could not, rather than four found over three that parsed.

What it costs

  • Every consultant reads every insight, including about clients they have never worked on. That is decision 1 and it is the feature working — it is also why the tab exists only behind Forefront's door and is never drawn inside a client's platform.
  • The industry filter is only as good as the client cards. Retail and retail & distribution typed on two cards make two industries, and this filter then answers half the question. Cheap to settle now, expensive in a year — flagged in the spec, and not done, because it touches the client card.
  • There is a screen between talking and saving, and it is deliberate. Six write-ups about real client situations, drafted by a model from a conversation, going straight into a memory the whole firm reads is the one thing worth a step. The cost is that a debrief is never one press.
  • The block shape depends on the assistant doing as it is told. Every assistant gets this right most of the time and none gets it right every time — which is what the amber block is: the honest outcome rather than a failure, still editable, still saveable.
  • The words on this page are placeholders. Insight, practice · hiccup · lesson, Add an insight — this is the drawing where they get read in place and settled, rather than argued in prose.
