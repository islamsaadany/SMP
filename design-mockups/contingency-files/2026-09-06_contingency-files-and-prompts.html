1What a “review deck builder” would be

You asked what I meant. Here it is without the jargon.

What exists todayone way of drawing a slide

The platform draws each review slide once, on screen. Present shows it. Print makes the PDF of it. Both are pictures of the same drawing, so they can never disagree.

  • One drawing, three uses.
  • Change a slide once — the projector, the PDF and the offline copy all change with it.
What a PowerPoint needsa second, separate drawing

A .pptx file is not a picture — PowerPoint has to be told where every box, table and colour goes, in its own language. So the same 21 kinds of slide have to be described a second time, from scratch.

  • About a day to write.
  • And then for ever: every future change to a slide has to be made twice, or the two drift apart.
  • This deck moved four times in the last fortnight.

The risk: the copy that goes stale is the one nobody opens until the emergency.

4The reminders — what you actually see

This is the part you said you did not follow. In plain words: as the review day gets closer, the platform starts asking the SMO team to take the files. It asks four times, it gets more insistent, and it stops the moment you have taken them.

The four reminders, counting back to the review
24 hours beforea quiet line on your welcome screen
12 hours beforesame line, now amber
6 hours beforea bar across the page
3 hours beforethe bar, and it names who still has not
Review day — 09:00reminders stop

Each person is asked separately, and each person’s reminders end when they have downloaded. Somebody who takes the files at 24 hours never sees the other three.

Question 3

Is the escalation right — two quiet ones on the welcome screen, then two as a bar across the page? Or should all four be the same, in one place?

One limit you should know before you decide

Nothing wakes this platform up on its own. A reminder appears the next time somebody opens it — so at three hours before, in the evening, with nobody’s tab open, nobody is reminded. An email has the same floor: it goes out on the next request anybody makes. The four reminders make that much less likely, but they cannot promise it.
