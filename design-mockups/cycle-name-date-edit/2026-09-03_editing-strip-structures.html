Editing the cycle strip

You are right on all three counts, and the third is the important one: nowhere else in SMP has a Save or a Cancel. Every other pen in the product writes as you type and the button just says Done editing. This banner invented a second model, and that is why it needs two buttons nothing else needs.

“why is the edit button still there it should turn into done editing so I click it and the box collapse saving what I did rather than having a save and cancel buttons inside the box itself”

TodayTwo models fighting

  • The button still says Edit while you are editing. The platform's own pen says Done editing and lights up — this one does neither.
  • Save and Cancel exist nowhere else in the product. A plan field, an objective, a person's row — all of them write as you leave the field.
  • 309px of banner to edit five short values.

Structure AThe values become the fields, where they are

Recommended

No box at all. The strip you are reading turns into the strip you are editing: the name becomes a field where the name was, the dates where the dates were, the month where the month was. Done editing is lit gold on the right, and Close the cycle sits beside it — still only reachable while the pen is open.

What it buys

  • It is what a pen means everywhere else here — you edit the thing where it is, and the button closes the mode.
  • Nothing is duplicated, because the fields have replaced the values rather than repeating them below.
  • One press in, one press out. No Save, no Cancel, no draft to be unsaved.

What it costs

  • No Cancel. A change is kept the moment you leave the field, exactly as every other field in SMP behaves — so an unwanted edit is corrected by typing again, not by backing out.
  • The OPEN badge and the 8 of 12 months line step aside while you edit. The line can stay as a hint under the month if you want it.

Structure BKeep the box, drop the two buttons

The smallest change

The banner stays where it is; Done editing moves to the strip and Save and Cancel go. A quiet line says changes are kept as you type, and Close the cycle keeps the far end.

What it buys

  • The least to change, and the least to re-test.
  • Room for the sentence about what the month decides.

What it costs

  • 253px, and the name still appears twice — once on the strip, once in the field below it.

Structure CFields on the left, ending it on the right

The most explicit

The destructive act gets a column of its own, behind a rule, with one line saying what it does — so it never sits in the same row as the fields you are typing in.

What it buys

  • Close is unmistakably a different kind of act — its own heading, its own sentence, its own side of a rule.

What it costs

  • 269px, the widest of the three, and the name still appears twice.
  • A 300px column spent on one button.

In all threeWhat changes regardless

  • Edit becomes Done editing, lit, using the platform's own pen wording and its own lit treatment — not a new vocabulary.
  • Save and Cancel go. Fields write when you leave them, like every other field in the product.
  • The guard that held Close back disappears with the draft. It existed only because there could be unsaved changes; with nothing unsaved there is nothing to hold. Close is still behind the pen, which is the part you asked for.
  • The closed cycle is untouched — Edit still opens the reopen dialog there.

The one thing worth deciding with your eyes open

Losing Cancel means a mistyped cycle name is saved the moment you click away. That is already true of every plan field, every objective and every person's row in SMP — but this name is what a closed cycle's figures get filed under, so it is the one field where I would understand you wanting the undo. Say so and B or C can keep a Cancel.
