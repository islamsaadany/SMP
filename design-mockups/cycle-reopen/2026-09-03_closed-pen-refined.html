The closed-cycle pen

My last two attempts changed the paint. The fault is the shape: a full-width band of facts, sitting directly under a full-width band of the same facts. No amount of alignment fixes two stacked bands saying the same thing. Three different shapes below — all shot on your tenant.

Shape ANo pen at all — Reopen is a control

The plainest

A closed cycle has nothing to edit, so it has no pen. Reopen is a quiet outlined button on the strip, going red only on hover; the confirmation carries the record. Nothing new is drawn on the page at all.

What it buys

  • Nothing to design and nothing to go wrong. The empty-value problem cannot occur, because no values are drawn twice.
  • One press. It is quieter than Open a new cycle…, which is the more destructive of the two.

What it costs

  • It reverses the guard you asked for — the strip carries a state-changing control again, though a milder one than Close.
  • The closing score has nowhere to live but the confirmation.

Shape BThe pen opens a card, anchored to itself

The compact one

Not a band across the page — a 340px card hanging under the button that opened it, the way the month picker already behaves. The facts are a proper two-column list, right-aligned values, and the action fills the foot.

What it buys

  • It belongs to its button, so it reads as that control's contents rather than as a second header.
  • Values align by construction — a definition list, not a row of boxes.
  • The guard survives: the act is still behind the pen.

What it costs

  • It floats over the table beneath it, and closes on scroll like every other popover.
  • A destructive act inside a popover is unusual — popovers are normally for choosing.

Shape CThe pen opens the platform's own dialog

Recommended

The record and the confirmation stop being two steps and become one thing: press Edit, and the platform's own dialog states what the cycle closed at, what it covers, and what reopening does — then asks.

What it buys

  • It removes a step rather than restyling one. Today it is a panel and then a browser confirm; this is one dialog.
  • It replaces a browser confirm() — which §95 already ruled against for the most irreversible act in the product, because a browser dialog can be permanently silenced on some other site and never appear again.
  • The platform's established shape for a decision (the merge wizard, the people dialog) — inert page behind it, focus returned, Escape closes.
  • No page real estate at all, so the empty-value problem cannot occur.

What it costs

  • The record is only visible while deciding, not while browsing.
  • Edit no longer means the same thing in both states — a panel while the cycle runs, a dialog once it has closed. Named, because it is the one thing I would push back on.

Why this is the recommendation

A closed cycle offers exactly one decision. A panel is the right shape for five fields you are editing, which is what the open cycle has — and the wrong shape for one question you are answering. C is the only one of the three that makes the screen simpler rather than tidier.

UnchangedThe open cycle keeps the panel you signed off

All three of these are about the closed state. While a cycle is running the pen still opens the five-field panel with Close the cycle at its far end — that shape is right, because there are five things to edit.
