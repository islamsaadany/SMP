Deleting for good

Three acts in the platform take something away with nothing behind them: a clip's bytes, a batch of them, and a filed archive. Everything else that reads like a deletion archives first — a plan replaced by an import, a pillar, a project, a retired person. These three are the exceptions, and they are the reason this shape has to be drawn rather than borrowed.

Today each one asks in the browser's box, which is why they are long: the box holds one run of text and no structure, so what goes, what stays and there is no way back arrive as one paragraph with blank lines in it. The platform's own dialog can lay those out as the three things they are. Nothing in the wording is being softened — if anything the proposed version says more.

Two words changed on purpose. Forget rather than Delete, because the thing in front of you is already a record of something deleted and “delete the archive of the deletion” is a sentence nobody can hold; the platform's own function is called forgetArchive. And the row's button would read Forget with it, so the control and the question use one word.

The shape, as one thing

All three are the same three statements in the same order: what goes, named precisely enough to recognise; what stays, because in every one of the three something does and the fear is that it does not; and there is no way back, which is the sentence the rest of the product can never truthfully say. The final button names the act. Cancel is on the left of it and carries no weight.

Worth stating plainly: the What stays row is the half the browser's box is worst at and the half that matters most. Twice out of three, the reason somebody hesitates is not “will this delete” but “what else does this take with it”.

What I need from you

  • The shape — yes or no. Goes / Stays / no way back, with the act named on the button. It is the platform's own dialog, the same frame the capability's removal uses.
  • Naming the twelve. It is the one real addition of substance here, and the one I would not ship without your word, because it makes that dialog the tallest in the product. Seven rows then scroll — or a flat count, as today, if you would rather it stayed short.
  • Forget or Delete for the archive, on the button and in the question. I recommend Forget.
  • Should any of the three ask for more than a press? Typing the file's name, say. My recommendation is no — nothing in the platform asks that today, and the act is reached only by somebody the server already lets destroy things. I raise it because these three are the only places where the answer could reasonably be yes.
