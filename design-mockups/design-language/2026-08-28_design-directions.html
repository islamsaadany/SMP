A Consulting Paper

The product as an advisory document. Warm paper neutrals, a serif voice (Newsreader) for what the practice says — titles and the headline figures — and hairline rules instead of boxes: cards become ruled sections, scores become set numerals with a fine underline meter. The client’s colours sit on it like a foil stamp on good paper.

  • Identity carrier: the serif + the warm paper. Both live entirely in the unbrandable half.
  • Closest relative: HR_ERP’s refined-consulting feel — cousin, not copy: rules-not-cards is its own stance.
  • Cost: ~90 KB embedded serif subsets in the offline file, and the warm neutrals touch every palette block.

B Command Ledger

The craft spent on the data itself. Cool graphite neutrals a step deeper than today, Archivo at heavy weight for titles and figures, mono numerals in the columns, and every score read off an inline meter — one glance down the column tells the story. Sharp 4–6px radius, a 3px accent spine on each card. The most authoritative room, and the cheapest migration: today’s bones, tuned.

  • Identity carrier: typographic weight + numeric discipline. Uppercase titles, tabular mono figures, meters.
  • Best for: a product whose every page is a table — which SMP mostly is.
  • Cost: ~55 KB Archivo subsets; the smallest token migration of the three.

C Soft Atlas

Air, light, and the client’s colour in the atmosphere. Manrope throughout at generous weights, 16px radius, layered soft shadows — and the washes (tab fills, table heads, the glow behind the page title) are mixed live from the client’s own accent with color-mix(), so the atmosphere itself re-brands. Pills for tabs and scores, a gentle lift on hover. The most modern room of the three.

  • Identity carrier: shape and depth — radius, layered shadow, tinted air. All unbrandable, yet the tint tracks the brand.
  • Best for: the dashboard and card pages; presentation mode would look superb in it.
  • Cost: ~45 KB Manrope subsets; shadow/radius retune across every component; needs color-mix() (fine in every browser the product supports).

What happens next, and what I need from you

  1. Pick a direction — A, B or C, or a blend (e.g. B’s tables inside A’s paper, which is a real option: the carriers don’t conflict).
  2. Confirm the font budget. The offline single file grows by the noted KB; today it embeds four trial faces we’d replace, so the net cost is small.
  3. Dark mode stays first-class in all three — flip your theme on this page to check each one. Tell me if any dark rendering reads wrong to you.
  4. A real second brand. Meridian and Aurelia are invented stress-tests. If you have an actual client’s bar + accent, give me the two hexes and I’ll add them to the switcher.

Once you choose, I write the reference file — the affirmative design-language statement (neutrals, faces, scale, shape, depth, motion) plus the token sheet — into the repo the way HR_ERP carries its own, and every future screen and mockup is built against it. Applying it to the live platform is a separate, later decision, page by page, with your sign-off each time.
