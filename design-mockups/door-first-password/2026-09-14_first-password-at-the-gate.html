First password at the gate

Raya Trade’s people have never been issued a login. You are about to issue one to all of them at once — and the door, as it stands, tells a first-time arrival nothing at all. Three places the message could go, each drawn at rest and refusing.

One thing first: the password itself never goes on this page

This address opens for anybody in the world — that is what a sign-in page is. A temporary password printed on it, next to a work email address anyone can guess from a name, is the whole of Raya Trade’s platform left open. So the door carries the instruction and you hand over the password yourself. Every drawing below is written that way.

Now

What your people met this week

The door says one sentence for a mistyped password and for an account that does not exist — it has to, or anybody could discover who is on Raya’s register by typing addresses. But it points nowhere, so somebody who has never been issued a password has no way to find that out.

What it costsA person with no login and a person who fat-fingered their password read the same sentence and are given the same nothing. Thirty people spent a day believing their password was wrong.
A

A standing line under the form

One quiet line below the button, on every client’s door, for ever. It says where a first password comes from and that it is temporary — before anybody has to fail to find out.

What it costsEverybody reads it every time, including the people who settled in months ago. On a page whose whole job is two fields, a permanent paragraph about a state most readers left behind is furniture — and this project has a rule against exactly that.
B

Only when the door refusesMy recommendation

The door is untouched until it turns somebody away. Then the refusal carries a second line naming the one place that can fix it — which is the rule this platform already follows everywhere else: a refusal says who can put it right.

What it costsNothing at all while the door is working — and that is the argument for it. The cost is that somebody is told after one failed attempt rather than before it. It also still gives nothing away: the same two lines appear whether the address is known or not.
C

A setting-up strip, while first passwords are outstanding

The platform knows how many logins on this client are still on their first password. Draw the strip only while that number is above nought, and it takes itself away the day the last person chooses their own.

What it costsIt is never furniture — it removes itself. But it tells anybody who opens this address that Raya Trade has people who have not signed in yet. That is a small thing to leak, and it is still the door saying something about the client, which it otherwise never does.
✓

The “change it after” half already works

You asked for two things. This one is built and has been since the door was written: sign in with a temporary password and the platform will not let you past this card. Nothing to decide — it is here so you can see what they land on.

What I need from you

  1. Which placement — A, B, C, or B and C together. I recommend B: it costs a settled reader nothing, and it is the only one that cannot become furniture.
  2. The wording. Every line above is mine. Change any of it — the door says what you want it to say.
  3. The hand-over sentence — worth building beside it, or leave it for now?
