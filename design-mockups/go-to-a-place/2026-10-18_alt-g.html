Going to a place

The last piece of heuristic 7. Alt+G opens a list of every place you can reach, you type a few letters, and Enter takes you there.

Almost none of this is new. The list is the navigation's own buttons, read straight off the page — so there is no second idea of what the places are, and no second way of getting to one: picking a row presses that place's real button, which is exactly how the product's existing go to this unit already works. The box and the rows are the searchable dropdown's own, and its keys came with it in §536.

An empty list with no sentence reads as a broken control. This one says what happened and what to press — and the foot drops the keys that would now do nothing, rather than naming keys with nothing behind them.

What this is, and what it is not

  • No second list of places. It reads the navigation's own buttons out of the page. A place added tomorrow appears here without anybody being told.
  • No second way of going somewhere. Picking a row presses the real button, so everything that happens on a normal click — leaving edit mode, landing on the right tab — happens here too, because it is the same press.
  • It shows only what you can reach. The buttons it reads are the ones the product drew for you, so somebody who holds one unit sees one place.
  • Places only, not tabs. Tabs got their arrows in §544; a list mixing the two would be two ideas in one box.
  • Alt+G is your letter, and it joins Alt+S, Alt+D and Alt+E — one modifier for everything that does something. It will not fire while you are typing.
