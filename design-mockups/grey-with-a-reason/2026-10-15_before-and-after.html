Why a button is grey

The product greys a control out in two ways. One leaves it alive: it looks grey, it refuses to act, and it still answers a pointer and the Tab key by saying why. That is Submit, and it is the rule the product set for itself. The other way is dead — the control becomes a picture, the pointer passes through it, Tab skips it, and nothing attached to it will ever be seen.

Four controls use the dead way, and each has an explanation written onto it. Four sentences in the product that nobody can reach. The audit named one of the four, and it is the mildest.

What this changes, and what it does not

  • The bubble is not new. It is the one Submit has used since §221, so there is no new shape to approve — only four controls joining it.
  • None of the four starts working. Each still refuses. What changes is that three of them can now say why, to a pointer and to the Tab key alike.
  • The wording is the product's own in all four cases. Nothing here is new copy.
  • One hidden repair comes with it: Apply to plan decides whether to act by asking whether it is dead. Once it is alive-but-refusing, that question answers wrongly and the button would start working. Its guard has to change in the same breath.
