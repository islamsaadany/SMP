The home mark is centred. The house inside it is not.

Measured on the running platform at 1500, 1280 and 1100 px and at 100%, 125% and 150% zoom — the same three numbers every time. The gold square sits dead centre in the navy bar and the drawing sits 6.5 px from all four of its edges, so §202's fix is intact. What is off is the painted house.

Two different causes, and neither is the box. A house has a pointed roof, so nearly all of its ink is in the lower half — its bounding box centres, its mass does not, and the eye reads mass. That is the 2 px. The half pixel across is arithmetic: a 21 px mark in a 34 px square leaves 13 px of slack, which does not halve to a whole pixel, so the browser snaps it to one side.

What it changes, and what it costs

  • Nothing moves on the row. The square stays 34 px in a 46 px bar, so no destination name shifts by a pixel — §41.8's rule, that the control you are about to press must not move.
  • The correction lives in the drawing, not in a CSS nudge. The path moves inside its own viewBox, so the four 6.5 px gaps checks/home-mark.py asserts stay true and go on meaning something — and a future mark inherits no offset meant for this one.
  • The mark goes 21 px → 20 px, which is what makes the 13 px of slack into 14 and stops the browser snapping it half a pixel sideways. The house is 5% smaller; at 34 px that is not a size you can see, and it is the only way to get a whole-pixel answer.
  • The cost of B is that 2 px is a judgement. It is measured rather than guessed, but a mass centroid is an approximation of what an eye does. If B reads high to you, A is the conservative half of it and the number can be anything between.
  • Nothing else is touched — not the gold, not the quiet state, not the box, not the hover, not what the mark does or when it turns gold.
