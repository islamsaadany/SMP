The header is the card's own top line: an icon, the title, a count, and its control on the right (Sort by on tasks; All tasks, All clients and the library as links), so each block reads as one object rather than a heading floating above a box. The tasks are one list without the two tabs: overdue first, its date in red, then this week, by date. The case studies' two halves sit in one card with a divider. A keeps the page light and lets the band stay the only navy; B is bolder and repeats the Tracker's navy, which with three cards is a lot of navy, so I would take A.

After · option A, tiles my pick

After · option B, one line each, the modules as words

Both drop the industry and the seat (the seat is on the client's own card under Clients). A gives each client a small tile: its mark and name on top, then its modules as a quiet list, where a module with something outstanding carries a small dot and the fact (amber for work owed, red for late or no plan) and the rest carry nothing. B keeps one line per client with the modules as plain words underneath, the mark only where there is one. A reads best with up to six clients; B is the better fit past that.

My tasks: Sort by date or by client

Before · live today, by date only

After · Sort by: Date (as today)

After · Sort by: Client

A Sort by switch sits on its own line under the band, beside Show (the admins' "whose tasks"). By date is today's Late / This week / Next week and later. By client makes each client a numbered section with its mark, its open count and its late count, clients with something late first; the client pill leaves the rows (the section says it) and the date column says Late in red where it is. The tick and the status work the same in both.
