The page, and where a button goes

Islam: “I need a mockup to refine this page and the buttons inside it as it’s too clumsy.” Three treatments, each drawn into the running platform rather than sketched beside it — so the type, the colours and every control below are the product’s own. The last of them is where Export everything would live.

As it is

Two states of one page. Nothing below is a matter of taste — each one is a number.

  • 01The controls are not beside the words that explain them, and they move. Step 1’s sentence ends at x 898 and its switch begins at x 1180 — on a row that starts at 286, so 894px of that row is empty before the first control. And it is a different number at every window size: 1180 / 860 / 680 at 1600, 1280 and 1100. The cause is one declaration, .minisw { margin-left:auto } — the first auto margin in a flex row eats all the free space. arrange.css already records that trap, at line 237, about a different row.
  • 02Progress mode puts six controls in five treatments on that one row — a segmented switch, a gold pill menu, a subject picker, and two bare link-buttons — running from x 830 to the pane’s right edge. Nothing says which of them is the one to press.
  • 03The control that governs the page is the smallest thing on it. Plan | Progress is 42×21 and 65×21 pixels, attached to the download button — so it reads as an option of that button. It decides both steps, every sentence on the page, and what the upload will accept.
  • 04The one control that does the work is the only unstyled thing on the page. The upload is the browser’s own Choose File, among six controls that all wear the product’s dress.
  • 05“Build it here” is a full-width amber band above the page’s own content — louder than anything the page is for, and amber is the platform’s warning ground.
  • 06“Archived plans” is written twice — the tab, and the heading directly under it.
  • 07And there is nowhere to put Export everything. The only row that holds controls is already full, and it is at the wrong end of the page. That is the part worth settling before it is built.

Three treatments

They are nested rather than rival: B is A plus panels, and C is B with the mode and the export moved. So picking one is really deciding how far to go.

A — straighten what is there

727 → 742px

The controls sit under the sentence that explains them; the mode switch moves onto the section line, where it governs the whole section rather than one button; the upload gets a real control.

  • MovesOne CSS declaration, the switch, and the file input. No markup restructured.
  • ExportHas nowhere to go but inside the Download menu — which is why you cannot see it in the picture.

What it costs. It fixes the misalignment and not the emptiness: the pane is 1325px and the page still uses about half of it. And the export would sit behind a button reading Download plan template, which it is not.

B — the controls get a place of their own

727 → 784px

Each step becomes a panel: the sentence at the top, a control strip along its foot. The gap between a button and its explanation becomes a panel gutter rather than dead page, and the fact about the file sits at the far end of the same strip instead of on a line of its own. Export everything becomes a third panel — which is what makes it findable.

  • MovesThe step body gains a foot; the export gets a card of its own.
  • ExportIts own row on the page, named in words.

What it costs. The tallest of the three, and the numbered steps now sit inside boxes — a heavier page than a plain sequence. The mode switch is still a small control on a heading line.

C — the mode becomes a tab

Recommended 727 → 642px

Plan or Progress is not a setting on a button — it is which of two things this page is doing, and the page already has a row for that. Import a plan · Import progress · Archived plans. The switch is deleted rather than restyled, the duplicated heading goes with it, and Export everything sits on the page’s own header line.

  • MovesThe mode leaves the step and becomes a tab; the export goes to the header line; B’s panels stay.
  • Precedent§130 put every Setup page’s controls on its header line. This is one of the few pages that never got one.
  • Height642px, against 727 today — the only one of the three that makes the page shorter.

What it costs. Three tabs where there were two, and one of them is not an import — the row stops meaning the two halves of importing and starts meaning the three things this page does. The Plan import heading goes, because the tab says it.

C = B + the mode as a tab + the export on the header line.
