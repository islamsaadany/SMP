Decision 1

Which destinations carry the tab

This is the cost you accepted when you picked C, so it is worth seeing once. The list is the client's, not the unit's — so it is identical whichever destination you are standing on.

What I would do: draw it on every destination a person can open — a unit, a supporting function, a capability, a company and the group. A tab that appears on some pages and not others is worse than one that repeats, because you have to remember where it lives. The repetition is the honest picture of what you chose: the door is in a familiar place, and the room behind it is the client's rather than the unit's.
Decision 2

What the address says, and what a sent link opens

Every tab writes itself into the address, so pressing this one would ordinarily produce:

smp…/raya-trade/strategy/mobile/insights

That names Mobile for a list that has nothing to do with Mobile, and it is the one place the untruth would outlive the screen — somebody sends that link and the person who opens it lands on Mobile.

It does not have to. The tab can write the module's own address instead, without leaving the page:

smp…/raya-trade/insights

What I would do: the second. Nothing about a unit ends up in a link about the reports, and a link somebody sends opens the reports. The cost, stated: if they then reload that page, they get the Insights page on its own rather than the tab — the same reports, the plainer chrome. Pressing Back from the tab still returns to whatever they were reading.
Decision 3

What happens to the four-square mark

With a tab, a unit head has no use for it at all — it would be a second door to the one place they can already reach. The office still needs it, because the Internal Tracker and Meeting Notes are theirs and have no tab.

What I would do: draw the mark only where there is something the tabs cannot reach. That is one rule rather than a design per role, and it closes the fault the first drawing found for free — a user is never again offered the office's two lists, because the menu that offered them is no longer drawn for them.
What changes about searching
A filtered library stops being a link you can send — from inside the tab. On the Insights page the search and the category are in the address, so "here are the market reports" is something you paste to somebody. Inside the platform the filter is a state of the screen rather than part of the address, the way every other filter in the product already is. The module's own page keeps its behaviour unchanged, so the sendable filtered link still exists — it is just not what the tab produces.
Not a decision — a note

The notices dot lands on the tab

The gold dot from the notices drawing sits on the modules mark today. With the mark gone for the people it is meant for, it moves onto the tab — shown in the first drawing. The email and who receives it are unchanged.

Say yes, or change one

  1. Every destination carries the tab — or name the ones that should.
  2. The address stays the module's own, so links are about the reports and not about a unit.
  3. The mark is drawn only where the tabs cannot reach — so the office keeps it and a user never sees it.
  4. The word on the tab: Insights, or something plainer like Reports? It is the module's name everywhere else, so changing it here means changing it everywhere.
  5. And the three still open on the notices drawing: the subject line, whether you get a copy, and whether a corrected report tells people twice.
