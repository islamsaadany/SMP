The question

A person who has two places to be should not have to find a menu

Today the reports live behind an unnamed four-square mark at the far top-left of the bar — a different row from all the navigation, and the word only appears if you hover it. For the office, which manages modules, that mark is right. For a unit head, who has exactly two places, it is a hidden menu of two. That is the complaint, and it is fair.

Two costs, and the second is the one I would not spend.
  • The row stops being true. Those three tabs are three views of Mobile — what we agreed, how it is going, what we are reporting. The reports are the same library whatever unit you stand on. Beside them, a divider or not, Insights reads as Mobile's reports. Press Retail Stores and it would show exactly the same list.
  • Or it costs a second copy of the library. If the tab is a link it crosses to the Insights page like the other options, and the row has lied for nothing. If it is a real tab, the strategy page has to draw the list of reports itself — a second renderer for something drawn in one place today. Two screens showing one list is the fault this product keeps finding and fixing; I would rather not add one deliberately.

What this does to the notices drawing

The mockup you already have puts the "2 new reports" dot on the four-square modules mark. If a user is not meant to use that mark, the dot is in the wrong place for exactly the people it is for. It moves onto whichever of these you pick — shown above as the small gold dot. The email is unchanged, and so is everything about who receives it.

What none of these changes

Who can see which reports, who publishes them, and the fact that publishing stays Forefront's on the client's card in the console. This is about where a door is drawn, not about what is behind it.

What I need from you

My recommendation is B. It is in the navigation proper, at a weight people read, always in the same place, and it uses a rule the row already has for things that belong to the whole client rather than to one unit — the same rule the settings gear sits under. C is the most findable and the row would be saying something untrue; A is the cheapest and the quietest.
