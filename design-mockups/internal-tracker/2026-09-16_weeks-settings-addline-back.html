What this costs, said before you choose.

A week is a day underneath. Nothing new is stored: picking a week writes that week's Thursday into the same date field, so late and "carried" go on being worked out the same way, and a list read by somebody with exact dates on shows the same Thursday. A row set by week reads This week, or its week number — W40 — with the days on the hover; once its Thursday has passed it reads Late 1 w, because in weeks a row is late from the Friday. A day chosen by the calendar shows as its week to somebody reading in weeks — a Monday is "this week" there.

A new line is due this week, not undated. Today Enter lands a line with no date. Drawn here, the add line starts on This week, so Enter lands it due this Thursday, and No date is a choice on the picker rather than the default. If you would rather a new line start with no date, say so — it is one word in the drawing.

Dates as and Group by are this browser's, like Group by is today — remembered as a cookie, never stored on the client, so two of the office can read one list two ways. The Due date grouping under weeks groups by the week rather than the day.

The note on the add line is the action's own notes, the same box the arrow opens afterwards — one line here, Tab reaches it, and it is always drawn under the action so nothing on the right moves. Enter from either line adds it all.

The way back sits above the title, where a back link is expected, and goes to the client's platform — Strategy, where the modules menu also leads. Your sentence ended at "to the", so if you meant somewhere else, say which.

The status on the add line stays Not started: you named the date, the owner and a note, and a line that starts In progress is one press on the row after.
