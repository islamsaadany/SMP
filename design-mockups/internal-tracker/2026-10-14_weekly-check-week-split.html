The weekly check — splitting the weeks

Islam: “the titles of last week this week and next week needs to be maybe highlighted as a full line to show the separation, as they are not clear somehow.”

He is right about why: what is there now announces a week, it does not separate one. A small word and a hairline sit at the same weight as the ruled rows around them, so the eye reads one long list with labels dropped into it. Four ways to make the split actually split, all on the same topic and the same rows, so only the separator differs.

What you asked for, literally: a full line, filled. The three weeks are one family at three weights — last week grey and settled, this week gold and live, next week an open dashed outline — so which one you are in is readable from across a meeting room. The days move to the far right, where the counts already sit, so the band has a left edge and a right edge instead of trailing off. Cost: three filled bands inside every topic, and there are four to eight topics on the page — a lot of tint on one screen.

A real rule, not a hairline. The line runs the full width and the week word straddles it, the way the topic heading already sits on its own dark rule — so the page keeps one language: a rule means a new section. This week's rule is gold and thicker; next week's is dashed, which reads as “not written yet” without any fill. Cost: it separates but does not enclose — the rows below still belong to the week only by being below it.

Cost, and it is a real one: every row shifts 18px right, so the ticks, names and statuses no longer line up with the topic heading above them — on a ledger page where every column lines up down the whole list, that is the thing most likely to look wrong once there are six topics.

Mine would be A, because it is what you asked for and it survives a glance from across the room, which is what a weekly check needs. B is the one I would argue for if the page starts to feel loud once there are six topics on it: it is the same separation done with a rule instead of a fill, and the page already uses a rule to mean “new section” at the topic heading. The two are the same idea at two volumes, so starting at A and turning it down to B later costs nothing.
