Refining what the assistant says, on the page it says it from

One pen on the Knowledge base page. What you edit there is what people read and what the bot answers from — the two can never disagree.

The door

The page as it is today, plus one control — the office's own edit idiom, on the page's header line.

The pen open

Every scenario becomes Question + Answer in place. Three states, each wearing its own mark.

  • An untouched entryShows the standard wording, editable in place. Typing into it makes it yours.
  • Edited for this platformYour wording, shown to everyone and answered by the bot. Back to the standard wording is always one click — the shipped text is never lost.
  • YoursA question you added — client-specific scenarios the standard corpus does not cover. Only these can be removed; a standard entry can be edited but never deleted, so the bot never loses a whole answer.
  • + Add a questionAt the foot of each group, so a new scenario lands where readers will look for it.
  • ParagraphsA blank line in the answer box is a paragraph break, exactly as the page draws them.

How it holds together

Your edits are stored on your tenant and laid over the shipped corpus — for the page, for the assistant, and for the section list, in the same breath. Saving works like every other page: the autosave, the same server-side authorisation, office only.

The tenant's vocabulary still applies on top: where an answer says “pillar”, a platform that renamed pillars reads its own word, in your edits as in the shipped text.

The one honest cost

An entry you have edited stops receiving improvements to the standard wording — that is the point of overriding it, and it cuts both ways. If we sharpen the shipped answer later, your version stands. The Edited chip is what makes those entries findable when you want to reconsider one.
