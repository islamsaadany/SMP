Master presentation

“For the presentation give an option for the SMO from the presentation list to do master presentation — a flow of presentations in a flow. He is just asked the flow of the units and functions who will present, he makes the flow, and all the slides are put back to back to be presented in one flow.”

Nothing is built. Everything below was drawn inside the running platform on the worked example, so what is shown is the product rather than a picture of it — the decks are real decks, assembled by the same builder the Present button calls. Six decisions are waiting at the end.

01What the platform already does

Measured before anything was proposed.

A subject's deck is assembled fresh every time it is opened, by one reader (deckHtmlFor), with the custodian's picture slides inserted, the office's hidden slides removed and the subject's own mark footed onto every page. Putting three of those decks end to end and opening them as one runs first time, and every pass still does its job.

02Where the entry lives

A fourth line in the menu that is already there.

The Presentation dropdown holds the decks: Present opens the review, Manage slides arranges it, Download the plan takes it away as a file. A master flow is a fourth thing to do with the same decks, so it costs no new control anywhere on the page — and like the download, it is narrowed to the office in the rule rather than in the menu, so the entry and the press cannot answer differently.

03Making the flow

Two shapes, both drawn in the platform's own dialog. Pick one.

Recommended: A. There is one list of subjects in the product and one order to put them in; two columns describe the same fact twice and make the picker a place where things are dragged between boxes. A also reads at a glance when nine of eighteen are presenting, which is the ordinary case — B splits that answer across two columns.

04Six decisions

Each one changes what gets built. The recommendation is mine; the call is yours.

1

Who may open it

  • Your words

    The SMO alone — the same narrowing as Download the plan, so the entry is simply not drawn for anybody else.

  • Cost

    A unit head cannot rehearse the flow their own deck sits in. They can still present their own.

2

Which shape the picker takes

  • Recommend

    A, one list. One list, one order, the arrows in the rows they move.

  • Or

    B, two columns. The flow stated separately, at the price of the width and a second place to look.

3

Is the flow remembered

  • Recommend

    Remembered. The order a board meeting runs in is agreed once and used every cycle; re-picking eighteen names before every meeting is real work. It would be stored on the group, as an absence — a tenant that has never opened this has nothing stored, and the picker opens on the order the cycle board already uses.

  • Or

    Asked every time. Nothing stored, nothing to authorise, and the picker starts from the board's order each time.

  • Cost

    Remembering means the server has to judge the change — one classification, the office's, beside the ones already there.

4

The Thank you between subjects

  • Recommend

    Only the last one keeps it. Seventeen Thank yous in the middle of a review read as the meeting ending, seventeen times.

  • Or

    Every deck whole — literally what was asked for, back to back, byte for byte what each subject sees when they present alone.

  • Cost

    Dropping it means a deck inside the flow is not identical to the same deck opened on its own. Nothing is deleted and no figure moves; it is a slide the flow does not draw.

5

What the bar says

  • Recommend

    The bar reads Master presentation · H1 2026 today. In a flow it should name the subject you are standing in — Retail Stores · 2 of 3 · H1 2026 — or seventy slides say one thing throughout.

6

Getting about a long deck

  • Measured

    The strip of dots under the deck draws one dot per slide. At 71 it already wraps to three rows and spills past the bar; at 335 it is unusable. This is true of the product today and the flow is what exposes it.

  • Recommend

    One dot per subject in a master flow — eighteen dots that jump to a subject's cover, with the counter still counting slides. It wants its own drawing before it is built.

05What this does not touch

Named so that nothing is discovered later.

  • Present and Manage slides are unchanged for every subject.
  • No score, figure or note is read, written or moved.
  • Hidden slides travel with each subject, exactly as they do alone.
  • Picture slides travel with each subject, in their own places.
  • Each subject's mark stays on its own slides.
  • Nothing is exported — the flow is assembled when it opens, as every deck already is.

Nothing has been built. On sign-off of the six decisions above this becomes one entry, one dialog, and one opener that walks the chosen subjects through the deck builder that already exists — plus whatever decision 6 settles.
