What the trial module opens on — and why the client’s name is the whole point

A static “hello” proves nothing: it would look identical on a page that never reached the client at all. A greeting that knows which client it is proves the module went through the spine, found this tenant, and read it.

Hello, Raya Trade.

This is the Trial module. It does nothing else — it exists to prove one can be added.

It read this client’s own register: 33 people

Three things this picture is claiming, and each is a thing that can fail: the name and the count come from this client’s data, not from the page; the bar is in this client’s colours, which the module gets free because branding is the spine’s; and there is no row of units, because a module brings its own navigation or none — the Strategy platform’s does not leak into it.

Three things that are yours

  • A or B for where the modules sit. I recommend B.
  • The word “Trial” — it is deliberately not a product name, so nobody can mistake it for something they bought. Say if you would rather it were called something else, or hidden from clients entirely and only ever switched on for the demo.
  • The bar says the product’s name, then the module’s — “Strategy Management Platform · Raya Trade › Trial”. That is E1 as you signed it off, and it reads slightly oddly the moment the module is not Strategy. Worth a look now that there is a second word to put there; changing it is not part of this slice unless you say so.
