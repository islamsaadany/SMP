The Forefront platform

Two corrections from Islam. The login page is only a login page, exactly as a client's is — everything else lives inside a platform with its own chrome. And a consultant's role is not a platform-wide thing: it is the seat they hold on a particular client, set on that client's configuration.

Proposed Not built · the current build puts these on the door
01

What changes

One screen moves, and everything on it moves with it.

Built today, and wrong: after signing in, the door itself grew the client cards, then the consultants table, then the access table. The page that should do one thing was carrying the whole product — and a login page with tabs is not a login page.

Proposed: the door signs you in and hands you over. The Forefront platform is a platform: a top line saying who you are, a navigation row of its own, and pages under it — Clients · Consultants · Who sees what. Opening a client leaves it for that client's SMP, and the client's name in that chrome brings you back.

Also built, and also wrong: a consultant carried ONE role across the whole platform — Admin, Lead, Consultant, Observer — and that role decided what they could do everywhere. It does not survive Islam's correction: "the roles are on the client level."

Proposed: two levels, each answering its own question. The platform has one admin — adding clients, adding consultants and setting the table below. Everything else is a seat on a client: Super user or SMO team, the client's own two, set on that client's configuration. Somebody can be Raya's Super user and on RHI's SMO team and nothing at all on El Abd.

This is where a consultant's role is set, and it is set per client: the seat they hold here. Super user holds this client's access matrix, retiring people and issuing passwords; SMO team runs cycles and corrects plans. One person can be Raya's Super user and on RHI's SMO team. Removing somebody retires their row on this client's register rather than deleting it. One super user per client — picking a second moves the seat rather than adding one.

Inside: who sees what

Nothing lit means nothing at all. What somebody may do INSIDE a client is not here — it is the seat they hold on that client, set on its configuration, and the client's own Roles & access decides the rest exactly as it does today.

  • Three states in the first column, not two: a client you hold no seat on can be hidden, listed (so the office knows what Forefront runs) or open to read. This is the setting Islam asked for when he said whether a consultant sees other clients belongs here.
  • The old four roles are gone. Admin · Lead · Consultant · Observer made sense only while a role was platform-wide; with seats on clients, a new joiner on no client already sees nothing, and a partner's standing is the seats they hold.

08

What I need from you

Sign off, or mark up. The pages themselves are the ones you have already approved — what is new here is that they sit inside a platform with its own chrome and navigation, and that the door does nothing but sign you in. Nothing moves in the code until you say.

  • The navigation: Clients · Consultants · Who sees what. Is that the right three, in that order?
  • The chrome: a light top line with the Forefront mark, and the navy navigation row under it — the same two-line shape a client's platform has.
  • A client's configuration opens from its own card. The alternative is a fourth entry in the navigation; I think the card is the better place, because the configuration is about one client rather than about all of them.
