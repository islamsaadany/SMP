Talking to the Strategy Office

A conversation between one person and the office, opened from a bubble in the bottom-right corner of every page, answered from a Setup page under Running the cycle. It is not a new idea so much as the missing half of one: §71 built the feedback tables, the endpoint and the rules, and the box that was meant to sit in that corner was never drawn. This is that box, reshaped from a form into a conversation.

1 · The corner

new

On every page, for everybody signed in. Quiet when there is nothing to say; it carries a count when the office has answered.

The bubble wears --panel — the tenant’s own bar colour, the thing Setup › Branding already sets — because it is chrome, not content. It follows the client’s brand the way the navigation bar does, so it never has to be re-decided per tenant (§41.10).

The count is the only accent on the screen it appears on. Gold, solid, 20px — a mark, not a slab, which is the accent budget’s own test (§41). It is a count of replies you have not read, never a nag: if nobody has written to you, there is nothing on the bubble.

It is not drawn everywhere. Not in Presentation mode — a chat bubble on a projector in front of the board is the wrong kind of surprise. Not from file://, where there is no server to carry a message and a control that cannot work is worse than no control (§16.7’s rule). Not on the sign-in gate, where nobody is anybody yet.

One conversation per person, never a list of tickets. You asked for something that sounds like a chat, and the moment a person has to decide “is this a new issue or the same one?” it has stopped being one. The office does that sorting on its own side, where it belongs — which is also exactly the queue it needs: these six people are waiting.

Where you were is captured, not typed (§71’s rule, unchanged). The page, the unit and the cycle are things the screen already knows; asking somebody to describe them is asking them to do the computer’s job, and they would get it wrong more often than the machine does. It shows under your own message so nothing is sent about you that you cannot see.

The screenshot is §71’s, already built and already tested — shrunk to 1600px, encoded as both PNG and JPEG with the smaller kept, capped at 3 MB, and refused outright if it is a URL rather than a picture. Nothing new to decide.

Replies are signed. You write to the office — so nobody is a dead end when somebody is on leave, and you never have to guess which of us owns your question — but the answer comes back with a name on it, because a reply from an institution is not a reply.

Reply to Ahmed…

The queue is people, not tickets — your own words: picks the people and replies to them. Two groups and no invented statuses: waiting on us, and answered. A conversation moves to answered when the office replies, and comes back the moment the person writes again. Nobody has to remember to set anything, which is the status nobody sets (§71).

Flagging is the office’s, and it is per-message. This is where §71’s issue / idea / question goes: the person should not have to classify their own question before they are allowed to ask it, but the office needs the three real bugs out of thirty conversations. Flag a message, and it is on the Flagged list for as long as you need it.

It is not the whole window’s width by accident — this is a Setup page like every other, inside the rail, and since §94.13 that means the page uses the whole window. The list column is fixed and the thread takes the rest.

Every row is one line, clipped, with the whole value one hover away (§88) — a message is the one thing on this screen that is arbitrarily long, so if the rows wrapped, the queue’s height would depend on how much somebody typed.

4 · The rules underneath

mostly already built

What is stored, who may read it, when it leaves the platform, and how it stays live without a socket.

QuestionAnswer, and why
Who may write Anybody signed in — §71’s rule exactly: the people most likely to spot a wrong number are the ones with the least access, and a box only they cannot reach is worse than none.
Who may read all of it The office — inOffice(), so Super user and SMO team (§89). Everybody else reads their own and nothing else. This is a rule, not a matrix cell: reading what people wrote in confidence is not something to leave to a tick, and §37 already settled three cells that way.
Where it lives Its own tables and its own endpoint, outside the state graph — a save TRUNCATEs thirty tables, and a message a save can erase is not a message. No foreign key to people, so a person removed from the register does not take the conversation with them (§69.23).
How it stays live Polling, because Vercel serves static files and functions and there is no socket to hold open. Three cadences: 4s while the panel is open, 60s for the badge while it is not, and on window focus. One small indexed query each.
When an email goes The reply is emailed only if the person is not on the platform — their own polling is what says so, and the office is told which way it will go before pressing Send, on the line above the box. Uses the §72 mail rail unchanged.
What it costs the tenant Two tables and one endpoint. The screenshots are the only real storage, and they are §71’s cap and §71’s drop already built.

The one piece with no clean answer is the deferred email, and it is worth saying plainly rather than discovering later. There is no scheduler here — no cron in vercel.json — so the choice is made at the moment of replying: are they here right now? Somebody who was reading a page thirty seconds ago and then shut their laptop gets no email. The mitigation is that the office can see the call being made and override it, which is why that line sits above the reply box instead of being a silent rule. A proper sweep needs a cron entry, and that is a later decision, not a blocker.

What I need signed off before touching any source

  1. The corner. One bubble in the tenant’s bar colour, a gold count for unread replies, absent in Presentation, on file:// and at the gate.
  2. One running conversation per person — no subject, no ticket, no kind to choose. The office classifies on its side by flagging individual messages.
  3. The office is the addressee; replies are signed by name.
  4. Setup › Running the cycle › Messages, second in the group, with the waiting count on the rail. Two groups: waiting on us, answered.
  5. Email only when they are away, with the decision shown to the office before they send.

What this replaces: §71’s feedback form, which has a server but no screen. Its tables and endpoint carry almost unchanged — a conversation is a feedback row with a thread on it, which is what feedback_replies already is. Nothing is thrown away and nothing is migrated away from.

What it costs: per-item statuses (new / open / done / parked) stop being a thing the product tracks, because the unit is now the person and not the ticket. Flagging is what is left, and it is deliberately weaker. If you want a real issue tracker as well, say so now — it is a different feature and it should not be smuggled in behind a chat bubble.

Where it lands: the current stack, like §71 and §74 — the single built file plus one serverless function. When R2 moves the shell onto Next.js (D5, still waiting on you), this ports with everything else.
