3How it behaves

The split

Waiting is the office’s work queue. My messages is their own conversation — the thing the bubble is today, kept for testing and for talking to the assistant. Two segments, one control, no second button hidden in a menu.

The badge

The number on the bubble counts people waiting, and it is the same number on the Waiting segment and the same length as the list. You asked for messages; I’d rather it match what you can see, because a badge saying 7 over four rows gets reported as a bug. Say the word and it counts messages instead.

Replying

Answering a conversation stops it waiting, so it leaves the list — but not while you are reading it. It drops off when you go back. We were bitten by exactly this on the Inbox page: you reply and the thing in front of you disappears.

Search

Reaches every message in every conversation, whether or not it is waiting. So the screen says so — “3 found in all conversations, waiting or not” — and each row shows the matching line rather than the last one, with the word marked. Otherwise a result appears with no visible reason for appearing.

4What this costs, before you approve it

Said plainly

Searching history is a question for the server, not the screen. Today the search filters what is already loaded. Reaching old messages means asking the database each time somebody types — so it waits for a pause in typing, and on a very large history it will be a moment, not instant.

Two of you in the queue at once. If a colleague answers somebody while you are looking at the list, that row disappears from yours on the next refresh. That is correct — it is answered — but it will feel abrupt the first time.

The corner is small. With many people waiting the list scrolls inside the bubble. The full Inbox page stays for anything more than a quick reply.

5The email rule, in the same breath

Separate from the drawing, and worth deciding together because it is the same feature area.

The fault today: the platform decides whether to email at the instant the office replies. If the person was on a page thirty seconds earlier they count as present, so no email is sent — and if they then shut their laptop and never come back, they are never told at all. Nothing visibly fails.

The proposal: decide later, not at send time. Email when the reply has gone unread for a while, rather than when the person merely looked absent.

Present, never came backNothing, ever Emailed after the wait

Everything it needs is already recorded — when the reply went, whether they have seen it, whether an email already went. Nothing new to store.

The honest limitation

There is no scheduler on our hosting, so the check happens on the way past, on ordinary requests — the same trick the platform already uses to clear expired sign-in attempts. If literally nobody touches the platform, nothing goes out until somebody does. In practice somebody is on it during working hours, and a proper scheduled job can be added later without changing the rule.

One number to agree: how long before it chases. I’d suggest 30 minutes, as a setting beside the away threshold — long enough that somebody reading their message is not emailed about it, short enough to be same-morning.
