01

The owner becomes a pick from the register

“for the owners in the plans, projects, tactics, milestones, let it be a searchable list from the registry” Islam, 27 August

What is there now

An owner is a typed line of text in three places behind the plan pen: a project’s owner, a milestone’s owner, and a tactic’s owner. Nothing checks it against anything.

38different owner names written across the demo plan
14of them name nobody on the people register
32 of 78tactics whose owner the platform cannot recognise

Why this is not only tidiness

A tactic’s owner is matched against the register by name to decide who may report that line — that is what makes somebody a Contributor of the unit. So a tactic owned by “Karim” or “Hossam” matches nobody, and the person who owns the line cannot enter its figure. It happens on 32 of the 78 tactics in the demo. Project and milestone owners are labels only — they decide nothing — but they are the same field to the person filling it in.

What I would do

The owner cell becomes a list of the people on the register. Past five names the platform already turns any list into a search box, so it arrives searchable without building a second control. A name already stored that is not on the register is kept at the top of its own list rather than shown wrong — nothing in a plan already uploaded is lost or silently changed.

Cost

Three owners in the demo are departments, not people — Finance, IT, Treasury on capability milestones. With a list of people, a department can no longer be typed as an owner; it would have to become a row on the register. The ones already stored keep showing, but nobody could write a new one.

Kept

What is stored stays the name, not a person key. Storing a key would be a database migration and would change the plan template, the upload and the export — more than this asks for, and it can be done later without redoing this.

Two things to answer

1. Should the list be people only — the tidy answer, and the one that makes the reporting match work — or should it also accept a name typed in, for the department case?

2. A pillar also carries an owner (it shows in the rail under the pillar’s name), and it is read-only everywhere in the product — there is no way to correct it at all. Should it join the other three?

02

A rail even when there is one

“in case of the function has 1 capability keep the rail there to keep the standard view even with 1 capability either in the strategy or the performance or reporting” Islam, 27 August

What is happening

The rail is dropped whenever the list it would hold has fewer than two things in it. It is not about capabilities as such — it is the list of projects inside one. Marketing shows both behaviours on one page: Brand Positioning has two projects and gets a rail; Product Mindset has one and does not — so two capabilities on the same screen are laid out differently and the project title starts at a different place down the page.

What I would do

Show the rail from one item. One rule, read in all four places it is asked — a capability’s projects, and a unit’s pillars on Plan, Performance and Reporting — so the two sides of the Units | Functions switch cannot drift apart again.

Cost

About 212px of width goes to a list with nothing to move between. That was the argument for dropping it, and standing still beats saving the width.

Note

With the pen on, the rail is already always drawn — Add a project lives in it. So this makes the reading view match the editing view rather than inventing a new one.

What I need from you

You named functions. I would apply it to units as well — a unit with one pillar loses its rail the same way, and changing one side of the switch and not the other is the drift the house rule exists to stop. Say if you want functions only.

03

The pinned title’s corners

“a very small visual glitch that on scrolling up in the pillars or project with the sticky title. the title has round corners so the scrolled up appears in the right and left top corners — have a look and tell me what is the fix” Islam, 27 August

What it is

You are right about the cause. The strip above the pinned title is already painted over with the page’s own background — that fix went in months ago. The two corners are not. The title’s grey does not reach into a rounded corner, so what shows there is the white card behind it and whatever row happens to be sliding past.

The fix, and why it cannot simply be “square it while pinned”

CSS cannot ask whether a pinned header is currently pinned. That is why the strip above it paints in both positions, and it is why any fix here paints in both positions too. So: fill the two corners with the page background — the same colour, from the same two numbers, as the strip already above them. One rule, no new machinery, no scroll watching.

The alternative

Square the title’s top corners outright. It also fixes it — and it disturbs twice as much at rest (measured: 26 px² against 13), because the grey corner then pokes about 2px outside the card’s rounded corner. I would take the fill.

Recommendation

Fill the corners. It removes the fault completely, costs one CSS rule, and the price is a corner detail nobody can see without magnifying it ten times.

Found on the way — not fixed

The rail’s navy header has the mirror of this: its square corners hang outside the rail’s rounded card. It is a different fault on a different element, it has always been there, and you did not ask about it. Say the word and I will fold it in; otherwise I leave it alone.
