Giving somebody a role, without a second dropdown

People register. Three things you found, one of them the reason nothing happened. Every picture is the real platform — same build, the proposal prototyped on the live page.

Your question

“It’s already in a cell — what am I missing?”

Nothing. You are right, and it is worse than redundant.

The Unit cell’s own dropdown already offers every place a role can be held — the group, every business unit, every supporting function, every company. It is the same list the second dropdown was drawing from, item for item.

And the grant already writes it back: giving somebody Business unit owner of Mobile sets their Unit to Mobile. Giving them Strategy custodian of Care sets it to Care. So the second dropdown asked a question the first had already answered, and then forced its own answer onto it. The two could never disagree, because the grant made them agree.

Which is why the fix is not to rename it. Choose where… should not be renamed to Unit — it should not be there. The Unit cell says where. The Roles cell says what. Two fields, no third question.

Why nothing happened

The second dropdown was drawn underneath the Email box

Before it goes, this is what it was doing. The Unit cell is one line and 158px wide (§88), so its two controls were laid out side by side rather than stacked: the second started 150px into a 158px cell, ran 133px past its own edge, and landed under the Email field, which took every click.

It only bit the roles with a real choice — unit owner, strategy custodian, company CEO, function head. Super user, SMO team and Group CEO are granted the moment the role is picked (§92), so they never drew a second half and always worked. That is exactly the difference between this and the SMO problem you reported before.

The proposal — prototyped, not drawn

Pick the role. It lands where the Unit says.

And this is what you meant by not blocking each other. Unit and Roles become two ordinary fields on the row. Set them in either order, change either one without the other emptying. Neither waits on the other — they simply both have to say something before somebody holds a role somewhere, and when they cannot agree the row explains it instead of doing nothing.

The second thing you found

Opening a row throws it to the top of the register

Reproduced and measured, on row 20 of 33 with the register scrolled to the top:

Opening the pen on row 20TodayFixed
The register’s own scroll0 → 533px0 → 0
Where the row sits on screen638px → 105px — the first line’s position638px → 638px
Cursor lands in the name fieldyesyes

The cause is the cursor, not the repaint: putting focus in the row’s first field lets the browser scroll that field into view, and the register is its own scrolling box, so it hauls the row up to the top. Focusing without letting the browser scroll holds the row exactly where you clicked it, and the cursor still lands in the right place.

It is on all seven tables with a pen, not just the register — the same two lines are written twice. And the check that exists to catch this (no-jump.py) measures repaints after a row is open; it never measured the act of opening one, which is why it has been green throughout.

What this costs, stated

What changes, and what does not

  • The register does not get wider and no row gets taller in the ordinary case — 1435px either way, 43px row. Only a refusal adds two lines, and only while it is on screen.
  • §69’s split survives in the part that mattered — the unit selection stays in the Unit column and the role selection stays in Roles. What goes is the duplicate, not the arrangement.
  • Giving one person two roles in two places still works, exactly as it does today: set the Unit, pick the role, set the Unit again, pick the second. They end up sitting at the last one — which is already what the platform does.
