Two problems, one cause. The legend is a reference strip — it explains what the colours below it mean — and the one thing a person came to this page to do during a cycle is parked inside it, in the same quiet outline as everything else. On a narrow window the legend wraps and the buttons go with it, so where Report sits depends on how wide the browser is.

Islam, after looking at it: “I think we can leave the 2 buttons in the same line with the reading colours and we can even shrink the reading colours a bit in font size so the buttons are more obvious.”

He is right, and the reason is worth keeping. The problem was never WHERE the buttons were — it was that they read as quietly as the legend beside them: two 12px uppercase controls against a 12.5px reference strip, all the same weight of grey. Moving them spent a whole row of vertical space to solve a contrast problem.

Built

v3.25

Same line as before. The legend drops to 10.5px so the buttons are the loudest thing in their own row.

One row, three volumes. Report shouts, Presentation speaks, the legend explains — 12px, 12px outlined, 10.5px quiet grey, with the label at 9.5px. The colour dots come down to 10px with the text; a 13px circle beside 10.5px type stops being a swatch and becomes a bullet.

10.5px is the floor. It is the size every uppercase key in the platform already wears, and the label at 9.5px is the smallest type in the design language — so the legend is now as quiet as anything the product says, rather than quieter than everything.

The check asserts the order of loudness rather than a position — the legend must measure smaller than the buttons beside it. That is what was actually wrong, and it is what a later stylesheet edit could silently undo.

The orange

Two tokens, one line per palette. No new colour is invented.

In light themes the fill is the palette’s deep orange — the value the palette already uses for orange WORDS — carrying white. In dark themes it is the palette’s bright orange carrying the page’s own near-black. That is §38.4 read the other way round: the bright orange that works as a fill on a card cannot carry white type (2.5 : 1), and a solid button is type on a fill. The button is themed with the tenant, so a client’s palette gets its own orange rather than this one.

It is one button, once, and only during an open cycle. Report is drawn only while a cycle is open and only for somebody who may report — so the accent budget (§41) is spent on the single act the page exists to prompt, and the page is back to its quiet register the rest of the quarter.

What was built

1 · Report and Presentation stay on the legend’s line, where they were.

2 · Report is a solid orange fill; Presentation stays outlined, so the two do not compete.

3 · The legend drops from 12.5px to 10.5px, its label to 9.5px, and its colour dots to 10px — so the buttons are the loudest thing in the row.

4 · The orange is the palette’s own, per the table above: it changes with the tenant’s branding rather than being fixed at this hex.
