Direction score cards — four directions

The two performance cards on a Direction page, redrawn four ways. Each one makes the main score the loudest thing on the card and turns the supporting figures down. Switch the direction to see a strong one and a weak one.

A

Hero number

  • Same layout as today, so the smallest change. Main score goes from 31px to 64px; the three small figures drop to 13px and lose their colour except the one that matters.
  • "4 scored" goes. When some measures haven't reported, one plain note says so instead: "2 not reported yet". It disappears when everything's in.
B

Number with a gauge

  • The score sits on a bar against the 100% target line. On key measures, a bracket shows the lowest-to-highest spread, so Highest and Lowest become a picture rather than two more numbers.
  • On execution, the bar is what was delivered and the line is the plan, so the gap is the variance.
C

Split tile

  • The score gets the left half of the card to itself, at 72px. The details stack on the right as label and value rows, read like a short list.
  • Cards get shorter, so the Key Measures table moves up the page.
D

One band, two scores

  • The two cards merge into one strip split by a thin line. Less box-on-box, and the two scores sit side by side for comparison.
  • The details become one sentence under each title, e.g. "6 measures · range 83–107%".
My pick: A, or B if you want something more visual.

A is a small change to today's card and fixes exactly what you raised. B tells the same story at a glance with less reading, but it's a bigger change and the gauge has to be built for the slides too. C and D save the most height.
