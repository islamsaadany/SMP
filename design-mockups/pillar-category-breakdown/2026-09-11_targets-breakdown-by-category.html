Why this shape

  • Each cell is the actual, then the target behind it — 4% / 7% — which is exactly how every reported figure in the platform already reads, two tables up. Their sheet spends two columns on each; this spends one, so three metrics fit where three would not.
  • None of these numbers is coloured. That is not a styling choice — in this platform a coloured figure means a score, and an uncoloured one with its target behind it means a reported fact. Drawn this way, the breakdown says “this explains the targets above” without anybody having to be told.
  • It sits under Key measures and above Tactics, because it explains the targets, not the work.
  • The client names it. “Categories”, “Growth”, “CM”, “Mix” are all typed in — so another client breaks down by region, or by channel, with nothing rebuilt.

The target sits behind each box so nobody has to remember it. These are asked for like any other figure — the tally counts them and Submit waits for all nineteen. What they do not do is score, and that has one consequence worth knowing: the platform demands an explanation when a figure is at risk, and a figure with no score can never be at risk, so a note here is always offered and never required.

What it costs, before you say yes

  • More to enter each cycle, and Submit waits for it. This pillar goes from four figures to nineteen, and a cycle cannot be submitted with any of them blank. Say so and they can be optional instead — but then a breakdown quietly half-filled is a breakdown nobody can trust.
  • It does not change the pillar’s score, and I think that is right: the breakdown explains the targets above it. If it scored as well, one pillar would have two different numbers and people would argue about which one counts. Say the word and it scores — but that is a bigger piece of work and a decision about how performance is read, not a column.
  • One control does not exist anywhere in the platform yet — naming a table’s columns. It is what stops this being a feature about categories.
  • It goes on the review slides too, as its own slide after that pillar’s targets.

What I need from you: A or B — and whether “it does not score” is right.
