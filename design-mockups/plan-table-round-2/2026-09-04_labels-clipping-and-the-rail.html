The boxes cutting the text is a bug, and it is mine

No decision needed — it ships with whatever we agree

This one has a single, embarrassing cause. A growing box is sized by measuring the text inside it, and that measurement happens at the end of a repaint. Yesterday's fold repaints only when the window crosses 1400px — so between crossings you can narrow the window, the boxes get narrower, the text needs another line, and nothing re-measures them. The box keeps the height it was given at the old width and clips.

It only ever happens on a resize, which is exactly your case, because a zoom is a resize. A page opened at any width is fine, which is why neither the checks nor I saw it:

 Mobile › MB01, pen open, the three tactics from your screenshot. “Cut” means the text needs more height than the box was given.

The fix is to re-measure the growing boxes when the window changes size. It repaints nothing, stores nothing, and cannot disturb what somebody is typing — it only sets a height. It also removes the reason the outcome box was cutting a word in half, which is the second half of your note.

A · name all three recommended

Says it outright, and keeps saying it when the tail folds underneath and the cell becomes a stack of five things. It is also the only one that names the outcome, which you asked for in your second note.

Costs: the table grows 935 → 1112px at 1150px of window (+19%). The words TACTIC and OUTCOME then appear twice on the same row — once as a column heading and once above the box.

B · name only the description

The column headings already say TACTIC and OUTCOME, so this adds a name exactly where there is none and nowhere else — nothing on the row is said twice.

Costs: 935 → 1020px (+9%), half of A. But the first box is named only by a heading five inches away, and on a folded row that heading is at the top of a table you may have scrolled past.

I lean to A despite the repetition: the point of the change is that somebody looking at one row can tell what they are looking at without reading the header, and B only half-delivers that. But the height is real, which is why it is worth reading it against the next section — the rail buys back more than the labels spend.

What it costs, stated before you choose

The strip scrolls sideways when the pillars do not fit: at 1200px with four pillars the fourth is half off the end, and you nudge the strip to reach it. The vertical rail shows all of them at once. The strip also drops the rail's own header and footer, and the table starts about 112px further down the page — paid once, against every row getting shorter.

Across at 1200px and below recommended

1200 is where the vertical rail stops paying for itself: above it the names still fit its 196px in two lines, below it they wrap to four and the column is taller than the pillar list is long. It also lands one step below yesterday's fold, so the two changes do not arrive together.

Across at 1400px, and fold the tail later

A different shape: give the table the width first, and keep Collabs and Quarters as real columns down to about 1100 rather than folding them at 1400. Seven columns are easier to run your eye down than a strip under a name.

Costs: the tactic column reads 387px at 1400 and 310px at 1300 this way, against 431 and 367 with the fold — so the columns survive longer and the prose is narrower while they do. It also changes what you signed off yesterday.
