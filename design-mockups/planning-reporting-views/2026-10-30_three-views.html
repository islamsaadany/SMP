Three ways the page could read

The page does two jobs at once: it sets the plan period and the cycle, and it watches who has reported. Today the two are stacked in one long column, with two separate Edits, and pressing either one pushes everything below it down the page. Each view below keeps every fact and control the page has now; what changes is where they sit.

Today

One long column

Plan period card, then the cycle strip with the headline figure under it, then the entry switch, then the whole board, then closed cycles at the very bottom. What gets in the way:

Two Edits for one ideaThe plan period and the cycle each have their own pen, though the strip's "6 of 12 months of the plan" is one fact that needs both.
Editing moves the pageOpening the cycle's pen drops the board and the headline figure 150px down the page; the review day and the red Close button share that pen.
The board is not a settingWho has reported is the office's daily watch, but it lives under the settings and past the switch, where nobody opens Setup to find it.
Closed cycles are out of sightH2 2025 and its 64% are a two-column table at the bottom; the timeline above never shows them.
A · recommended

Set up on one tab, watch on the other

The smallest move from what is signed off. The plan period still comes first and the cycle sits under it, but the settings and the board get a tab each, there is one Edit, and it opens a panel beside the facts instead of pushing the page down.

  • Two tabs under the title: The set-up and Who has reported, the second carrying the 234 of 255 so the count is readable from either tab.
  • One card, The year and the cycle: the timeline across the top with the due date and review day pinned on it, the facts in three short columns under it.
  • One Edit opens a panel on the right with the plan period, the cycle and the review day in that order. Close the cycle is at the panel's foot, in its own red strip, away from the boxes you type in.
  • Closed cycles become a row of chips under the card ("H2 2025 · 64%"), where the timeline can show them too.
  • The board gets a filter row: All · Not started · Needs a note · Submitted, and a search box.

B

The year as a calendar

For an office that thinks in dates. The whole page hangs off one year strip: the plan period is a lane, every cycle is a lane under it, closed ones included, and the one you press opens on the right. The board is grouped by state, so what is still owed is read first.

  • Two lanes on the strip: Plan period and Cycles. H2 2025 sits in the past in grey with its 64%, H1 2026 is outlined, and a dashed next cycle ghost from July is where "Open a new cycle" lives.
  • Press a cycle and its card opens beside the strip: dates, state, progress, Edit and Close. Nothing under the strip moves.
  • The plan period's Edit stays on its own lane, as now.
  • The board becomes three groups, Not started first, then In progress, then Submitted, each a compact grid of lines rather than eight columns. The full table is one press away ("Show every column").

The cycle as five steps

For an office that is new to the platform, or a client whose SMO team changes. The page reads as the order the work happens in; the step you are on is lit, and what it needs is drawn under it. Everything else is one line with its own Edit.

  • Five steps across the top: Plan period, Open the cycle, Collect reports, Review day, Close. Done ones carry a tick and their fact; the current one is outlined in gold.
  • Under the steps, the current step opens: for Collect reports that is who still owes something first (3 lines), with the 15 complete lines folded under one row.
  • Pressing another step opens it in the same place: Plan period shows the pickers and the timeline, Review day its three boxes, Close its one red button and the sentence that explains it.
  • Closed cycles sit under step 5 as the record of every Close pressed before.

Which to pick

A if the aim is a calmer page with the least change: it keeps the order Islam signed off and fixes the three things that get in the way today. It also carries B's timeline, so nothing is lost by starting here.

B if the office mostly wants to see the year: where the cycle sits, what closed before, when the next one starts. It is the biggest redraw and the board loses its eight columns on the first screen.

C if the people running the cycle are new to it and need to be led through. It is the most opinionated, and an office that knows the platform may find the steps in the way.
