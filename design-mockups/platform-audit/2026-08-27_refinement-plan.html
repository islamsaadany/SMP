P0Broken now — fix before any polish

Confirmed live on this build. No design debate needed, only the shape of each fix.

01Navigation breaks below ~1100px

Seen At 1024px the unit row wraps, its second line paints under the tab row, and the two rows of text overlap. Worse than the look: the hidden second line eats the clicks — my sweep’s tap on a function’s Strategy tab was intercepted and the walk hung. Recorded as §118.7; confirmed on this build, on multiple pages.

Hurts 1024–1100px is any laptop at 125% zoom, any split screen, most projector mirrors. In that window the product looks corrupted and half the navigation is dead — to the person being shown a demo, it is simply broken.

Fix Let the chrome own its real height: the destination row may wrap, and the chrome publishes its measured height the way §130.10 already publishes the Setup header’s (ResizeObserver → --chrome-h), so the tab row sits below the second line instead of on it. Alternative to weigh at mockup: the row scrolls sideways with edge fades instead of wrapping.

Cost: every sticky offset reads the chrome height, so this re-runs the width, no-jump and rail checks; it is the one item here that touches layout arithmetic everywhere. Which shape wins is a mockup decision.

02The last edit can be lost silently

Seen Autosave is debounced 800ms with no flush when the page goes away (§126.1, recorded not fixed). Flip a switch, close the tab a moment later: the screen showed the new value, the database never got it. Every page shares this path — branding, terminology, the access matrix.

Hurts It is the worst kind of fault: invisible, rare, and it spends the user’s trust when it fires — “I set that last week” with no way to prove it.

Fix Flush the pending save on visibilitychange/pagehide, in sync.js — one place, because there is one autosave.

Cost: touches the save path of the whole product, so it gets its own session: the round-trip test against a real Postgres, plus a check that leaves the page 150ms after an edit and asks the server what it holds.

03A failed render says nothing

Seen A throw mid-paint() keeps the previous page on screen with the only witness in the hidden console (§118.7) — a data fault reads as a dead click. This is how the CF-tab incident looked from production.

Hurts The person reports the wrong symptom (“the tab does nothing”), and diagnosis starts a page away from the fault every time.

Fix One guard at paint’s single chokepoint: on a throw, draw a plain failure card — what page failed and one line of the error — in the panel, in the product’s own words. §32’s rule one level deeper.

Cost: small and contained; the design of the failure card is a five-minute mockup.

P1High-friction UX

Nothing is broken — but these are where real minutes go, in the two busiest weeks of a quarter most of all.

04Reporting: the controls scroll away from the work

Seen Mobile’s report is 41 figures long. The tally, the progress bar, Submit and Save draft sit in a card at the top and scroll off with the first pillar; the “10 figures need a note” alarm names a number but offers no way to reach the rows that owe one.

Hurts The reporter works blind: how many are left, and whether they can submit yet, is always a scroll away — on the busiest screen in the product.

Fix Pin the tally bar under the chrome while reporting (the pinned-pair machinery from §53.7/§130.10 already exists), and make the alarm a control: “10 figures need a note — go to the next”.

Cost: a new pinned element is exactly the class of arithmetic §122.5 warns about, so it ships with its own measured check. The jump control is cheap.

05Hover, focus and disabled colours were never measured

Seen The contrast sweep walks pages, not states (§16.17): every :hover, :focus and :disabled colour in the product is unmeasured. One is already caught by accident — .editbtn:hover at 4.34:1 — and §16.15 holds 31 recorded light-mode failures on a unit’s Performance page, deliberately parked as a palette decision.

Hurts The palette’s promise — “the colour and the word can never disagree” — is only proven at rest. The moment a hand is on a control is exactly when its label matters.

Fix One palette pass that settles §16.15’s 31 and the state colours together, then teach the sweep to measure hovered/focused/disabled states so it stays settled.

Cost: a colour decision, so it comes to you as a before/after strip first. The sweep extension is the durable half.

06Columns that say nothing on every row

Seen On the plan and performance tables, DIR. shows a bare ≤ / ≥ glyph, and COMPILED reads “Latest” on nearly every row of every table — a column with one value is furniture wearing a heading.

Hurts A CEO reading a scorecard should not need the knowledge base to decode ≥; and every dead cell dilutes the columns that do work.

Fix To align, two candidates: (a) glyphs gain their words on hover and the compile column shows only exceptions (anything not “Latest”), the default folded into the measure’s hover; (b) keep both columns but render the default value in the quiet ink so exceptions alone carry weight. Workbooks untouched either way — this is display only.

Cost: a table-shape change across three panes, so it is a mockup decision; small build.

07“Viewing as” cuts off who you are viewing as

Seen The switcher reads “Mohamed Essam — …” at every width — the truncated half is the role and place, which is the half that says what you are looking at. The roles do appear beside it for the current pick, but the dropdown’s own options clip.

Fix Size the control to its content (it lives on a row with room), or move the role detail into each option’s hint the way the owner picker’s data-hint already does (§130.9’s pattern, reused not reinvented).

Cost: small; one control, asserted in the existing header check.

08No stated minimum window

Seen Below ~1100px item 01 bites; below 900px the rail stops being a column (§130.9); nothing asserts any width between 600 and 1000. CEOs open links on iPads — 1024 landscape is a real audience, and it is today’s broken width.

Fix Decide the floor together (recommendation: 1024 first-class, 768 readable-not-editable), then sweep the checks at those widths so the floor cannot rot. This item is the policy; item 01 is its first repair.

Cost: mostly check-writing; any layout repairs it finds are their own aligned items.

P2Design-language refinements

Each is mockup-first, one at a time. None changes what any page does.

09The landing page stops at a third of the screen

Seen The group’s Overall performance — the first screen of every session — is three cards and then 330px of empty page on a 900px window. “Who needs my attention” is one click further, on the Business units section.

Hurts The first screen answers “how are we doing” but not “where do I look next” — the one question a landing page owes an executive.

Fix A quiet heat strip under the three cards: each unit’s name and score chip in its scoring colour, one row, clicking through to the unit. The data is already computed for the section next door; nothing new is stored.

Cost: a real addition to the most senior screen in the product — mockup first, and the “Business units” section stays as the detail view so nothing is duplicated at full volume.

10Chip pile-ups in card headers

Seen A summary card header can carry the title, an (i) mark, a PRIMARY chip, a ▲3 delta and a status chip — five voices on one line (group landing, company page, function overview). The delta ▲3 sits away from the number it qualifies and never says against what.

Fix One loudness order, applied everywhere (§94.8’s own logic): the status chip keeps its colour and its corner; PRIMARY drops to a quiet outline micro-chip; the delta moves beside the big number it belongs to, with its meaning (“vs last cycle”) on the hover.

Cost: one CSS/markup pass over the card family; mockup of a single card settles it for all of them.

11Italic all-caps captions fight the reader

Seen Section captions like “KEY MEASURES — AS PLANNED: THIS YEAR’S TARGET, AND HOW IT COMPILES” set a full sentence in italic capitals with letter-spacing — three emphases at once, on the hardest form to read at length.

Fix The noun keeps its caps; the explainer drops to sentence-case quiet ink. Same words, one emphasis each.

Cost: typography only; one rule, swept everywhere the caption pattern appears.

12Branding’s “not set” swatch shows a foreign colour

Seen The colour pickers on Setup › Branding default to #4F46E5 — an indigo that exists nowhere in the product — so an unbranded tenant’s Branding page shows two indigo swatches while the platform actually paints navy. The one page that defines colour opens with a small lie about it.

Fix Default each picker to the token the platform is actually painting (read it off the computed style, never a literal — §25).

Cost: one line each; no data change.

13The typeface decision is still open

Seen Four faces ride in every build behind the top-bar switch, undecided since §38.7 — “B is how you decide, A is how you ship”, and the deciding never happened. The product ships on the system stack by default.

Hurts Type is the largest single lever on how considered the whole product feels, and ~110KB of undecided fonts travel in every handover.

Fix A side-by-side of the four candidates on two real screens (a scorecard and the register), you pick one, the other three leave the file.

Cost: the comparison is cheap; the pick then touches metrics everywhere, so it lands alone with the width checks re-run.

14Name and Full Name read as one column, twice

Seen On the register, Name and Full Name are identical for every row until somebody types a short name — today that is all 33 rows, so the frozen column appears duplicated beside itself. §93.8’s two-column decision is right; the default view is what needs a look.

Fix To align: Full Name leaves the default column set (it stays one tick away in Columns, which already remembers per person), or it stays and we accept the doubling until short names are in use. My recommendation is the first.

Cost: a default-columns change only — stored preferences are merged with defaults (§30.2), so nobody who chose columns is touched.

P3Recorded debts — scheduled, behind decisions

Already in the decisions log as flagged-not-fixed. Listed so this plan is the whole picture; each needs its decision before its build.

15Project tables: drag grips bound to nothing §99.6

The grips render and do nothing, pending one decision: what a drop between the two halves (deliverables ↔ outcomes) means. Decide “refused with the reason shown”, and the build is small.

16A pillars function’s key objectives cannot be authored §129

Readable on the Overview, writable nowhere. Needs a home — the natural one is the same builder flow §129 shipped.

17The deck still shows a Due date column the panes lost §104.8

A column on the projector that is absent in the product. Decide: align the deck, or keep it deliberately and record why.

18The tour’s replay button is unreachable §119.4

The knowledge base became the office’s and the replay lives there, while the stories fit people who can no longer open the page. Either the replay moves, or the office gets a story.

19Keyboard and screen-reader pass

Nothing in the suite tabs through the product: the searchable dropdowns, the dialogs, the eye/pen matrix and the icon-only controls have never been asserted from the keyboard. One session: walk it, fix the cheap gaps (labels, focus order), record the structural ones as their own items.

20Prose measure at ≥2560px §94.13

Held on purpose: a Foundation line reaches 1166px on a 2560px monitor. Stays parked until that monitor exists in the audience.

How this lands

  • Wave 1 — items 01–03. Defects first; no visual decisions in the way, only the shape of the navigation fix (one mockup).
  • Wave 2 — items 04–08. The friction set; 04 and 06 come with mockups, 05 with a before/after colour strip.
  • Wave 3 — items 09–14. One at a time, each as a static HTML mockup published as an artifact for sign-off before any source is touched — per the house rules.
  • P3 items are scheduled as their decisions are made; none blocks the waves above.
  • Every change follows the standing discipline: snapshot to ui-versions/, checks proved able to fail, decisions log updated in the same commit.
