Platform Design Variations

Two concepts read against what SMP already holds, then two variations drawn in the platform's own tokens on Mobile's real H1 2026 figures. Today's page is beside them. Both share one question: does the navigation stay in the top bar or move to a rail?

Concept 1 — light, tabular, with a detail drawer

What it is doing well, and what SMP can honestly take from it

  • A grouped left rail — where you go · what is yours to do · setup. SMP has a rail already, on Setup only.
  • One flat table of every measure with a status dot per row. Today a unit's page draws one pillar at a time; a reader wanting every measure below band walks four panes.
  • A detail drawer of four tiles (target · actual · progress · variance) with history beside it. History exists since §262.
  • The grey line under Plan — rule 1b-ii, not built.
  • Unit / FY / quarter filters — a unit is a destination, the cycle is one for the tenant, and there is no quarter. The cycle chip takes that slot.
  • A quarterly trend line — the platform holds a score per closed cycle, so a trend is per cycle and only once cycles have closed.
  • Global search with ⌘K — a feature, not a look. Drawn as the table's own filter only.

Concept 2 — dark rail, dashboard landing

What it is doing well, and what SMP can honestly take from it

  • A hero band that says whose strategy this is — name, planning period, aspiration. The aspiration is stored today and shown only on Foundation.
  • Four cards, and the fourth is what is outstanding — SMP already counts missing plan items, figures owed and the submission state.
  • Pillar cards with a bar and a band pill, the code as the mark.
  • Recent activity — this is History (§262), read as a list.
  • Upcoming dates — the cycle's own: covers to, reports due.
  • Photographs on pillar cards — invented content, and nowhere to upload them. A pillar's code and band carry the card instead.
  • "Key insights" as written prose — kept only where the sentence is a fact the platform computes (a pillar off track, focus measures short).
  • The closing tagline banner — decoration.
  • Their blue — every surface here wears --panel, which is what Setup › Branding sets.

What is Concept 1's, what is Concept 2's

  • The rail's grouping (where · yours to do · setup) is Concept 1; its navy is Concept 2 and it is --panel, so a tenant's branding colours it.
  • The flat table with a band dot per row, the pillar filter and Below band only are Concept 1. The dot is the score's band, never a fourth colour.
  • The drawer's four tiles are Concept 1's; the fourth is YTD target, which is what the platform actually judges a figure against (§239).
  • The three headline cards, the tabs and the orange Reporting tab are today's, unchanged.

What it costs

  • 232px of every page goes to the rail. A unit's Plan pane is 1395px wide today at 1670px; here it is about 1160px. §267's tail fold would start earlier.
  • Every check that presses the top row moves — the destination row, Units | Functions, the home mark. This is the largest structural change the product has had since Setup became a page (§46).
  • The flat table is a new view: today's one-pillar-at-a-time pane stays for Plan and Reporting; this table replaces only the Performance pane's rail.
  • Twelve units and eight functions do not fit a rail without folding; the drawing folds companies and the last three units.

What is Concept 2's, what is Concept 1's

  • The hero band is Concept 2's; it holds the unit's stored aspiration, end in mind and this cycle's note — all real, none of it new to store. No photograph.
  • Four cards are Concept 2's shape; three are today's headline numbers and the fourth is Outstanding, built from counts the page already computes (missing items, notes owed, focus short, submitted).
  • Pillar cards with a bar and a band pill are Concept 2's; the code stands where its photograph was.
  • What the numbers say and Recently changed are Concept 2's two columns; the first only says what the platform computes, the second is History as a list.
  • The cycle strip carries Concept 2's upcoming dates with the platform's own three dates.
  • The chrome is today's, untouched — the only Concept 1 borrowing is the drawer, which B does not need.

What it costs

  • A new page above the pillar rail: the Performance tab would open on this overview and the rail-and-pane below it (today's screen) would sit under it, or become a second section. Which is a decision.
  • The hero is tall (about 190px) and says the aspiration on every visit; a unit with no aspiration shows the band with the name alone.
  • "What the numbers say" needs a rule per sentence — four kinds are drawn; each is a small reader over figures already on the page, never prose the office writes.
  • Recently changed reads History for this unit only; History exists for the office today and would need the unit's own read opened (§262 already allows one place per role).
  • Nothing moves for checks that press the chrome. Both sides of the switch (a unit and a function) get the same page.

What the two variations have in common

  • Every colour is a platform token; the rail and the hero are --panel, so Setup › Branding recolours them.
  • The accent budget holds: one gold fill per page (the lit rail row in A, the pillar cards' top rule in B); orange stays the reporting tab's alone.
  • No description under a heading (1b-ii). Where Concept 1 wrote one, the space is empty or a fact.
  • Nothing new is stored. Both draw from figures, dates and history the tenant already holds.

The one question to answer first

Navigation in the top bar, or in a rail? A decides yes and pays for it in width and in every check; B keeps the chrome and spends the change on the landing page. The two can be combined (the rail from A, the landing from B) but that is the largest change of the three and should be its own round.

Recommendation — B first. It changes one page, stores nothing, and answers the question the concepts were really asking, which is what does a unit see when it arrives.
