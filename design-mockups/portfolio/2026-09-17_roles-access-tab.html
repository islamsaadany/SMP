Portfolio — the Roles & access tab

Spec 046 gives each module a tab of its own on Roles & access. This is Portfolio's. The proposal is that it needs three areas and two rules — not the six roles ScopePlan carries — because SMP already works out “sees the whole plan, works only their own rows” from being named on a row rather than from a grant.

True whatever the table says

  • Only a lead completes an activity. Whoever is doing the work marks it Done. Marking it Completed — and setting the real end date — is the project's lead or the office. Everything sitting at Done queues for them.
  • An assignee reports; a collaborator comments. Read off the activity, never granted: being named as the assignee is what lets somebody enter a figure, being tagged as a collaborator is what lets them write on it.
  • Restructuring is never partial. Planning Mode moves rows belonging to other people, so Plan · Edit is the whole project or nothing — a role bounded to its own rows cannot hold it, and the table will not offer it.
  • Forefront's own team is not on this table. Consultants reach every client by their seat, the way the Internal Tracker and Meeting Notes already work. This page is the client deciding about the client's own people.

ScopePlan's six roles, landed

Three of them are not grants at all here — SMP derives them from the plan naming somebody, which is how a Project owner already works in Strategy.

Senior ContributorPlan View + Progress Edit. Sees the whole tree, works only the rows that name them.

CollaboratorDerived. Tagged on an activity rather than assigned it — comments, does not report.
ViewerAny role with View and no Edit. Not a role of its own.

What this costs, and what it opens

  • A module has nowhere to put a role. Spec 046 promises a module brings “its own roles and areas”, and only areas were built. Portfolio lead is the first role a module has ever needed — marked on the table above. It is either added to the client's shared role list, which puts a Portfolio word into the frozen Strategy product, or modules get somewhere of their own to declare one.
  • Edit starts with the office alone, every row below it reading — the shipped answer Strategy already uses. A client's Super user opens it deliberately, and an unanswered cell means not decided yet rather than no.
  • “Own projects” means a project that names you — as its lead, or on any activity inside it. Everything else is Other projects.
  • Three areas, not four. Sign-off is deliberately not a column: it is a governance rule, and a tick somebody could set on a bad afternoon is the wrong instrument for it.

Press the cells. They behave as the real ones do — two toggles, and nothing lit is itself the answer, which is why there is no third button for “none”. Not drawn yet: the project list, the plan tree, the sign-off queue. This tab is first because it decides what those three screens are allowed to show.
