Two views, one list

  • The tree and the timeline are the same rows. Press Timeline. Nothing is computed twice and nothing is drawn twice — which is why they cannot disagree, and why a bar can never say something the row beside it does not.
  • Every figure above an activity is derived. A phase is its children averaged; an activity is its sub-activities by weight. Nothing stores a phase percentage — theirs had two columns for it and never once wrote to either. The first draft of this page typed two of these and both were wrong, which is the argument in one sentence.
  • The plan is one list, not a rail and a pane. A delivery plan is read top to bottom, so phases fold rather than hiding behind a picker.
  • Six columns, and everything else opens. Their version puts twelve facts on a row, and five columns of controls is what breaks a table on an ordinary laptop.

What the timeline says that the list cannot

  • Today is a line, and it is the whole point. A bar chart of dates without it says nothing about being behind. Phase 3 has barely started and its bar runs to August — you read that in one glance and nowhere in the list.
  • An overrun is drawn in red past the planned bar. 2.2 was planned to 20 February and ran to the 24th, and you can see it crossing into 2.3's own window — which is why 2.3 is blocked. The list says Blocked; the timeline shows what by.
  • Dependencies are drawn, not listed. One per activity, so the connectors stay readable rather than becoming spaghetti — the dotted elbow runs from what must finish to what waits.
  • A milestone is a diamond, not a bar, because it is a date rather than a stretch of work. Hollow until it is reached.
  • A phase draws a summary bar — thinner, quieter, spanning its children — so the shape of the project reads before any single activity does.

Three things a plan is actually for

  • Done is not Completed. Whoever does the work marks it done; it counts as finished when a Lead signs it off and sets the real end date. 2.2 is in that state — the row says so in the platform's own navy, and the panel holds the only control that closes it. Reopening is gated the same way, or a Contributor could undo a Lead's sign-off.
  • Late is red, on the date itself, with the count at the top of the page rather than somewhere you have to go looking for it.
  • Blocked is a fact about the row, not a status. 2.3 cannot start because 2.2 has not finished. Its status is still Not started — that is what it IS — and the mark says why.
  • Billable is a flag and nothing else (2026-09-18, Islam). It sits on the activity, it is drawn on the panel, and nothing counts it, totals it or invoices from it. The invoice-trigger flag their system carries is not ported: the level it reported into is the one decision 2 deleted.
