Why the plan is one list

  • The sequence is the content. A rail down the side would hide phases 2 and 3 while you read phase 1, and a delivery plan is read top to bottom — so phases fold rather than being hidden behind a picker, and the whole plan is one scan.
  • Six columns, and everything else opens. The reference puts twelve facts on a row; five columns of controls is what breaks a table on an ordinary laptop, so the row carries what you scan — number, name, owner, window, status, how far — and an activity opens into the panel below for what it produces, its real dates, what it depends on and what it blocks.
  • Every figure above an activity is derived. Phase 2 reads 67% because its three activities average to it; 3.1 reads 20% and phase 3 reads 10% the same way. Nobody types a phase percentage and nothing stores one — theirs had two columns for this and never once wrote to either.
  • 86% is the sub-activities by their weights. Also derived, and the panel says so where the number is, so nobody wonders whether somebody typed it.

The three things a plan is actually for

  • Done is not Completed. Whoever does the work marks it done; it counts as finished when a Lead signs it off and sets the real end date. 2.2 is in that state — the row says so in the platform's own navy, and the panel carries the only control that closes it. Reopening is gated the same way, or a Contributor could undo a Lead's sign-off.
  • Late is on the row, in red, on the date itself. Two activities are past their planned end, and the count is at the top of the page rather than somewhere you have to go looking.
  • Blocked is a fact about the row, not a status. 2.3 cannot start because 2.2 has not finished. Its status is still Not started — that is what it IS — and the mark says why.

Three things I could not settle from what is decided

  • The Timeline view is a button here and nothing behind it. A Gantt is the other half of this screen and it is a drawing of its own — this one is about what the rows say.
  • An activity carries two commercial flags in the reference — is it billable, does it trigger an invoice — and the level they reported into is the one we dropped. Nothing here draws them. If they are worked against, they come back; if nobody uses them, they go.
  • The Charter tab is new. The module's navigation was written before the charter existed, so it listed Plan, Progress and Analytics. Four tabs, with the charter first, because it is where somebody who has never seen the project starts.
