Writing a plan

Five of Portfolio's six screens read. This is the one that writes: putting the tree together, changing an activity's facts, moving a date, and reporting against a breakdown. Drawn on the real Plan page — the stylesheet and every row below are the served page's own bytes, so what is proposed here is measured against what is already there.

The one thing that decides the shape

  • There is no Planning Mode, and that was settled on the audit (spec 060 §7.3). Their version keeps every added, changed and deleted row in the browser behind a banner and a Save All. This platform deleted exactly that shape once and wrote down why: a field writes when the cursor leaves it and the autosave carries it, so there is no Save and no Cancel anywhere in the product — a draft is what forces both, plus a guard on closing the tab.
  • So writing a plan here is a pen. Press it, the tree grows handles, you work, you press it again. Nothing is held back and nothing is thrown away. Building a two-hundred-row plan without a request per keystroke is what the workbook is for (§9.3), which is this product's own draft-and-commit.
  • The cost, stated: restructuring a plan is visible to anybody looking at it while it happens, where Planning Mode let you finish first.

What edit mode does and does not do

  • The row gains a strip of its own, on the right. Move up, move down, add beneath, remove. The fields are not edited in the row — they are edited on the panel, where all of them already are, so there is one answer to where is an activity changed rather than two. It also avoids a fault this product has already paid for (§267): with controls in the prose columns, five of seven columns stop shrinking and the name pays for the window.
  • Adding is a name and Enter, for all three kinds — a phase, a work package, an activity. That is the Internal Tracker's idiom, chosen there for the same reason (we are shifting from a simple google sheet, so it needs to be super simple). Get the skeleton down, then open a row and fill it in.
  • Status is not a field here. It is worked out: a real start date is stamped the first time progress leaves nought, a real end date at sign-off. The way to change a status is to report against the row or to accept it on Progress — never to pick it from a list.
  • Nor is the per-cent. A parent's figure is its children by weight; an activity's is its breakdown. Where there is a breakdown there is no box to type in, because the figure is not a second opinion.

Two audiences, one panel

  • A Lead or a seat writes every fact on this row, and everything above is a box.
  • The person the row is assigned to gets the breakdown and Mark it done and nothing else — they report their own work, they do not restructure the plan. That is this platform's own rule, in its own words: see the plan, write your own rows.
  • A Viewer sees the read panel, unchanged, and no control on it. Everybody put on a project lands at Viewer, so read-only is what the default quietly grants — which is the only default that fails safely.
  • Where there is no breakdown at all the per-cent is a box and the person reporting types it. Adding the first step is what takes the typing away.

3 · Moving a date — it shows you first

One of the six rules the plan is actually for: moving a date moves what depends on it, and shows you before anything commits. Press the end date above, or the button here. 2.2 blocks 2.3, and 2.3 blocks nothing further — a chain is walked to its end, and a chain that loops back on itself is refused rather than walked for ever.

Why it is a ticking list and not two buttons

  • Both answers are real. Sometimes the work downstream was going to slip anyway; sometimes it has a date of its own that nothing may touch. A pair of buttons — move them against leave them — cannot express a mix, and a plan with six things downstream usually is one.
  • So every row that would shift arrives ticked and you untick what stays. One control, any answer, and the platform's ticking lists already behave this way everywhere else.
  • Nothing is written until the press. Closing it changes nothing, including the date you were moving.

4 · Removing a row — and the refusal

A phase holds work. Removing one that still does is refused, and the refusal names what is in the way — the same answer already settled one screen over for taking somebody off a project (refusal is better). The press is live either way, because a control that is simply missing tells nobody why.

What removing costs, said rather than discovered

  • There is no undo and no archive. Strategy archives a plan before an upload replaces it, because an upload replaces the whole thing; here a row is removed one at a time and deliberately, so the guard is the refusal and the confirmation rather than a copy nobody would ever open. If that is the wrong call, this is the place to say so — an archive is a table and a restore screen.
  • Every number after it moves. Removing 2.2 makes the old 2.3 into the new 2.2. That is rule 5 working, and it is why the line above the tree says so.

5 · The one question with no answer in it yet

Where does who is on a project hang?

  • That screen is drawn and signed off — the ticking list out of the client's register, everybody landing as a Viewer, and taking somebody off who holds activities refused by name. What was never settled is where you reach it from, because the tab row was agreed as four: Charter, Plan, Progress, Analytics.
  • A fifth tab — Team, after Analytics. One press from anywhere in the project, and it makes the row five wide.
  • Or a line on the charter, which already names the four accountable people, with the team opening from there. The tab row stays four and the team is one press further away.
  • The recommendation is the fifth tab: naming people is something you do while the plan is in front of you, not while reading the charter — and the charter is agreed once where the team changes through the work.

And three smaller ones

  • Can a Lead name people? Today they cannot: naming is the seat's, which is the narrow answer and the one that can be widened without taking anything back. The cost is that a Lead correcting the charter cannot change who leads the project from that row.
  • Is a removed row worth keeping? The panel above says there is no archive. One sentence either way settles it.
  • Does the plan need an undo the last thing I did? Nothing else in the platform has one. With no Planning Mode, a mistyped name is retyped and a removed row is retyped — which is cheap for a name and not for a row with a breakdown on it.
