Priorities inside a process: steps that deserve to be a process of their own

Three ways to do it, drawn on the same example (Hire to Retire). Click the tabs. All names and numbers are made up.

Idea: open a process in the Score list and every step gets the same three dots (importance, complexity, risk). A step that scores high gets a "Make it a process" button.

  • Same scoring everywhere, one idea to learn.

Not so good

  • Most steps are plainly not candidates, so most of the effort is wasted.
Idea: steps are not scored. The system points at steps that look like hotspots, using what is already on the map. You flag the ones that matter with one click and say why. Flagged steps show under their process as "steps to watch", and can be made into a process.

What the system looks at (all already on the map)

A decision point, a hand-over to another process, the number of roles involved, a high or medium risk logged against the step, and the number of sub-tasks. Suggestions are for the office only, like the existing suggested scores.

Good

  • Light: you look at a few steps, not hundreds.
  • Catches a step that is already a process elsewhere, so you link rather than duplicate.
  • Flagged steps show on the process card in the plan.

Not so good

  • A step has no score, so it is not ranked against processes until it becomes one.

What "Make it a process" does, in all three options. It uses the "branch from a step" link that sub-processes already have, so nothing new in the map.

You choose before it happens

  • The name and code (suggested from the step).
  • Whether the step stays in the main process as a link to the new one (recommended), or is removed from it.
  • The new process starts as a Draft and is not part of any wave until you decide.

Nothing is deleted or moved without a confirmation, and the original step is never lost.

My recommendation: B first, then C if the lists get long

B keeps you in the process, needs almost no scoring, and turns a step into a proper process that is then scored with everything else. C is the same signals shown across the whole company, and it is cheap to add on top of B later. A is the heaviest, and most steps will never be candidates.
