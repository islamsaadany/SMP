Counted from the module as it stands on 23 October 2026, page by page. The parts are good. The paths between them are where the time goes, and most of that cost sits on a process's own page, where four kinds of navigation are stacked on one screen and change as you move.

What makes it hard, worst first

Each one names what was counted, what it costs the consultant, and where the redesign answers it.

Confuses

The navigation changes under you

On a process's page and its map you get the tabs. Open RACI, Authority or AI review and the tabs are gone; the only way back to Risks, Quality or Lifecycle is the strip's “Process” step, which lands on Overview first.

FixOne header and one row of tabs on every view of a process.
Slows you down

Read in one place, edited in another

Purpose and scope are shown on Overview and document control on Lifecycle, but all three are edited on the map page, above the drawing. The Lifecycle tab says so in words: “Edit these on the process map page.”

FixEdit where you read it, with a pen on each block, the way a Portfolio charter works.
Slows you down

Switching process means going back to the list

To go from Order-to-Cash's RACI to Procure-to-Pay's RACI: left menu, find the row, Open, then RACI. Four clicks, and you pass through Overview on the way.

FixA rail of processes beside the page, as Strategy lists a unit's pillars. Click another process and you stay on the tab you were on.
Confuses

Two measures of progress that disagree

The strip counts Process, Map, RACI, Authority, AI Review and marks one NEXT. The deep-dive plan counts Map, RACI, Authority, Controls, SOP, Approved. Authority never turns green on the strip, so a finished process says “Authority Matrix, NEXT” for ever.

FixOne readiness line, the six deep-dive marks, each a link to the tab where it is fixed.
Confuses

Three tabs that lead nowhere

People, Compliance and Connections each say “not recorded yet” and offer nothing to record it with. They look like work waiting to be done, and none of it can be.

FixTake them off the row. Who is involved and the hand-overs go on Overview; Compliance comes back when it holds something.
Slows you down

The main buttons are at the bottom

New process, Draft with AI and Import sit under the whole list: with 48 processes, that is 48 rows down. Add step sits under the map.

FixOne New process button at the top, with the three ways to start in its menu. Add step in the map's own toolbar.
Slows you down

The list is hard to act on

Nine columns squeezed into an 848-pixel column. Every row carries four text buttons (Edit, Clone, Delete, Open) and the name is not a link, so the most-used action is the small “Open →” at the far right. There is no owner column.

FixThe name opens the process. Edit, Clone and Delete go behind ⋯. Owner and readiness come in; the page takes SMP's full width.
Slows you down

The front page is a settings page

“Dashboard” holds the logo and colours, industry and description, and three counts: processes, RACI drafts and members. Nothing on it says what needs doing this week.

FixAn Overview that opens on what needs attention and how far the deep dives have got.
Slows you down

Governance is one long page

Dashboard, review calendar, profile form, assessment, activity log, then a block for every single process, stacked in one scroll.

FixFive views under one switch, the way a unit's Strategy splits Foundation, SWOT, Drivers and Plan.
Slows you down

No way up

A sub-process names its parent in grey text that is not a link, and no process page has a trail back to the list. The left menu is the only way out.

FixA trail on every page: Raya Trade · Processes › parent › this one, each part a link.
Another product

It does not look like SMP

Its own typeface (Geist, where SMP uses Source Sans), indigo and slate where SMP uses navy and gold, the client's colour as a gradient banner on some pages and a plain title on others, and a left menu where every other module has a bar along the top. Beside Strategy, Portfolio and the Tracker it reads as a different product.

FixSMP's palette, type, tables, cards and section bar throughout, as drawn in screens 2 to 5.
Polish

Names that need explaining

“Helicopter View” is consultant shorthand, and “Export Report” is a thing you do, sitting in the menu as if it were a place.

FixLandscape, holding the value chain and the map of connections. Export as a button in the header.
