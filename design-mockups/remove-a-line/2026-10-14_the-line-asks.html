Removing a line

The last family. Nine controls across six screens ask one sentence — “Remove X? This cannot be undone here.” — in the browser's box. It is one conversion, not nine, and it is the only one of the twenty-nine with no drawing behind it: the other shapes were either already in the product or drawn and signed off last week.

It needs its own because the control is different. Everything converted so far hangs off a row with a menu, a form with a button, or a page with room. These nine are a 24-pixel × beside a line you are in the middle of typing, in a list of them.

It does not say which line. You pressed a × in a list of nine, the box covers the list, and the only thing it tells you is that something will go. “This cannot be undone here” is also doing quiet work the reader cannot check — it means the plan is archived on the way out of other doors, not that this line is recoverable.

Recommended. The question stands exactly where the line is, so which line is not something to work out — it is the one that has turned into the question. Nothing moves on the page, the list keeps its length and its numbering while you decide, and the rest of the editor stays usable. It is the in-place shape the row menus already use, at the size this control has.

The cost, stated: the line's text is hidden while it asks, because the row is only so wide. For a long line the question truncates it — which is the one thing Treatment B fixes.

The line is still readable while you answer, and the mark on the field says which one. The cost is the opposite one: the list grows by a row as you press, so everything below it moves — on the revenue tree, where the lines are table rows, that is a visible jump.

Three things that must survive the conversion

One. Eight of the nine only ask when the line holds something. An empty line you just added goes on the first press, with no question at all, and that is right — a version that always asked would be worse than what is there now. Both treatments keep it: the × removes an empty line outright and only turns into a question when there is something to lose.

Two. Three of the nine take more than the line. Removing a route on the revenue tree takes its periods and their drivers; removing a period takes its drivers; removing a value on How we compete takes its hows and its measures. Those three say so today and must keep saying so — which is one more reason the question has to carry the line's own words rather than a fixed sentence.

Three. This page is already inconsistent and the conversion does not fix it: removing a BU from the list, and taking a BU off a mapping, both go on the first press with no question anywhere. I am not proposing to add questions there — I am naming it so the difference is a decision rather than an accident. Say if you want them to match.

What I need from you

  • A or B. I recommend A: the question lands where the line is, nothing on the page moves, and the thing you pressed is the thing that asks. B is right if reading the line while you decide matters more than the list holding still.
  • The two words: Keep and Remove. The browser gave you Cancel and Okay, which name nothing.
  • The sentence for the three that take more than the line — today it is “Remove ‘Walk-in’ and its 3 drivers?”. I would keep exactly that, in the line's own place.
  • Whether the two controls that ask nothing at all should join this family, or stay as they are.
