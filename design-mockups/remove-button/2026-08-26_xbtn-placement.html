The remove button’s seat

The × that removes a row rides inside the name cell — deliberately, because a column of its own would cost every row its width — but the field is full-width, so the × wraps to a second line and every editable row grows from 56px to 76px. Both candidates below put it on the line; both are the real platform, measured: row back to 56px, × still hittable at its centre.

Today— the × takes a line of its own

The row is 20px taller than its neighbours for the sake of a 14px glyph.

A— beside the fieldRecommended

The field yields 30px and the × sits after it, outside the border. Outside is the point: this × removes the row, and a × drawn inside an input is the search box’s clear-the-text idiom — somebody pressing it to erase a name would delete the whole measure. Attached to the row, not to the text.

B— inside the field's right edge

Tighter, and the field keeps its full width. The cost is the borrowed meaning above — and a long name now runs under the ×, so the last characters of exactly the values being edited sit beneath a delete control.

Where it applies

One CSS rule, keyed on the pair (a field followed by an × in the same cell), so it reaches every table that uses the pattern — measures, tactics, deliverables & outcomes, milestones — and touches nothing else. Nothing changes in read mode. Say A or B and I will make it real.
