Reporting as its own tab

Three ways to make it orange, shot from the real page. The Report button goes with it — a tab and a button that do the same thing are one control too many.

“How about for the reporting to split it as a tab beside the Strategy and Performance to be more obvious to the users, with its distinct orange colour.”

Today

Two tabs, and Reporting hidden behind a button on the right of the row

Reporting is a mode you enter from Performance. It is only visible once you have gone to the right page and noticed a button among three others.

Distinct without shouting. The rule is the only thing that is new to the row’s vocabulary.

Reporting open, with the bar on the same row

The count, Submit, Save draft and Close already ride this row. Measured with the tab added: one line at 1500, 1280, 1100 and 1000px, with no overflow. Below that the row scrolls sideways, as it already does.

What changes besides the colour

  • The Report button goes. A button that opens what a tab beside it opens is a duplicate, not a choice.
  • The tab is only drawn when there is a cycle open and you may report into it — the same condition the button had. A tab that opens nothing is worse than no tab.
  • The “not submitted yet” dot moves from Performance to Reporting, where the thing it is about now lives.
  • Leaving the tab leaves reporting, exactly as pressing Close does today.
What this reverses, and what it does not §63 removed a Reporting section from inside Performance, on your point that “performance is a result of reporting, so having inside performance 2 buttons performance and reporting doesn’t make sense.” That argument was about two siblings under one heading. A tab at the top level is a different placement — Reporting stops being buried inside the thing it produces. Worth recording as a deliberate change of mind rather than an oversight.
One cost The row gains a third tab and Presentation and Bands stay on the right, so at 1000px it is full. It does not wrap — it scrolls — but on a small laptop, with a report open, the right-hand controls will need a sideways nudge to reach.
