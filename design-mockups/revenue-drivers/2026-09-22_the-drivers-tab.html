Revenue drivers — the screens, for your sign-off

Three screens, drawn before anything is built. The arithmetic behind them is built and is proved against your own tool, so every figure on this page is a real number out of your five-channel tree — nothing is typed in to look plausible.

  • Real — every baseline, uplift, month count and total, and the whole growth split.
  • Invented, and marked where it appears — the actual figures on screen 3 (nobody has reported against a tree yet, so there are none), and which rows are connected to which objective.
What I could not check. This page is drawn from the platform's own colours, type sizes and table styles, taken out of the live stylesheet. It is not a screenshot of the running platform — the tool I normally use to drive it is not installed in this session. So the shapes and colours are the product's; the exact pixel fit of a long table at a narrow window is not proved until it is built.
Screen 1 — where the tree lives, on a business unit that sells one way
Decision 1 · where it sits I am recommending a section inside Strategy, between SWOT and Plan — so a unit reads who we are → where we stand → where the number comes from → what we will do about it. It costs nothing: it uses the same permission the Plan already uses, so nobody's access changes and no new column appears on Roles & access.

The alternative is a tab of its own beside Strategy, Performance and Reporting. That is more obvious to find, and it makes a five-tab row where there are four today, and it separates the tree from the plan it exists to justify. Say which you want.

  • The three kinds of period are the navy bands. Base year, a season with its real dates, and an increment that has no baseline at all. The band carries the months and the money, so the page never asks you to add up the parts yourself.
  • The months are worked out, never typed. The base year is twelve minus the seasons used here, so the periods always come to twelve and can never overlap. Change Ramadan's dates once and every base period in the client moves with it.
  • The why sits under the driver's name, the way a tactic's description already does — not in a column of its own, which would take about 150px off the driver name at every width.
  • Three states in the last column, and the difference matters at review time: connected to something somebody is working on, an assumption that moves for a reason nobody is scored on, and nobody has said yet — which is the only one the page complains about.
Decision 2 · the last column An assumption row and an unanswered row look different on purpose. Do you want the page to hold the plan back until every row is either connected or deliberately marked as an assumption — the way it already holds a report back when a figure is missing — or just say so quietly and let you get on?

Decision 3 · where this reading lives I have drawn it on Performance, beside the strategy score, because the whole point is reading the two side by side. It could instead be a fourth section under Drivers. Which?
Decision 4 · how much of a driver's actual gets typed You said the office types an unconnected row's actual, for now. On this screen that is five of the seven rows per period. The connected ones read themselves from the measure. Is that the right split to start with, or should the office type all of them and the connection only be used for reading, not for filling?
Decision 5 · the seasons page Seasons are defined once for the whole client — a name and two dates — and used by name in any unit. I have not drawn it because it is four fields and a list. It goes in Setup, beside the reporting cycle. Any objection?

What happens next

Nothing on these screens is built. The arithmetic is, and it is checked against your tool on all five channels — every period, every roll-up and the growth split, 59 checks with nothing typed in by hand. Once you have answered the five questions above I build the screens and nothing else changes in the platform.
