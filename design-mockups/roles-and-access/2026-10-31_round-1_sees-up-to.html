Roles & access as a ladder

Nine columns become one question per role: how far up the ladder they see. What they report is not a setting at all: a person reports the piece they hold and nothing else, whatever they can see above it.

  • The ladder, bottom up: own piece · the plan it sits in · the company · the group. Each rung includes everything on it: seeing the plan means seeing the sibling directions too.
  • Seeing is not reporting. A direction owner set to see the plan reads every direction in it and enters figures on his own alone. The pen stays with the holder.
  • The office writes everything and runs the cycle and Setup. By rule, not by cell.
  • A role sets the default; a person can be moved one rung up or down in the register.

Roles & access

Each role reports what it holds. Set how far up it sees.

Reports is a word, never a control: it is what the role holds, and the register decides who holds what. Sees up to is the one control; the rung a role cannot stand on is struck through (a CEO holds no piece, so "own piece" is not a choice for him). The two office roles have no control: they see and write everything.

Roles & access

The same table with one more column: where the role opens on sign-in.

  • The window shows the same rungs. Its row of units and functions is exactly what the person reaches on the ladder, no more. It never carries a pen, so nothing is reported from it.
  • Opens in replaces today's computed rule and the client-wide switch. A role that reports nothing (the CEOs) opens in the window by default; a role that holds a piece opens on the workbench, where its pen is.
  • Read is a button for everyone, on every page, as today. Opens in only decides the first screen.
