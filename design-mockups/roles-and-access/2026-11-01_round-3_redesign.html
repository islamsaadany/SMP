Roles & access, three ways

Each design answers the same three things for every role: who is in it, how far up they see, and whether they enter and submit what they hold. The sentence under each role says what the settings mean in plain words, and changes as you press. The settings are shared, so a press in one design shows in all three.

A

The table, made readable

closest to today; one row per role

What changes from the built page. Each row names the roles it covers in this client's words, so "Owner" reads "Function head" at El Abd. Seeing and writing are two controls: the level buttons for seeing, a switch for entering and submitting. The Copilot is a switch too, in the same style. The office's two seats move to one line above the table, since nothing about them can be set. The last column says what the row's settings mean, including what the role does not see.

B

Cards with an upright ladder

the levels drawn as a ladder, top at the top

Why a card. Each role gets room for its meaning, and the ladder stands upright so "higher" means higher. The green rung is where the role works; the navy rungs above it are what it reads. Press a rung to read up to it, or press the top lit rung to step down.

C

Pick a role, see what it gets

a list on the left, the settings and a live preview on the right

Why a preview. The SMO team adjusts a role by looking at what a real person in it would get: the navigation row they would see and the items they would enter or only read. It takes more space, and it shows one role at a time.

My pick is A, with C's preview opening from a row when you want to check one. A is scannable across all roles at once, which is what an SMO team does when it reviews access, and it keeps today's page where people already look for it.
