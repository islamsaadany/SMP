01

The remove button sits too close to the one beside it

Broke recently

On a plan row, two small buttons sit side by side: the eye that hides a row from the presentation, and the × that removes the row altogether. They are now 23.5 pixels apart, centre to centre. The standard asks for at least 24, and the reason is not pedantry: at that distance a thumb aiming for one lands on the other, and one of the two throws a row away.

It is 18 rows on a single plan, and it is new. Nothing about the buttons was touched. When the platform moved to one typeface, the × character in the new face is narrower than the old one — and that button has no size of its own, it is only as wide as the character inside it. So it quietly shrank from 19.7 pixels to 15.

Today
The two circles are the room each button needs. They overlap, which is the fault.
Proposed
The × is given a minimum size and a little more air. The circles come apart.

02

The boxes you type into are hard to find

Your decision

Every box on an editing page is drawn with the same hairline the tables use for their gridlines. Against the white inside the box, that line reads at 1.38 to one. The standard asks for 3 to one for the edge of anything you are meant to use, and on a plan page with twenty boxes on it the practical effect is that you cannot see at a glance where one box ends and the next begins.

The fix is one new colour used only where a border is the thing that makes a box a box — about fourteen places. Deliberately not the gridline colour itself, which is used in 271 places: darkening that would turn every table in the platform into a grid.

Three strengths, same picture each time. The question is how firm you want it.

Shown in

A visible step up, and still quiet. Short of what the standard asks for.

Standard 3.01 to one What I suggest

Exactly the minimum the standard asks for, and the page still reads as a plan rather than as a form.

Firmer 4.06 to one

Unmistakable, and it starts to make a table of twenty rows look busy.

What I suggest

Standard. It is the lowest strength that satisfies the rule, so it is the one I can defend without an argument about taste, and in the pictures above it is the first one where you can run your eye down a column of boxes without hunting.

If you prefer Firmer I would take it; if you prefer Softer I would say out loud that we have chosen not to meet the standard on this one point, so it is written down rather than discovered later.

03

The help page is shut to the people it was written for

Drawn, not photographed Your decision

The knowledge base answers questions like how do I reopen my report and who can change a target. It is the office’s page only: a unit head or a strategy custodian cannot open it at all, so the one place that explains the platform is shut to almost everybody who uses it.

That was decided on purpose, because some answers are about running the office and are not a client’s business. But every answer already carries a mark saying who it is for — and counting them, 61 of the 73 are marked as everybody’s. Only 12 are the office’s own. The filter that reads those marks was written, and the assistant is the only thing that uses it; the page itself never asks. So it could be opened tomorrow, each person seeing the answers marked for their side, with no new writing and no new rule.

04

Nothing records what we do in the console

Drawn, not photographed Your decision

Inside a client, every change is written down: who moved a target, when, from what to what, with a page to read it on and a way to put it back. Outside, in Forefront’s own console, nothing is recorded at all — adding a client, archiving one, giving somebody the super user seat, handing out a password, turning a module on. There is a table in the database waiting for it, made some time ago, and nothing has ever written a row into it.

Everything the console does goes through one door, so this is one line of writing at that door rather than a change to fourteen screens.

Two with no picture

Nothing on any screen moves, so two identical images would be worse than a sentence.

Nothing measures how big a button is, or how far it is from its neighbour

There are 180-odd automatic checks on this platform and not one of them looks at the size of a control or the gap beside it. That is exactly why the first fix on this page could shrink a button by five pixels and ship unnoticed. One check, run with the rest, closes the whole class rather than this one instance.

No picture: a check has no screen.

The new module pages have never been colour-checked

The sweep that measures every colour against its background opens one file — the main platform. Insights, Notes, the Tracker, Portfolio and the Copilot are separate pages served by the server, and the sweep has never once opened any of them. They may well be fine. Nobody has looked.

No picture: until it has been run, I do not know what it would show, and a guessed picture would be a fiction.

One thing I got wrong

Correcting it is cheaper than carrying it.

“Two boxes with no label”

I reported that two boxes on the reporting page have nothing naming them. Measuring it properly, that is wrong. The reporting notes sit in a column headed Note, and a column heading is the name of every box under it — to a screen reader as much as to the eye. The cycle note sits under a heading that reads The owner’s note on this cycle.

What is left is one small nicety: the cycle note’s heading is not formally tied to the box, so a screen reader announces the two separately rather than together. One line, no visible change, and nowhere near worth a picture.
