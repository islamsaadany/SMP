The headings are gone. You are right, and it is the platform's own rule: a heading that restates what the rows already show is furniture. A row with a last message is a conversation. A row without one is somebody who has not written. In a panel this small each heading cost a whole row of the list.

Drawn in the real corner. The platform was opened, the corner opened, the search typed — only the list is replaced. Every other pixel is the product's own. The names and places are from your screens.

1Today, and the proposal

2The cap speaks at the foot

You asked for the second group to stop at ten. With no headings there is nowhere to say so — so it goes to the foot, which is where it belongs anyway: that is where the list runs out and where somebody is looking when they have not found their person.

Why not a heading with a count. A heading sits at the top, where you have not yet run out of anything. The line only means something once you have read to the bottom without finding who you wanted — so that is where it is.

3The one line that stays, and why

“5 found in all conversations and on the register” is not a description — it is the only thing on the screen saying where the search looked. Without it, results reach conversations that are answered while the Waiting tab is the one lit, and the office is quietly misled. It now says the register too.

It is one line, and it fits on one line.

4Settled
