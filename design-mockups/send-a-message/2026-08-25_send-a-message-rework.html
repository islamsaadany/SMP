Send a message — what to fix

Measured on the built file, not estimated. The page works; what it does badly is hold the one irreversible act in the product at the far end of a scroll that gets longer the more carefully you choose who it goes to.

What is actually wrong

  • The primary action runs away from you. Every recipient draws a named chip, so choosing more people pushes Send further down. At 33 people the block is 299px; on your register it is roughly double.
  • The page does three jobs in one scroll. Compose, pick up a draft, review what was sent — and the two you might have come for are underneath the one you did not.
  • You always land in an empty composer. There is no way in from a draft or from something already sent.
  • The confirmation is confirm(). A browser dialog that can be permanently silenced — and then the send just happens. §69.22 removed exactly this from the register's password reset for exactly this reason.
  • “A button” is a section. Two optional fields sit between the message and Send with a heading as loud as “Who gets it”.
  • No way to see it in your own inbox first before it goes to seventy-six people and cannot be recalled.

The browser's confirm() can be silenced by a checkbox the person ticked six months ago on a different site, and after that the most irreversible act in the product happens on one press with nothing shown. The platform's own dialog also gets to name the three who will not receive it — which is the fault that started all of this.

The six changes

  • The audience result becomes a summary line. Two counts and a disclosure; the names open on request. Fixed height, so Send stops moving.
  • Drafts and Sent become header dropdowns. §90's move: a thing done occasionally belongs in the header, and the page is for the thing you came to do.
  • A sticky send bar at the foot of the pane, carrying the count.
  • “A button” folds into “Write it” as one row. It is part of the message, not a peer of the audience.
  • The confirmation becomes a real dialog that names the subject, the count and the skipped.
  • “Send me a copy first” — the missing safeguard. One message, to you, with this audience's own resolution, before the real send.

Not proposed, deliberately: the audience is resolved on the server on every tick, which is right — the server holds the register and the screen must not guess. Worth debouncing so five ticks are one request, but that is a performance fix, not a design one, and it changes nothing you can see.
