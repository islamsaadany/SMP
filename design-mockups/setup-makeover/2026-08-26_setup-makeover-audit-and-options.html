The Setup makeover

The audit of the Setup page as it is today, the practices worth taking from HR_ERP's admin home, two structures to choose between, and the naming decisions that apply to either. Nothing in the real sources has been touched — this page is the proposal.

1The audit — seven findings, worst first

Measured on the current build (v3.29 sources), signed in as the SMO, 1600×1000 window.

The rail is taller than the window.

18 entries under 5 group headers run past 1,000px — measured: “How it looks” (Branding, Communication) sits below the fold at a 1000px-tall window. A control below the fold is a control that does nothing (§90's own lesson). Folding groups helps only if you already know which group to unfold.

Nothing tells the SMO team what is waiting on them.

The gear lands on Reporting cycle. Unanswered conversations, an open figure-set claim, units with no custodian, people never issued a password, a “they said where they work” declaration — each lives on its own page, so finding out whether anything needs you is a walk through five pages. The group badges count entries (6 · 3 · 4 · 3 · 2): numbers that never change, wearing the shape of numbers that should.

Three names from one word family, in three different groups.

“Messages” (the office inbox), “Send a message” (the broadcast composer), “Communication” (email settings). HR_ERP hit exactly this — “Announcements” beside “Communications” — and fixed it by merging and renaming until each name meant one thing.

Daily work is one group out of five; set-once pages take the other twelve rows.

“Running the cycle” is where the SMO team lives every week. The other four groups are visited rarely — yet they occupy two-thirds of the rail every day, which is most of why finding number 1 exists.

Several names don't say what is behind them.

“Import” (of what? — plans), “Labels” (it renames the tenant's vocabulary), “Official BU list” sitting two rows from “Business units” (a pair §54/§58 can tell apart and a new SMO team member cannot), “Communication” beside “Send a message”.

No search.

18 entries is past the size where scanning beats typing — and some errands don't know their page: “where do I change the logo?” is Branding, “where do I reset a password?” is People register.

Related halves live apart.

Import and Archived plans are two pages about one subject — a plan arriving, and the plan it replaced. Figure sets vs Figures I report split the setting from the doing across two groups. Figure sets itself already settled this shape once: one page, two sections (§46.2).

2What HR_ERP's admin home does right

Read from its source, not from memory. Five practices carry over; the two options below differ only in how far they take them.

Attention pills, never “0”A row where something waits carries a gold count; a row with nothing waiting carries nothing. Zero states never render a pill.
The daily queue leadsSections are ordered by workflow — the one where things wait on the admin comes first, the registry second, set-once last.
Grouped rows, one screenDestinations are compact rows dealt into two balanced columns, so the whole admin fits without scrolling.
Description beside the nameEvery destination says in one line what you'd walk into, so nobody opens a page to find out what it is.
One quiet glyph per rowDrawn marks that inherit the text colour — never emoji — so a lit row reads as attention, not decoration.

3Option A — the rail keeps the door, an Overview opens it Recommended

The railed page stays (§47.7's one door holds). Three additions: an Overview landing that shows the cycle and everything waiting on the office; a search at the top of the rail; and attention pills on the rows that owe something. Plus the regrouping and renames from section 5, and Import + Archived plans merged into one page with two sections.

What it costs. A new page that must stay truthful — its counts have to come from the same functions the pages themselves use, or the Overview becomes a page that lies (§53.5's drift, invited on purpose, so the check must assert agreement). The rail's group badges change meaning from entry-count to waiting-count, and rows gain a glyph column the rest of the product's rails don't have. Merging Import with Archived plans is a small reversal of their §46 split, recorded as one.

4Option B — the hub, HR_ERP's shape taken whole Alternative

No rail. The gear opens a hub: every destination as a described row, grouped, dealt into two balanced columns, pills where something waits, search on top. Each page then carries a “‹ Setup” way back.

Descriptions sit on the row rather than behind a hover — SMP's hub has the room, and a description you can read without hunting is the better half of that practice.

What it costs. Every hop between two Setup pages goes through the hub — one extra click, dozens of times a day for the SMO team, which is exactly who this makeover is for. It abandons the rail pattern the rest of the product uses (a unit's plan, a capability's projects), and it reverses §47.7's page shape, which would be recorded as a reversal. This is why A is the recommendation: A's Overview page gives the hub's one real gift — seeing everything waiting at a glance — without giving up one-click movement.

5The namings — each row is its own decision

Stored keys never change (§30.2, §58 — renaming a stored field is a migration for a word); every rename below is a label. Accept, edit or refuse them row by row.

TodayProposedThe argument
MessagesInbox “Messages” and “Send a message” read as the same thing. Inbox says which one you answer; Send a message stays what it is.
CommunicationEmail The page sets the email's display name, reply-to, kicker and footer — nothing else. Naming it Email ends the three-way collision.
Import + Archived plansPlan import & archives Two pages, one subject: the plan arriving and the plan it replaced. One page, two sections — the shape Figure sets already settled (§46.2).
LabelsTerminology The page renames what the tenant calls a pillar, a theme, an aspiration. “Labels” sounds like stickers; Terminology says vocabulary.
Official BU listkeep “Official BU” is the client's own word (§58) and the page exists to hold it. The confusion with Business units is answered by the one-line description beside it, not by a new name.
WhoPeople & access Group names below are the §46 question-style set, invited for rethink. “Who” is the question; People & access is the answer, and answers scan faster in a rail.
What we runThe organisation Units, companies, functions, capabilities — the shape of the business, in one word the client already uses.
How it's measuredMeasurement Same answer-not-question move.
How it looksBranding & email With Communication renamed Email, the group's two rows are Branding and Email — the name can simply say so.
Running the cyclekeep It already names exactly what the group is, and it is the one group name people have lived with daily since §46.

6What I need from you

Pick the structure.

A, B, or A with changes. My recommendation is A — the Overview gives the hub's visibility without the extra click on every hop.

Where does the gear land?

On the new Overview (A's default, drawn above), or keep landing on Reporting cycle with Overview one row above it. I recommend landing on Overview — it answers “is anything waiting on me?” before anything else.

How deep does search go?

Page names + hidden keywords (“logo” → Branding) as the first step, or also live data — a typed person's name jumping to their register row, a unit name to its page. I recommend keywords first; data-search is a real feature with its own decisions and can be a later step.

The namings.

Accept, edit or refuse section 5 row by row — none of them blocks the rest.

Anything else from HR_ERP's admin you want that I didn't take?

I took the pills, the grouping discipline, the descriptions and the glyphs. I left its hover-only descriptions (A's rail rows are too narrow; B puts them on the row) and its two-column card home (that is Option B).
