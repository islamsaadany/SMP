Strategy module — notes and five ideas to lift it

I walked the built module as the Strategy Office sees it: the group, a business unit (Mobile), and every tab under each. Then I read it beside the Internal Tracker, My tasks, Insights, Portfolio, Meeting Notes and the Copilot. Notes first, then five screens, each one idea that stands on its own.

Mockups only · nothing here is built
What already works — keep it
  • The tables. Navy header, zebra rows, codes in the mono face. They are consistent across Plan, Performance and Focus, and they read well.
  • The three performance cards. Key Objectives, Pillars, Execution, each with a band and a way in. A reader gets the answer before the detail.
  • The Temple. The one screen that shows the strategy as a shape instead of a list.
  • Reporting as a button, not a tab. It is the only thing on the row that asks for action, and it looks like it.
  • Empty states that explain. “Mobile has no revenue tree yet” says what a tree is for before it asks for one.
What holds it back
  • There is no front page. The module opens on a table (the group’s one capability). Nobody is told where the strategy stands or what needs them. → idea 1
  • It looks like a different product from its neighbours. Tracker, My tasks and Insights all open on the navy panel with a title and counts. Strategy was left for the end of that round and still has none. → idea 1
  • The story is cut into tabs with no thread. Foundation, SWOT, Drivers and Plan sit side by side, but nothing shows which pillar serves which objective, or which weakness a pillar answers. A strategy’s logic is its links, and the links are not on screen. → idea 2
  • A pillar is a title over two tables. No owner, no line of intent, no standing, and the timing of five tactics is twenty tiny circles. → idea 3
  • The other modules are invisible from here. A report in Insights that backs a pillar, a task in the Tracker chasing a late tactic, a decision in a meeting note: all one module-switch away and none linked. → idea 4
  • Four rows of navigation before any content. About 150px of every screen, and the unit row has no room left at ten units. → idea 5
  • What is missing is scattered. “Not in the register” sits inside a table cell; “15 places have nothing marked” sits at the foot of Focus. There is no one place that lists what the plan still lacks. → idea 1

IDEA 02

Line of sight

A fifth section beside Foundation, SWOT, Drivers and Plan that shows the links: which pillar serves which key objective, and which pillar answers each weakness, threat and opportunity. An objective no pillar serves, or a threat nothing answers, shows as a gap. This is the question a consultant asks first in a strategy review, and today the screen cannot answer it.

The aspiration, objectives, pillars and SWOT points are Mobile’s own. The links between them are drawn for illustration; on the real page the unit’s people choose them and the page starts empty.

What changes for the user
  • A SWOT point and a pillar can be tied together; an objective and a pillar likewise.
  • The gaps show without anyone counting: a threat nothing answers, a pillar that serves no objective.
  • The Copilot’s SWOT-to-directions flow already reasons this way. Its suggestions could land here as proposed links to accept or drop.
To agree
  • Is tying a SWOT point to a pillar something your clients would actually maintain, or should the Copilot propose the links and a person only confirm?
  • Its own section, as drawn, or folded into the Temple so the Temple becomes the map?
IDEA 03

The pillar as a page

The pillar gets a proper head: its owner, a line of intent, the objectives it serves, and its two scores, visible on the Plan and not only on Performance. Under it, the tactics can be read as a roadmap across the four quarters as well as a table, with today marked. The table stays one press away and does not change.

IDEA 04

Around this pillar

A rail on the pillar that gathers what the other modules already hold about it: open actions from the Tracker, reports in Insights, projects in Portfolio, decisions from Meeting Notes, and a line to ask the Copilot with this pillar already in view. Strategy becomes the place the rest of the platform hangs from, which is what the client bought it as.

The measures are the demo’s. Every line in the rail is invented for the drawing. A group shows only for a module the client has and the viewer may open; the Internal Tracker’s is the office’s alone.

What changes for the user
  • An off-track measure sits beside the action chasing it and the decision that explains it.
  • A consultant preparing a review has the supporting reports to hand without leaving the pillar.
  • From the other side, a task, report or note can be tagged to a pillar where it is written.
To agree
  • Which of the four are worth linking first? My pick is Actions and Decisions, since they explain a number, which is what a review needs.
  • Tagged by a person, or suggested by the Copilot and confirmed?
  • Does the client’s own staff ever see the Actions group, or only the office?
IDEA 05

Two rows instead of four

Today the place (group, company, unit or function) is chosen on one row, the view on a second, and the section on a third, under the top bar. Proposed: the place becomes one control that says where you are and opens a searchable list with each place’s standing, and the views sit beside it. One navy row and the section row remain.

If only one is built

Idea 1, because it fixes three of the notes at once (no front page, the odd one out among the modules, scattered gaps) and uses only what the platform already knows. Idea 2 is the one that would most change what a client thinks the product is: it turns a well-kept record of a strategy into something that checks the strategy’s logic. Ideas 3 and 4 make a strong second round together. Idea 5 I would test with the Strategy Office before drawing it further.
