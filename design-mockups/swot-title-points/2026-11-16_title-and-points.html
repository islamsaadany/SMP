A SWOT line: its title and its points

What you type in the box, and what the page prints once the pen closes. The first line is the title; every line under it is a point. Six cases, each one as typed beside the same line as printed.

Title bold, points as bullets, each in line with itself. The dash you typed is taken off the front of the line and drawn as the bullet in a column of its own; the words sit in their own column, so a point that runs to a second line lines up under its first word, not under the dash. The empty lines you left between points are dropped, so the points sit close under their title.

The first line is the title whatever it looks like. Capitals are not required, and a dash is not required on a point: a point typed without one gets the same bullet as a point typed with one, so a box reads the same however its lines were typed. A leading dash, bullet or asterisk is taken as the marker; anything else is the point's own words.

The left is what is built and pushed: a one-line entry keeps the look it has had all along, and only a line that carries points gets its title in bold. The right makes every entry read as a heading, which also changes every SWOT already written. Say which you want; switching to the right is a one-line change.

What does not change: the box you type in. A text box cannot show one of its lines in bold, so while the pen is open the title looks like the rest of the text. The spreadsheet and PowerPoint downloads keep the raw text with its line breaks.
