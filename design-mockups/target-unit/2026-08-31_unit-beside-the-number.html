The unit sits beside the number, not inside it

You type 1 into a target whose unit is already %, save, reopen — and it reads 1% again. Nothing is broken: that is the inheritance you asked for in §199.6. What is wrong is that the number and its unit share one box, so there is no way to tell whether you are meant to type the unit or not. This is the fix, drawn.

What happens now

Row 1 is mid-edit. The unit column says % and the box says 1 — and row 2 beside it says 50%. Two rows, two spellings of the same thing, and nothing on the screen says which one the platform wants. On blur the 1 becomes 1%, which reads as the platform undoing your typing.

The box holds the number only. The unit is fixed beside it, on its own quiet ground, taken from the Unit column — you cannot type into it and you never have to. Row 4 has no unit, so there is nothing beside the box and the field behaves exactly as it does today.

What a person does, step by step

1

They pick % in the Unit column. The % appears beside every target box on that row, at once — that is the whole point: the screen says the unit is handled before they type anything.

2

They type 90. They see 90 %, made of their number and the row's unit, so there is no question about whether to add the sign.

3

They leave the field. Nothing visibly changes — which is the repair. Today the box silently becomes 90%; here what was on screen while typing is what is on screen afterwards.

4

They reopen it tomorrow and the box still says 90 with % beside it. Today it says 90%, and typing over it starts the confusion again.

Nothing about storage changes. The plan still holds 90% as one value, the reading view still shows 90% as one figure, the deck and the workbook are untouched, and the server judges exactly what it judges today. This is only how the field is drawn while somebody is writing in it — which is where the confusion was.

Where it applies

Everywhere the Unit column already exists, and nowhere else — a key objective's 3-year and this-year targets, and a pillar measure's target, in the office's pen and in a custodian's fill mode alike. One builder for all of them, so the two sides of the platform cannot come to disagree about how a target is written (§53.5).

Before I build it

Two things worth your word, because both are visible and neither is obvious:

The number sits to the right, against its unit, the way a figure and its unit read on a page. Today it is centred in the box. Say if you would rather it stayed centred.

The unit's chip is quiet grey, not gold. It is a fact about the row rather than something to press, and gold on this screen means you can act on this.
