Track Record: how it works, step by step

Step 1

The client registry

Super user only

One list of every client and its assignments. Nobody else creates a client or types dates and teams. Everything later reads from here. The list you send gets loaded once; after that, a row is added here.

ATake this prompt to your assistant

I am writing up a Planning assignment my consulting firm finished, for consultants who were not there.

  Client:   Raya Trade (Distribution, Large)
  Work:     Strategy build (Planning)
  When:     January to June 2025
  Our team: Islam S. (lead), Sara M., Omar K.

Interview me. One question at a time. Push back if my answer is vague.

Ask about: why the client came to us, what we were asked to do, their people and roles, the main stages by month, each deliverable, what came of it, who on our team to ask about what.

Then ask for learnings: what went wrong (hiccup), what worked (practice), what I know now (lesson).

When I say done, write it back in the fixed shape below. Use my words. Leave out anything I only half answered.

Step 7

Adding a learning at any time

Every consultant

For something that happened during the work, without waiting for the write-up. Pick the client, then its assignment. That gives the learning its context. Pick what kind it is. Four short questions, and it appears on the client's page under that assignment.

What changes from today

  • The name becomes Track Record, and each entry is an assignment's write-up.
  • Clients and assignments come from one registry, kept by the super user. Nobody types a client, dates or a team anywhere else.
  • No admin has to ask for a write-up. The task appears on the lead's My work when the assignment ends.
  • The prompt is the first thing you see when you write one up, already filled from the registry, with Open in Claude beside it.
  • Check and publish happen on the same page as the paste. Two states only: Draft and Published.
  • Every learning is tied to an assignment, so a Planning hiccup is never mixed up with a Retainer one. "Write up a period" goes away; the write-up's prompt asks for learnings too.
  • Gone: "Open a piece of work", "Ask for the write-up", the desk and shelves, and "Not yet in a case study".
