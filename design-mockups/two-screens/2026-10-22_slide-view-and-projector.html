Presenting on two screens

The projector shows the slides, full screen, as today. The laptop keeps the slide view you already use, so the office can scroll the slides, see the keys, and leave for any page of the platform, without the room seeing any of it. The keys on the laptop move the room's screen. Real screenshots of the platform with the new parts drawn in.

1. Following the presentation

A dark strip under the slide view's bar says what the room is seeing, which slide of how many, and the time left, with the keys. The slide on the room's screen is marked ON SCREEN in the list. → and ← move the room; T, R and Y work as they do in the presentation.

2. Looking ahead without the room seeing

Clicking another slide in the list shows it on the laptop only, with a note saying the room cannot see it. Enter puts it on the room's screen; Esc brings the laptop back to what the room is seeing. The projector does not change.

Laptop

Projector, unchanged

3. Working anywhere in the platform

Leave the slide view to fix a figure or look something up: the projector keeps its slide, and a strip at the top of every page says what the room is seeing, with the time, buttons to move the room or pause, and Back to slides. While on a page the keys stay the page's own, so typing never moves a slide. After fixing a figure, R back in the slide view puts it on the room's screen.

Projector, unchanged

How it starts

Connect the projector as a second screen (Windows: Win+P → Extend). In the slide view, Present opens the slides full screen on the projector and the laptop stays on the slide view. The first time, the browser asks once to let the platform use the second screen. With one screen, Present works exactly as it does today. For the office only, for now.

To decide

  • The strip on other pages: the buttons drawn (back, forward, pause, Back to slides) are enough, or more.
