Not taste — the two things a typeface actually changes in this product: how much of the register fits without scrolling, and how much weight every person downloads.

The system stack loses on a fact, not an opinion: it is the widest by 66px — the only one that makes the register scroll sideways — and it renders differently on every machine, so nobody can be shown a screenshot and trust it matches what they will see.

On the People register

The densest screen in the product, and the one that shows a face's real manners at 13px.

My recommendation SOURCE SANS 3

Why: it is the narrowest of the four in real text (“Mohamed Essam” at 89px against Inter's 101px), which on a register of 33 people and nine columns is the difference between fitting and scrolling. It is humanist rather than geometric, so it stays readable at the 10.5–13px this product lives at, and it carries warmth without personality that competes — right for a consulting product a client reads their own numbers in.

Against Manrope: lighter still (24.3 KB) and handsome in the headline figures, but geometric faces flatten at small sizes and half this product is small text.

Against Inter and Plex: Inter is the safe modern default and reads as such — the audit's own brief warned against exactly that choice; Plex is excellent but its engineered character pulls toward a technical product rather than a boardroom one.

Name one — Source Sans 3, Manrope, IBM Plex Sans, Inter, or keep the system stack — and I will make it the product's face, delete the other three from the file, and re-run the width checks (a face change moves every measurement in the product, so the sweep matters more here than usual).
