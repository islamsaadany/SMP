Narrow Windows

Items 08 and 10 of the audit, measured before anything was proposed — and most of what item 08 predicted did not survive the measuring.

The audit said the product had no stated minimum window and that 1024 — the iPad landscape a CEO opens a link on — was “today’s broken width”. That was true when it was written. Wave 1 fixed it. What is left is one narrow band nobody had looked at, and a check so the floor cannot rot.

W4 · 1A table scrolls and never says sodefect

Between 821 and about 960 pixels a unit’s plan tables are wider than the pane by 35–75px. They scroll — §141 gave them a box for exactly this — but nothing on screen says they do: no scrollbar track, no fade, no shadow. A column heading is simply cut mid-word.

Below 821 the layout stacks and the table fits the full width. Above ~960 it fits beside the rail. The defect lives only in between — which is why walking the product at 1440 and at 768 finds nothing.

This is §108.5’s own rule, on a table

That section capped the Setup rail to the window and shipped the cap with the sign — a visible scrollbar track and a sticky fade — on the argument that a list that says “it ends here” when it does not is worse than a page that scrolls. The same is true of a table, and the table never got the same treatment.

Two ways to close it

Recommended

Give the box the sign

A always-visible thin scrollbar track plus a right-edge fade that rests once the last column is reached — §108.5’s pair, reused rather than reinvented. Touches only .tblscroll; nothing moves at any width where the table already fits.

Stack earlier instead

Raise the stack breakpoint from 820 so the band never happens. I drew this and it is worse: at 1024 stacking pushes the pane 270px down the page and leaves the rail row half empty. The arithmetic said +212px of pane; looking at it said no.

No sideways page scroll at any width. Nothing off screen. No control covered by another. The plan pen’s fields shrink but stay usable — the narrowest is the direction picker at 41px, which holds one character.

So there is nothing to repair and something to write down: a check that asserts the floor at 1440 / 1280 / 1024 / 900 / 820 / 768, so a later change cannot quietly break a width nobody opens.

My own probe cried wolf twice, and that is worth a line

It first reported a blocked control at 1024 and 900. Both were the probe: it measured the centre of a destination half-scrolled under the navigation’s clip, so the point it tested lands past the clip edge on the gear — while the visible half clicks perfectly well. Corrected to press the visible box, every width comes back clean. A correct build reported broken costs exactly as much as a broken build reported clean.

W4 · 4Six things parked, each needing a word rather than a build

These are the audit’s P3 list. None is broken; each is waiting on a decision that only you can make.

  1. A project’s drag grips are bound to nothing They render and do nothing, pending one answer: what does dropping a deliverable among the outcomes mean? Say “refused, with the reason shown” and the build is small.
  2. A pillars function’s key objectives can be read and not written Readable on the Overview, authorable nowhere. The natural home is the plan builder §129 shipped.
  3. The deck still shows a Due date column the panes dropped A column on the projector that is absent in the product. Align the deck, or keep it deliberately and I record why.
  4. The tour’s replay button sits where its audience cannot reach it The knowledge base became the office’s; the two stories fit the custodian and the owner, who can no longer open it. Either the replay moves, or the office gets a story of its own.
  5. Nothing in the suite has ever used a keyboard The searchable dropdowns, the dialogs, the eye/pen matrix and every icon-only control are unasserted from the keyboard. One session to walk it, fix the cheap gaps, and record the structural ones.
  6. Prose runs to 1166px on a 2560px monitor Held on purpose since §94.13. Stays parked until that monitor is in the audience.
