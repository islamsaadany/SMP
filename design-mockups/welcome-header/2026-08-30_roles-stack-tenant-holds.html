The welcome header, when somebody holds two roles

Islam: the roles can come under each other if that long, to avoid that the company name and SMO section come down. Measured on your own screen’s content — Hala, holding two roles over Customer Experience.

What is happening

The navy band lays the greeting and the tenant block side by side and is allowed to wrap. Two long role chips fill the line, so the whole Raya Trade block — the mark, the name, Strategy Management Office — drops underneath them.

It is not a narrow-window problem: it happens at every width measured, 1440 included.

The change

Four lines of CSS. The greeting takes the room that is left and its chips stack inside it; the tenant block is told not to wrap away.

.welcomeover .whero   { flex-wrap: nowrap; align-items: stretch; }
.welcomeover .wgreet  { flex: 1 1 0; min-width: 0; }
.welcomeover .wtenant { flex: 0 0 auto; }

/* Below 820px there is genuinely no room for two columns,
   so the old stacking comes back on purpose. */
@media (max-width: 820px) { .welcomeover .whero { flex-wrap: wrap; } }

What it costs

  • The greeting column gets narrower on a wide window, because the tenant block now always reserves its width. At 1440px the chips still sit on one line; a third long role would put them on two.
  • Below 820px nothing changes. The columns stack as they do today — that is the width at which side by side stops being honest.
  • Nothing else on the screen moves. The change is scoped to the navy band; the list of what is waiting on you, your pages and the way out are untouched.
