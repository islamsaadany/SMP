# The knowledge base's missing half — forty-four task recipes

**Status:** drafted, awaiting Islam's review. **Not yet in the product.**

The knowledge base explains how things *work*; these explain how to *do* things,
which is what somebody types into a bubble in the corner. They are written for
a person who has just hit a wall — **no section numbers, no internal
vocabulary**, and the tenant's own words for a pillar and a business unit are
substituted at read time.

**The voice, as settled:** concise — answer, then what to do next. **A recipe
that refuses something keeps one clause of *why*.** A bare "not yourself" reads
as the software being obstructive; "not yourself — a plan you're measured
against isn't yours to rewrite" reads as a decision somebody made on purpose.
Thirteen of these refuse something, and those thirteen are the ones people will
be annoyed by, which is where the eight extra words earn their keep.

---

## Reporting

### 1 · How do I report a figure?

Open your unit, go to **Performance**, and press **Report**. That turns the page
into a reporting form: each measure takes its actual, and a note if it needs one.

**Save draft** keeps what you have typed without telling anyone. **Submit** sends
it to the office.

### 2 · What is the difference between Save draft and Submit?

A draft is yours — saved, and nobody is told. Submitting hands the figures to
the office and marks your unit as having reported for the cycle.

Draft as often as you like; submit once the whole set is right.

### 3 · Can I still report once the cycle is locked?

No — once the office locks a cycle the figures are fixed. That is what makes a
closed cycle a record of what was true at the time rather than something that
keeps moving.

If a number is wrong, say so here. The office can still correct it.

### 4 · Why is one of my figures somebody else's to enter?

Some numbers belong to a team rather than to the unit that reports them —
revenue exists in Finance before anyone asks you for it. Those are grouped into
a named set with an owner, and the owner enters them.

It still counts towards your unit's total, so you can chase it. The page names
who owes it.

### 5 · Why does my score show a dash instead of 0?

Nothing has been reported for it yet. A dash means *not yet measurable*; 0 would
mean a real result of nothing, and would pull your average down.

Report an actual and the score appears.

### 6 · How do I add a note to a figure, and who reads it?

The note sits beside the actual on the reporting form. The office reads it, and
it travels with the figure into the review.

Use it for the thing a number cannot say — why it moved, or what happens next.

### 7 · I submitted by mistake — what now?

Tell the office here. Submitting is how a unit speaks for itself, so it is not
something you can quietly take back, but the office can reopen it for you.

### 8 · What is the dot beside Performance?

It means this subject owes a submission and you are able to make it. It clears
when you submit.

If you cannot report, you never see it — the platform does not nag somebody who
has no control that would clear it.

### 9 · Who can submit for my unit?

Whoever holds the unit — its head, its custodian, or the office. Submitting
speaks for the whole unit, so a contributor who reports their own lines cannot
do it.

### 10 · I can only see some of the lines on the reporting form. Why?

You are named on those and not the others. A contributor reports what the plan
names them on, and nothing else.

If you should have more, ask here.

## My plan

### 11 · There is a mistake in my plan — how do I fix it?

Not yourself. Plans are corrected by the Strategy Office — a plan you are
measured against is not yours to rewrite.

Tell them here what is wrong. That covers the aspiration and the SWOT too, not
only the measures.

### 12 · How do I add a pillar, a measure or a tactic?

The office adds them, on your unit's **Plan** page. Ask here and say where it
belongs and what it should be called.

### 13 · Why can't I reorder my pillars any more?

Because the order is part of what was agreed, so changing it is authoring the
plan — and plans are the office's. It used to be open and it is not any more.

Ask here and the office will move them.

### 14 · Who can edit my unit's plan, and why not me?

The Strategy Office. A plan is the agreement you are measured against, so it
stays with the people who keep the agreement rather than with either side of it.

Everything else — reading it, reporting against it, explaining a figure — is
yours.

### 15 · How does a plan arrive in the first place?

By upload. The office downloads the template, the unit fills it in, and
uploading it **authors** the plan — the codes are minted on arrival, which is
why the template carries none.

### 16 · What happens to my old plan when a new one is uploaded?

It is archived, not deleted, and it can be restored. Nothing an import does is a
deletion.

### 17 · What is a collaborator on a tactic?

Somebody who supports the tactic alongside the person accountable for it. Being
named as one is what lets a contributor report that line.

The office sets them, because being named there decides who may report.

### 18 · Can I change my targets during the year?

Not yourself, and the office will usually say no mid-cycle — a target that moves
while it is being measured stops being a target.

If something genuinely changed, say what and why here.

## Understanding the numbers

### 19 · How is my unit's headline number worked out?

From your **key objectives** — each actual against its target.

Not from the pillars below, which is why the two can disagree without either
being wrong. They sit at different altitudes.

### 20 · Why does a pillar disagree with my objectives?

A pillar's measures are internal to that pillar; your objectives are what the
unit is judged on. A pillar can struggle while the objectives are fine.

The platform keeps them apart rather than blending them into one number that
answers neither.

### 21 · What is execution, and why is 100% not "finished"?

It is what has been delivered divided by what should have been delivered **by
now**, worked out from each tactic's quarters.

So 100% means exactly on plan, not complete.

### 22 · What do the colours mean?

One scale for every figure judged against a benchmark: on track from 85%, needs
attention from 70%, at risk from 50%, off track below that.

The colour and the word come from the same place, so they can never disagree.

### 23 · What is a focus measure?

One the CEO has marked as a measure the year turns on. It changes no score — it
changes what gets looked at first.

### 24 · What is the reward line?

The point at which delivering the commitment earns. At 100% it is the target
itself; set higher, a measure can meet its target without clearing the line and
reads as *met, not earning*.

Either way it is scored against its own target exactly as before.

### 25 · How does my unit's number reach the group's?

Weighted, not averaged. Each unit carries a weight set on the group's
**Weighting** tab, and the group's figure is the weighted average of the unit
headlines.

Position on the page is not weight — arranging changes nothing.

## Capabilities and projects

### 26 · How do I add a project to a capability?

On the capability's **Projects** page, with the pen open. Projects belong to the
supporting function that owns the capability.

### 27 · Deliverable or outcome — which is this?

A **deliverable** is a thing you produce; an **outcome** is a change you can
measure. If it has a target and a direction, it is an outcome.

Both are evidence, and the score reads them as two halves.

### 28 · How is a project's performance worked out?

Half from its deliverables and half from its outcomes. Where a project has only
one of the two, that side carries the whole score.

### 29 · Due date or measure date — what is the difference?

A **due date** is when a milestone or deliverable should be done. A **measure
date** is when an outcome gets read.

### 30 · Why has my project no outcomes section?

Because it has none yet. An empty half is not drawn — a project with no outcomes
is a plan that committed to no measurable change, not a broken screen.

Open the pen and both halves appear, so you can add the first one.

## Getting in, and getting at things

### 31 · How do I sign in — my email or my name?

Your email, as it appears on the people register. The short sign-in name still
works if you know it.

If your address is not on the register, ask here.

### 32 · I have forgotten my password.

Ask the office here, or by any other route you have. They issue a temporary one,
and you choose your own the moment you use it.

### 33 · Why can't I open another business unit?

Access is by page: a unit you are not attached to is removed, not trimmed. You
see what your role is attached to.

Need something you can't open? Ask here — granting it is the office's to do.

### 34 · What does my role let me do?

It depends on what it is attached to as much as what it is called — holding a
unit gives you that unit, not all of them. Hold several roles and you get the
most generous answer of them, each about its own thing.

The **Knowledge base** page has the full table.

### 35 · How do I get access to something I cannot open?

Ask here, and say which page and why. Access is the office's to grant.

## The office's own work

### 36 · How do I add somebody to the register?

**Setup › People**, on the register itself. Give them an employee number or an
email — a name is not an identifier, and two people really can share one.

### 37 · How do I upload the people file?

**Setup › People**, from the register's file menu. It **adds and amends and
never removes** — nobody is retired by being left out of a file.

Anything it cannot match is set aside for you to answer rather than guessed at.

### 38 · How do I open a new reporting cycle?

**Setup › Running the cycle**. Opening one archives the figures and then clears
them, so every unit is asked again rather than inheriting last quarter's
answers.

### 39 · How do I run a review, or present one?

On a unit's **Performance** page — **Report** to enter figures, **Presentation**
to project the deck or to manage its slides.

### 40 · How do I put a picture into the review deck?

Under **Presentation › Manage slides**. Pick where it goes and it is assembled
into the deck when the deck opens.

It belongs to the cycle, so it is archived with the figures.

### 41 · How do I set the colours and the logo?

**Setup › Branding**. The mark must be a PNG — an uploaded SVG can carry code,
which is not a risk worth taking for a logo.

### 42 · What does Demo data do, and why can't I save in it?

It shows the full worked example — an invented tenant, so you can see a filled
platform without touching yours. It refuses to save on purpose.

**Clear project** shows the opposite: exactly what a new deployment looks like
on day one.

## The platform itself

### 43 · Does it work with no internet, and can I install it?

Yes to both. It is a single page and it keeps itself, so it opens offline, and
your browser will offer to install it with its own icon and window.

What it cannot do offline is save — anything typed goes up when you are back.

### 44 · Where does my data live?

In a database belonging to this deployment. Figures, plans and people are stored
there; what you have chosen on screen — light or dark, which columns you show —
stays in your own browser.
