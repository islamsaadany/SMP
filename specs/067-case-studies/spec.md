# 067 · Case studies — what we did for a client, written for the next person

**Status:** **clarified and DRAWN 2026-10-23, waiting on sign-off; nothing
built.** Eleven decisions are Islam's, taken the same day as three rounds of
questions (§3), and the name is his too: *"call it case studies"*.
**Mockup:** `design-mockups/case-studies/2026-10-23_case-studies-library.html`
— Case studies as **a library** (rule 1c, A17), nine drawings, waiting on
sign-off. Two earlier drawings are kept as the record and both were turned
down by Islam: the first, `2026-10-23_case-studies.html`, as *"very poor
design"*; the second, `2026-10-23_case-studies-tracker-look.html`, because
*"that view is suitable for the tracker only. the view of the cases should be
different … how would a library look like?"* (§10.1, §10.2). His three
answers on the first are in §10.

**Read with spec 045 (the consulting memory)**, which this changes rather than
sits beside: a case study is the main thing and the memory's entries move
inside it (decision 9). Where it lives, who reaches it, the interview prompt
and the assistant all follow 045, and where this departs from 045 it says so.

---

## 1 · What is asked

Islam, 2026-10-23: *"do we have a section for case studies .. real cases s what
happened with the clients in a format that can be read by newly joined team
with clear deliverables etc.?"*

We did not. The memory (spec 045) holds single lessons — one hiccup, one
practice — each naming its client. Nothing holds the story of a piece of work:
what we were asked to do, who did it, when, what we handed over, and what came
of it. A new joiner reading the memory reads forty separate lessons, not *here
is what we did for Raya Trade*.

**The memory's own spec already recorded the gap** (045, *Recorded, not done*):
an entry's period is free text, not a named engagement, and nothing before the
memory was built is in it. This spec closes both.

---

## 2 · What already exists and is used, not rebuilt

- **The memory** (spec 045): the four after-action questions, the interview
  prompt, the paste that is split into drafts somebody reads, the author named
  on every entry, the assistant that names its sources. All of it is kept.
- **The client card's industry and size** — the standard list, one per client
  (§322). A case study inherits both and stores neither (§53.5).
- **Archiving** (spec 047): a client set aside with its door closed and nothing
  lost. A past client is an archived client (decision 5).
- **My work** (§400, §583): one person's list across clients. It is where a
  case study waiting to be written appears (decision 7).
- **The file store** (§261) behind Resources and the Copilot's files: where a
  deliverable's file is kept (decision 6).
- **The Consultants list**: who is on our side of the team, picked rather than
  typed, so a name cannot be spelt two ways.

**Costs, stated when asked and accepted.** Each piece of work needs a name, and
a client can have several case studies (4). Every past client costs a short
set-up before its story can be written (5). Deliverables take longer to write
up than one box, and files take storage (6). Every case study is assigned by
somebody, and that somebody is an admin (7, 11). Nobody reads a case study
before new joiners do (10).

## 4 · What a case study holds

**The piece of work** — the thing a case study is about — carries:

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **client** | required; an active or an archived client, never the worked example (§317) |
| **name** | one line — *Strategy build 2025*, *Reporting cycle 2026* — Islam's word for each, typed by whoever opens it |
| **start / end** | a month each; end is empty while the work is running |
| **writer** | the consultant asked to write it up (decision 7) |
| **due** | when the write-up is wanted, which is what orders it on My work |
| **state** | *open* (work running) · *to write* (assigned) · *done* (decision 10) |

**The case study itself is eight sections** (decision 8), in this order, which
is the order a new joiner reads it:

1. **In short** — one paragraph to read first.
2. **The client and why they came to us.**
3. **What we were asked to do.**
4. **The team** — ours, picked from the Consultants list; theirs, typed, one
   line each with their role. Their people are typed because a past client has
   no people register, and an archived client's register is behind a closed
   door.
5. **Timeline** — the start and end above, and the main stages as short lines.
6. **Deliverables** — one line each: name · what it was · when it was handed
   over · the file (decision 6). A line may be saved before its file is found;
   it then says *No file attached* rather than nothing (§35).
7. **What came of it.**
8. **Who to ask** — one line per person on our team: what they know about.

**Prose, except where a reader scans.** The team, the timeline's two dates and
the deliverables are lines because a new joiner counts and opens them; the rest
is prose, for 045's reason — twenty fields is what nobody fills in.

**The industry and size are the client card's** and are drawn, never stored
(§53.5). Re-classify a client and every one of its case studies follows.

## 5 · The memory goes inside it (decision 9)

**A lesson belongs to a piece of work.** Each memory entry gains one optional
link: which piece of work it came from. Inside a case study its lessons are
listed under it, kind and title and author, each opening as it does today.

**So the piece of work is opened when the work starts, not when it ends.** That
is what lets a lesson written on the day it happens land in the right place
months before anybody writes the case study. Opening one is a name and a start
month on the client's card, by an admin; asking for the write-up at the end is
a second step on the same record (decision 1, decision 11).

**A lesson with no piece of work is still saved** — 045's one rule that keeps
the memory alive, *a title and a client are enough*, is not taken back. It sits
under its client marked *Not yet in a case study*, and its author or an admin
attaches it later. **Every entry written before today is one of these**, and
nothing attaches them by guessing from dates: an entry's period is free text,
and a guess that put a lesson in the wrong story is worse than one waiting to
be placed.

**What a reader opens first is the case study.** The section lists pieces of
work; a case study opens with its eight sections and its lessons after them.
**Searching and filtering by kind still reach every lesson across all case
studies** — *"show me the hiccups on distribution clients"* is 045's own
question and stays answerable. What the tab is called, now that it holds both,
is settled on the mockup (§10).

## 6 · The way in — the interview, for new work and for the backlog

**The same mechanism as the memory's period debrief**, which is decision 3's
*"something like a memory, a prompt for them to check and reply to"*:

1. The writer opens the case study from My work.
2. **Copy the prompt.** It carries the client, its industry, the piece of
   work's name and months, and the team already named. They take it to
   whatever assistant they use and talk it through.
3. The assistant **interviews rather than collects** — one question at a time,
   pushing back on a vague answer — and writes the eight sections back in one
   fixed shape, each under its own heading, deliverables as one line each.
4. **They paste the whole answer back in one box.** The platform splits it into
   the eight sections as a **draft the writer reads**, every section editable.
5. They attach each deliverable's file — an assistant cannot hand over a file —
   and mark it done.

**The prompt forbids invention and the draft exists because that cannot be
enforced** (045's reasoning, unchanged): *use my words; if I only half answered
something, leave it out.* A section the paste did not fill stays empty and
says so. **A paste the splitter cannot read lands whole in the first section,
never dropped** — losing what somebody dictated is the one unacceptable
outcome (§184).

**The interview ends on the lessons**, in the memory's existing block shape, so
one conversation fills both and the existing splitter reads them. That matters
most for the backlog: past work had no memory running beside it, so the end of
the interview is the only time those lessons are ever written. **Cost:** a
longer conversation; a writer may stop before that part, and the case study
saves without it.

A writer may also type every section in by hand. The prompt is the usual way
in, not the only one.

## 7 · Past clients — the backlog (decisions 3 and 5)

**Add a past client** on the Clients page: name, industry, size, and nothing
else. It is created **already archived** — no modules, no people, no plan, its
door closed — because it exists to be written about, not worked in. It appears
in the Archived band with every other set-aside client.

**Then an admin opens its piece of work and asks for the write-up in one go**,
since the work is already over: name, months, writer, due date.

**Two things that today skip an archived client must not skip it here:**
My work reads only active clients' Trackers, and the memory's client picker
offers only the cards' clients, which leave archived ones out. Both are
widened for this and only for this (§8).

**Deleting a client that holds a case study is refused by name**, the memory's
own rule (`ON DELETE RESTRICT`, 045): the story outlives the engagement.

## 8 · Where it lives, and what it touches

**Forefront's side only**, beside the memory, for 045's whole reason: a case
study names a client's numbers and is read across clients. **The platform
schema, never a tenant table. A client's own platform draws nothing of it**,
and the tenant role is refused its tables. Every office account reads a
*done* one (decision 2); a draft is read by its writer and the admins
(decision 10).

**My work gains a second source.** Today every row is read from a client's
Tracker. A case study to write is not a Tracker row and cannot be one: the
backlog is on archived clients, whose Trackers are behind a closed door and
which My work does not read. So it is drawn in My work's own row — the same
look, the same *Late / This week / Next week and later* by its due date — and
pressing it opens the case study on Forefront's side. **Cost:** My work stops
being *only the Tracker*, which §400 said it was. **The row draws no tick and
no status** (§61: a control is drawn only where it would be accepted) — a case
study is done when it is marked done on the case study, never from a list.

**Files** are kept in the existing store and reached only through our own
route, minted per read, as Resources' files are (`library-file.ts`'s reason: a
store address is permanent and forwardable). Only an office account is served
one.

**Who may do what, on the server** (§42 — the rule, not the screen):

| | |
| --- | --- |
| open a piece of work, ask for a write-up, add a past client | an admin |
| write and mark done | the writer named on it, or an admin |
| correct a done one | its writer or an admin (045's rule for an entry) |
| read a done one | every office account (decision 2, "for now") |
| read a draft | its writer and the admins |
| attach a lesson | the lesson's author or an admin |

---

## 9 · The way out

**The memory's assistant reads case studies too**, under 045's two rules: every
answer names its sources — which case study, which client, who wrote it — and
it answers only from what the asker may read. A draft is not in its corpus.
It is built last, for 045's reason: an assistant over an empty store answers
confidently from nothing.

## 10 · Put to Islam on the mockup — answered 2026-10-23

Islam: *"1 yes 2 keep insight 3 yes"*. So all three below are **decisions**:
a piece of work is opened when the work starts; the small entries stay

### 10.1 · The first drawing was rejected, and why the second is the Tracker's

*"very poor design. check internal tracker and show me what we can adjust."*
The first drawing borrowed the memory's own screens — a card for the reading
view, boxed monospace labels on every row, forms for every act — and that is
a list of furniture, not a page. **The Internal Tracker is the console's
signed-off way of laying out a list of work** (§497's ledger, and §583 already
carried it onto My work), so the second drawing is built from it and nothing
else: the navy band with what the page is, four counts and the views as tabs;
the gold write line; numbered sections under a heavy rule; one-line rows of
name · client · when · person · status word · the way in; a row that opens in
place for its detail. **What that changes beyond the look, and is asked rather
than assumed:** an insight opens under its own row instead of on a page of its
own; one gold line asks the case studies on the list, writes an insight under
Insights and inside a case study, and — for an admin — opens a piece of work
with its client, months and writer arriving beside the name, as the Tracker's
topic, week and owner do. The list groups by state (*To write · Running ·
Done*), and *Group by* offers client and industry.

### 10.2 · Not the Tracker either: a library

Islam, of the second drawing: *"I think that view is suitable for the tracker
only. the view of the cases should be different change your perspective and
think how would a library look like?"* The Tracker's ledger is for work that
moves every week; a case study is finished work that people come to **read
and browse**. So the third drawing is a library, and says where it is new:

- **The front of the library**: one search across every case study, with
  *Ask a question instead* beside it (the assistant); the industries as the
  row under it — the rule spec 053 already set for the client's Insights
  library, *a library's second row is its categories*; then **covers on
  shelves**, a *Newest* shelf and one shelf per industry.
- **A cover**: the client's mark, the industry, the name of the work and its
  months on Forefront's navy; the client and the counts under it. *New* for a
  case study finished in the last 30 days.
- **The desk**: running and being-written work stays **off the shelves** until
  it is done, so a shelf only ever holds finished case studies.
- **A case study reads as a report**: the cover becomes the header, *In short*
  is the opening paragraph in large type, the story runs down the left, and
  *At a glance*, the contents and the buttons stay on the right. The timeline
  is a strip of months with each stage as a bar; deliverables are file tiles;
  the team and who to ask are faces.
- **Insights are index cards**: kind, title, and *what the next person should
  know* as the line most worth reading; the card names its case study or says
  it has none yet.
- **The draft is the report with every part open to change**: dashed edges
  are editable, a missing file is a gold tile to drop it on.

**Costs, stated on the drawing:** a look no other console page has; a past
client added in three fields has no mark, so its cover shows initials until a
logo is added; covers take more room than rows.

**And the way a past case study gets written**, drawn the same day on Islam's
*"yes draw them"* (drawings 6–9): **adding a past client** on Clients — three
fields, created archived, its card carrying *Case studies · 1 being written*;
**opening a piece of work** — the form beside a live preview of *the cover it
makes*, which says where it will stand (the desk, then its industry's shelf);
**the writer's My work** — the console's row with a book mark where the tick
is; **the interview** — the cover as header, *1 The interview · 2 The draft ·
3 Marked done, on the shelf*, and the two steps side by side, the prompt to
copy on the left and the paste on the right. The words on the desk say *Being
written* where §4 says *to write*; the stored state is unchanged, the label
is the drawing's.

## 11 · Build order

**Drawn first, then three slices**, each of which works without the next:

1. **The record and the reading page** — pieces of work, the eight sections,
   lessons inside, *Not yet in a case study*, past clients added archived,
   written by hand.
2. **The way in** — the interview prompt, the paste split into a draft, the
   files, the request from an admin and the row on My work.
3. **The assistant** over case studies and lessons together.

---

## 12 · Recorded, not done

- **No link to a Portfolio project.** Portfolio (spec 060) is the delivery plan
  for a piece of work, read by the client too, and a running engagement may
  have one. Pulling its name, months and charter deliverables in would save
  typing them twice; it is a second decision, and past work has no Portfolio
  project at all.
- **No sign-off before a case study is read** (decision 10). If new joiners
  start learning from one that was wrong, that is the decision to revisit.
- **Old decks are not imported.** The backlog is written by consultants through
  the interview (decision 3); reading old files in automatically is a decision
  about effort, not code.
- **Nothing records who read a case study**, for 045 decision 4's reason.
- **Clients never see their own case study.** If one is ever to be shared with
  the client as a reference, that is a different document with a different
  reader, and a second decision.

## 13 · Verification, when it is built

- **The boundary, both ends** (045's `memory-boundary` shape): a client's own
  shell draws no case study anywhere, the tenant role is refused the tables —
  and a done case study IS read by an office account in the same run, or a
  build that dropped the feature would pass the absence half.
- **A draft is invisible** to every office account but its writer and the
  admins, asserted against the server, not the screen.
- **Only an admin** opens a piece of work, asks for a write-up or adds a past
  client; refused by name for anybody else. Falsified both ways.
- **The split** — eight headed sections read into eight; a section missing
  stays empty and says so; a paste with no headings lands whole in the first
  section and is never dropped; nothing is saved until the draft is saved.
- **My work** shows a case study to write on an **archived** client, under the
  right heading by its due date, and opening it lands on the case study.
- **A lesson** saves with no piece of work, attaches later, and then appears
  inside its case study; one attached to nothing is listed under its client.
- **A deliverable's file** is served to an office account through our route
  and refused to a client account.
- **Deleting a client** that holds a case study is refused by name.
